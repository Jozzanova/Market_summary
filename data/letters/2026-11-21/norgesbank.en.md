# Norges Bank, weekly report week 47 (13–20 November 2026)

**The Norwegian week brought a record trade surplus, labour cost growth of 4.7 per cent and an Expectations Survey with broadly stable long-term inflation expectations among economists. No economist changed view on the 17 December decision: those quoted expect the policy rate to be held at 4.5 per cent. The krone weakened 1.4 per cent against the euro as oil prices and global equities fell, while the rate differential changed little.**

## New data

- **External trade.** The trade surplus rose to 121.4 billion kroner in October, the highest since 2022, driven by gas exports ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/utenriksokonomi/utenrikshandel/statistikk/utenrikshandel-med-varer/artikler/hoyeste-handelsoverskudd-siden-2022)).
- **Labour costs.** Labour costs in manufacturing rose 4.7 per cent from a year earlier in the third quarter ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/lonn-og-arbeidskraftkostnader/statistikk/arbeidskraftkostnadsindeks)).
- **Expectations Survey.** Economists expect inflation of 2.4 per cent five years ahead, unchanged, while households' five-year expectation rose 0.3 percentage point to 5.1 per cent; the share of business leaders expecting weaker profitability rose 7.6 percentage points to 37.3 per cent ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/publications/expectations-survey/2026/q4-2026/)).

*Reading.* Stable expectations among economists and the social partners suggest that the anchor holds among those closest to wage formation, while weaker expected profitability points to softer activity. On balance the week's data do not strengthen the case for a further hike.

## What others say

- **On the survey.** Kjetil Olsen, chief economist at Nordea, said that by keeping the rate high Norges Bank helps to dampen activity in the Norwegian economy; Nejra Macic, chief economist at NHO, pointed to the third of businesses expecting weaker profitability ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/rogaland/norges-bank-med-ny-forventningsundersokelse_-stangeland-maskin-frykter-inflasjonen-ikke-vil-bremse-1.18084262)). NHO also said on Monday that the wage settlements need not stand in the way of Norges Bank ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/yzvere/trenger-ikke-aa-spenne-bein-paa-norges-bank)).
- **On the rate path.** No economist published a new view on the December decision during the week. Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects a hold, and Harald Magnus Andreassen of SpareBank 1 Markets sees inflation on the track Norges Bank assumed ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)); Frank Jullum of Danske Bank believes the peak has been reached, while Allan Monks of JP Morgan expects a further hike in the first quarter ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/eG3QBa/tror-rentetoppen-er-naadd-balanserer-paa-en-knivsegg)). None of the economists quoted expects a hike in December.
- **Market pricing.** No new figure was found; the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** Over the week EUR/NOK rose 1.40 per cent to 10.716, USD/NOK 2.16 per cent to 9.770 and I-44 1.28 per cent to 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone).

**Why.** Brent fell 3.4 per cent, which weakens the terms of trade, and global equities fell markedly; the krone, a small and less liquid currency, tends to weaken when investors reduce risk. The differentials explain little: the 12-month differential in government rates against the euro area was 1.78 percentage points on Thursday, about 4 basis points narrower than a week earlier, and against the United States it widened about 3 basis points to 0.16 percentage point. The policy rate differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 8 basis points to 4.28 per cent, against 4 basis points for the German 10-year.
