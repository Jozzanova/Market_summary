# Key points, weekly report week 47 (13–20 November 2026)

**The week was shaped by a broad fall in equities that began in AI and semiconductor shares and spread to most sectors and regions; Friday's report of a regulatory probe into Nvidia's accounting deepened it. In contrast to the week before, when higher Fed expectations drove yields up, government bonds now served as a haven and US yields fell markedly, while a Fed hike in December is still priced. The krone weakened as oil prices and risk appetite fell.**

**Equities.** The S&P 500 fell 8.61 per cent over the week, the Nasdaq 10.02 per cent and the Dow 8.02 per cent; the S&P 500 and the Dow are in correction territory, and the Nasdaq had its worst week since March 2020 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/sp-500-correction-market-sell-off.html)). SOX fell 17.13 per cent and Nvidia about 23 per cent, 14 per cent of it on Friday. STOXX Europe 600 fell 5.79 per cent, its largest weekly fall since March 2025, the Nikkei 7.70 per cent and KOSPI 7.95 per cent ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/20/global-stock-markets-slump-anthropic-ipo-ai-sell-off)). Oslo Børs fell 3.51 per cent, less than abroad given its small technology weight ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/ez8RGy/nedgang-paa-oslo-boers)). The VIX rose 16.6 points to 35.9.

**Government bonds.** The US 5-year yield fell 28 basis points over the week to 4.70 per cent and the 10-year 24 basis points to 4.91 per cent, about two thirds of it on Friday; the 10-year breakeven fell 9 basis points. Fed funds futures price a Fed hike of 22 basis points on 9 December and 16 more by January, against 26 and 20 a week ago. The German 10-year fell 4 basis points to 3.39 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year (live) 8 basis points to 4.28 per cent.

**Currencies.** The dollar index rose 0.39 per cent to 103.81, EUR/USD fell to 1.097 and USD/JPY was little changed at 159.7. EUR/NOK rose 1.40 per cent to 10.716, USD/NOK 2.16 per cent to 9.770 and I-44 1.28 per cent to 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone): lower oil prices and lower risk appetite, which tends to weaken a small currency, appear to explain the move, as the rate differential changed little.

**Commodities.** Brent fell 3.41 per cent to 92.57 dollars as expectations of a supply surplus outweighed attacks in the Gulf. Gold fell 9.54 per cent to 3,515 dollars, its worst week since March, as Fed expectations and a firmer dollar weighed and investors with losses elsewhere sold gold to raise cash; silver fell 16.3 per cent and copper 9.9 per cent. TTF gas fell 2.75 per cent to 88.45 euros.

**Data and central banks.** The Fed's minutes showed almost all participants expecting further increases ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm)), Governor Waller raised the possibility of a 50 basis point hike ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/federal-reserve-governor-christopher-waller.html)) and Governor Cook on Friday doubted that one hike is enough ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20261120a.htm)). US retail sales rose 0.2 per cent in October, while the Philadelphia Fed index fell to 6.3 and households' one-year inflation expectations to 3.7 per cent ([University of Michigan](https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/)). UK inflation of 3.6 per cent was below the Bank of England's forecast ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/october2026)); Japanese core inflation rose to 2.2 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/japan-inflation-cpi-october.html)). No G10 central bank changed rates. On Friday President Trump threatened a "tremendous tax" on companies building data centres abroad ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/trump-data-centers-ai-companies-tax.html)).

**Norway.** The trade surplus rose to 121.4 billion kroner in October ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/utenriksokonomi/utenrikshandel/statistikk/utenrikshandel-med-varer/artikler/hoyeste-handelsoverskudd-siden-2022)), and economists' five-year inflation expectation in the Expectations Survey was unchanged at 2.4 per cent. The differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, 1.78 percentage points at twelve months in government rates, about 4 basis points narrower over the week, and 0.88 percentage point at ten years. No economist changed view: those quoted expect a hold on 17 December, and the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike. The red-green parties missed Friday's budget deadline and continue over the weekend ([NTB](https://www.tv2.no/nyheter/forhandlinger-om-statsbudsjettet-fortsetter-pa-overtid/19454639/)).

**The week ahead.** Flash PMIs on Monday; on Wednesday US GDP for the third quarter and PCE prices for October, where Governor Cook cited estimates of 3.8 per cent headline and 3.2 per cent core, and the Norwegian labour force survey; Thursday brings Norway's third-quarter national accounts and the Bank of England's Financial Stability Report, with US markets closed for Thanksgiving; on Friday Norwegian retail trade and registered unemployment. OPEC+ and the Spanish election follow on 29 November. Markets enter the week with a Fed hike in December about 89 per cent priced.
