# Markets, weekly report week 47 (13–20 November 2026)

**The fall in AI and semiconductor shares, which began after the change of chief executive at Nvidia, turned into a broad decline in global equities this week. It intensified on Friday after a report that the US securities regulator is examining Nvidia's accounting. Unlike the week before, government bonds acted as a haven: US yields fell markedly even though the Federal Reserve is still expected to raise its policy rate in December. Commodities fell across the board, and the krone weakened as risk appetite declined.**

## Equities

- **United States: the worst week since spring 2025.** The S&P 500 fell 8.61 per cent over the week, the Nasdaq Composite 10.02 per cent and the Dow 8.02 per cent; the S&P 500 and the Dow closed in correction territory, more than 10 per cent below their records, and the Nasdaq had its worst week since March 2020 and is now down for the year ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/sp-500-correction-market-sell-off.html)). On Friday alone the S&P 500 fell 3.70 per cent and the Nasdaq 5.28 per cent. The VIX rose 16.6 points over the week to 35.9.
- **AI and semiconductors set the tone.** The semiconductor index SOX fell 17.13 per cent, 10.73 per cent of it on Friday; NYSE FANG+ fell 11.87 per cent, the Magnificent Seven ETF 10.20 per cent and the AI ETF AIQ 13.11 per cent. Nvidia fell 14 per cent on Friday after Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining possible accounting issues, about 23 per cent over the week ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/sp-500-correction-market-sell-off.html)). Memory makers SanDisk and Micron fell 14 and 12 per cent on Friday ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/20/global-stock-markets-slump-anthropic-ipo-ai-sell-off)). Anthropic, priced at 83 dollars, closed 17 per cent higher on its first day but fell back to 88.91 dollars on Friday.
- **The decline broadened.** Technology fell 12.49 per cent over the week and communication services 7.97 per cent, but financials (−7.00 per cent), industrials (−7.35 per cent) and energy (−6.85 per cent) also fell markedly; consumer staples (−2.05 per cent) and utilities (−3.06 per cent) held up best. CNBC points to a technical amplifier: once the S&P 500 broke below its 200-day moving average, trend-following funds, which buy rising and sell falling markets according to fixed rules, sold automatically and added to the decline. IG's Chris Beauchamp suggested the record Anthropic listing may have drained liquidity, as investors sold other technology shares to pay for their allotments ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/20/global-stock-markets-slump-anthropic-ipo-ai-sell-off)).
- **Europe and Asia.** STOXX Europe 600 fell 5.79 per cent, its largest weekly fall since March 2025, the DAX 7.28 per cent and the FTSE 100 5.10 per cent. The Nikkei fell 7.70 per cent and KOSPI 7.95 per cent, with falls of about 5 per cent on Friday; SoftBank fell 14.5 per cent and Taiwan's market 6.7 per cent on Friday ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/20/global-stock-markets-slump-anthropic-ipo-ai-sell-off)).
- **Oslo Børs fell least.** The benchmark index fell 3.51 per cent over the week and 1.5 per cent on Friday, its fifth daily decline; Nordic Semiconductor fell 6.7 per cent and Aker BP and Kongsberg Gruppen 3.3 per cent on Friday, while Frontline rose 2.1 per cent after its results ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/ez8RGy/nedgang-paa-oslo-boers)). The small weight of technology in the index limited the fall.

## Fixed income and credit

- **Treasuries as a haven.** The US 5-year yield fell 28 basis points over the week to 4.70 per cent and the 10-year 24 basis points to 4.91 per cent, with falls of 17 and 14 basis points on Friday; the 10-year thus fell back below 5 per cent. The 3-month bill was unchanged, so the decline reflects less expected tightening beyond December and demand for safe assets rather than a change in the December outlook. The 10-year breakeven inflation rate fell 9 basis points, in line with lower oil prices.
- **Europe and Norway.** The German 10-year yield fell 4 basis points over the week to 3.39 per cent, 6 basis points of it on Friday. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 8 basis points to 4.28 per cent.
- **Credit.** The US high-yield spread widened 32 basis points in the week to Thursday, to 3.52 percentage points; investment grade widened 5 basis points.

## Commodities and currencies

- **Commodities.** Brent fell 3.41 per cent to 92.57 dollars as expectations of a supply surplus outweighed attacks in the Gulf. Gold fell 9.54 per cent to 3,515 dollars and silver 16.31 per cent, as expectations of Fed tightening and selling to raise cash weighed; copper fell 9.91 per cent. TTF gas fell 2.75 per cent to 88.45 euros.
- **Currencies.** The dollar index rose 0.39 per cent to 103.81 and EUR/USD fell 0.72 per cent to 1.097; USD/JPY was little changed at 159.7. The krone weakened: EUR/NOK rose 1.40 per cent to 10.716, USD/NOK 2.16 per cent to 9.770, and I-44 1.28 per cent to 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone). Lower oil prices and lower risk appetite are the likely causes; the rate differential changed little. Bitcoin fell 12.2 per cent and dropped below 60,000 dollars for the first time since July.

## Turning points of the week

- **Monday 16:** new US licence requirements for chip exports; SOX fell 5.28 per cent, Brent 3.6 per cent.
- **Tuesday 17:** STOXX Europe 600 had its largest daily fall since March ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/european-stocks-mark-worst-day-in-eight-months-on-ai-jitters-deutsche-bank-slides-ce785edcdd8df723)); Nvidia's results beat expectations after the close.
- **Wednesday 18:** the Fed minutes and Governor Waller's call for a possible 50 basis point hike.
- **Thursday 19:** Anthropic's debut; the S&P 500 fell 2.02 per cent despite early gains.
- **Friday 20:** the report of an SEC probe into Nvidia; the Nasdaq fell 5.28 per cent and the US 5-year yield 17 basis points.
