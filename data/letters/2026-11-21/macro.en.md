# Macro, weekly report week 47 (13–20 November 2026)

**Central bank communication pointed to further tightening throughout the week: the minutes of the Fed's October meeting showed almost all participants expecting further increases, and several officials argued for continued hikes. Market pricing nonetheless eased somewhat towards the end of the week as equities fell, mostly for the meetings after December. The data were mixed: US consumption held up while manufacturing surveys weakened, UK inflation came in below the Bank of England's forecast, and Japanese core inflation rose. No G10 central bank changed its policy rate.**

**United States.** Consumption held up in October, while the regional manufacturing surveys weakened and prices paid rose: weaker activity combined with higher input costs is the combination the Fed finds hardest to respond to. The fall in households' one-year inflation expectations from 4.7 to 3.7 per cent is the clearest sign of relief in the week's data and likely reflects lower petrol prices. Governor Lisa Cook cited PCE inflation of 3.8 per cent over the twelve months to October and core inflation of 3.2 per cent ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20261120a.htm)); the official figures follow on 25 November.

**Elsewhere.** UK inflation below the Bank of England's forecast reduced the probability of a December hike there, while higher unemployment in Australia led markets to price out further RBA hikes. In Japan, core inflation of 2.2 per cent supports a Bank of Japan hike on 18 December, which LSEG data price at 72 per cent. Chinese activity data for October were weaker than expected ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/chinas-factory-output-retail-sales-020559490.html)).

## Central banks

- **Federal Reserve.** The minutes of the 27–28 October meeting show that almost all participants judged further increases likely to be appropriate ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm)). Governor Christopher Waller said a 50 basis point hike is "probably necessary" in December unless the Fed signals a further move in January ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/federal-reserve-governor-christopher-waller.html)); St. Louis Fed president Alberto Musalem expects several hikes before spring 2027 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/alberto-musalem-fed-interest-rates.html)). On Friday Governor Cook, who dissented in October in favour of a hike, said she is "skeptical that September's single 1/4 point rate increase will be enough to change the path of inflation" ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20261120a.htm)).
- **ECB.** Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel argued on Friday for a December hike that takes policy into restrictive territory, according to Bloomberg's headline ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/ecb-needs-december-rate-hike-into-restrictive-zone-nagel-says)); President Lagarde's speech in Frankfurt on Friday did not address the rate outlook ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/key/date/2026/html/ecb.sp261120~e52bcc4db2.en.html)). Policymakers will discuss raising the minimum reserve requirement on 25 November ([Reuters](https://www.1049thewolf.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-ecb-to-discuss-raising-reserve-requirement-next-week-sources-say/)).
- **Bank of Canada.** The summary of deliberations shows that the October hike to 2.5 per cent reflected energy costs passing through to businesses' prices, and that further increases "could" be needed ([Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/11/summary-of-governing-council-deliberations-fixed-announcement-date-of-october-28-2026/)).

## What pricing did over the week

Fed funds futures price a Fed hike of 22 basis points on 9 December, a probability of about 89 per cent, and a further 16 basis points by 27 January; a week earlier the figures were about 26 and 20 basis points. The easing came mainly on Friday and was largest for January, consistent with the fall in the 5-year yield. No new report on the pricing of the ECB meeting on 17 December was found; all 71 economists in last week's Reuters poll expect a hike to 2.75 per cent. For Norges Bank the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike on 17 December.

## Assessment

The week shifted the balance of risks. Inflation remains well above target in the United States, the euro area and the United Kingdom, and central bank communication has not changed. But a 9 per cent fall in US equities, wider credit spreads and lower oil prices tighten financial conditions and reduce inflation pressures, which is the mechanism through which policy works. If the fall in equities persists, the case for a 50 basis point Fed hike weakens, while a 25 basis point hike in December still looks likely. The PCE figures on 25 November and the November employment report on 4 December are the next tests.
