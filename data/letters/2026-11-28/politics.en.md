# Politics, weekly report week 48 (20–27 November 2026)

**Washington's involvement in the technology sector was the political theme of the week: the administration backed Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia, which set off Monday's recovery in equities, and proposed new tariffs that it then capped at the rates in existing trade deals. In Europe, leaders agreed on the next ECB President but not on the long-term budget, and S&P put France on a negative outlook. In the Gulf, attacks on tankers and refineries kept a risk premium in oil, and in Norway the 2027 budget still lacks a majority.**

## United States

- **Nvidia and the administration.** Nvidia's board said on Monday, citing input from the US government, that Huang's return "poses no national security risk" ([Nvidia](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm)), after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had said on Sunday that the administration still needed answers on export compliance ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/bessent-nvidia-china-ai-chips-alice-huang-canard/)). Bloomberg describes the week's message from Washington as that America must do whatever it takes to win the AI race with China ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/trump-s-ai-gambit-fuels-wild-thanksgiving-week-on-wall-street)). Market channel: government support lowers the risk of an abrupt break in Nvidia's management, but the FT argues that political involvement in single companies is itself a cost for US equities, as it has been for the dollar and Treasuries ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/3318a827-bf20-4826-947d-9327381c3c10?syn-25a6b1a6=1)). Huang appears before the House Foreign Affairs Committee on 1 December and the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December.
- **Tariffs proposed, then capped.** The US Trade Representative proposed Section 301 tariffs of 15 to 35 per cent on fourteen economies, none before a public hearing on 25 January ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural)), and then said it would cap them at the rates in existing deals, 15 per cent for the EU ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/european-stocks-rally-tech-banks-083233000.html)). Market channel: the cap removed the worst outcome for European exporters and was one reason for Thursday's rise in European equities. A US jury also convicted Huawei on ten counts ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/11/24/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-is-guilty-of-conspiracy-to-steal-trade-secrets-us-jury-finds/)).

## Europe

- **ECB President agreed, budget not.** Euro-area leaders recommended Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde ([Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/26/european-council-puts-forward-pablo-hernandez-de-cos-as-next-president-of-the-european-central-bank/)), while the summit ended without agreement on the 2028–2034 budget and the proposed loan to Ukraine was postponed to December ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/26/eu-summit-leaders-ready-to-spar-over-new-budget-and-ukraine-aid)).
- **France.** On Friday S&P revised the outlook on France's rating to negative from stable, saying political instability will make it harder to reduce the deficit ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/4e16110a-9604-4455-8557-4edece5df01a?syn-25a6b1a6=1)). Market channel: a negative outlook signals a possible downgrade, which would raise the cost of French borrowing relative to Germany; the reaction comes on Monday.
- **Export controls.** France said it backs the Netherlands in opposing a proposed US law that would require European companies such as ASML to apply US restrictions ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/france-joins-the-dutch-in-opposing-proposed-us-export-curbs)).
- **Spain votes on Sunday.** Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez made a final appeal for turnout in a snap election in which he is the underdog ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/sanchez-seeks-unlikely-win-with-plea-for-spaniards-to-go-vote)).

## Middle East and energy

- **Attacks in the Gulf.** The Houthis claimed attacks on the Ras Tanura refinery on Wednesday ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/oil-wti-brent-hormuz-middle-east.html)), a drone attack in Kuwait killed two Italian technicians and Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed mine strikes on tankers near Hormuz, which could not be independently confirmed ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-military-ai-weapons-oil/)). On Friday the US struck a vessel attempting to run the blockade near Iran as diplomatic talks stalled ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/u-s-strikes-vessel-attempting-blockade-run-near-iran-338439e4?mod=rss_worldnews)). Market channel: fewer transits through the strait raise the risk premium in oil and shipping, which supported Brent midweek.

## Norway

- **Budget without a majority.** Labour, the Centre Party, the Socialist Left and Red agreed on the 2027 budget on Wednesday, raising the use of oil revenue by 7.4 billion kroner, but the Greens stayed out ([Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/politikk/i/0vn14B/fire-partier-enige-om-statsbudsjett-kutter-i-drivstoffavgift)). The fall in the oil fund had lowered the fiscal room ([NRK](https://nrk.no/artikkel/oljefondet-har-krympet-over-1000-milliarder-blast-bort-1533745)). Market channel: limited, since the change in the oil revenue spending is small, but a failure to find a majority would add uncertainty to fiscal policy in 2027.
