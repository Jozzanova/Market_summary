# Key points, weekly report week 48 (20–27 November 2026)

**The week reversed much of the previous week's fall in equities: Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia with the US administration's support set off a broad recovery that faltered midweek and widened again on Friday. In contrast to the week before, government bonds lost their haven role, US yields ended slightly higher and a Fed hike on 9 December remains almost fully priced after firm core inflation. The krone weakened in the first half of the week and recovered in the second.**

**Equities.** The S&P 500 rose 5.60 per cent over the week, its best week since April 2025, the Nasdaq 6.68 per cent and the Dow 5.46 per cent ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/2026/11/27/stocks-jump-to-close-out-an-erratic-week-that-was-also-wall-streets-best-in-more-than-a-year/)). The SOX rose 9.25 per cent and the Magnificent Seven ETF 7.90 per cent, with Nvidia up 31.5 per cent on Monday alone; energy, utilities and staples lagged. STOXX Europe 600 rose 2.91 per cent, helped on Thursday by the US cap on new tariffs, and Oslo Børs 1.74 per cent, where Norse Atlantic was declared bankrupt ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/wdPMQ1/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)). The Nikkei rose 4.03 per cent and KOSPI 0.94 per cent after large swings. The VIX fell 12.1 points to 23.8.

**Government bonds.** The US 5-year yield rose 4.5 basis points over the week to 4.75 per cent and the 10-year 2.4 basis points to 4.94 per cent; yields fell until Wednesday and rose about 9 basis points on Friday as demand for havens eased. Fed funds futures imply a Fed hike of 23.6 basis points on 9 December and 17 more by January, against 22 and 16 a week ago. The German 10-year fell 3 basis points to 3.36 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year (live) 14.5 basis points to 4.14 per cent. The last reported pricing is an 87 per cent probability of an ECB hike on 17 December.

**Currencies.** The dollar index rose 0.19 per cent to 104.01, EUR/USD was unchanged at 1.097 and USD/JPY eased to 159.3. EUR/NOK fell 0.13 per cent to 10.702, USD/NOK rose 0.40 per cent to 9.809 and I-44 was unchanged at 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone): lower oil prices and the sell-off in technology shares weakened the krone until Wednesday, and the return of risk appetite reversed the move.

**Commodities.** Brent's January contract rose 3.10 per cent to 95.44 dollars as attacks on tankers and Gulf refineries outweighed expectations of a supply surplus; the February contract ended at 90.84 dollars ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting. TTF gas fell 5.94 per cent to 83.20 euros on milder forecasts. Gold rose 3.53 per cent to 3,640 dollars and silver 5.20 per cent as risk appetite returned and investors no longer needed to sell metals to cover losses elsewhere.

**Data and central banks.** US core PCE rose 3.3 per cent over twelve months, above the 3.2 per cent expected, and third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/pce-inflation-report-october-2026.html)). New York Fed President John Williams would not exclude hikes at consecutive meetings ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/fed-lower-inflation-williams-says-210546185.html)). The ECB account showed broad support for a hike already in October ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/accounts/2026/html/ecb.mg261126~ccf28c8071.en.html)), and Vice President Vujčić and Martin Kocher pointed to further tightening ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/vujcic-and-kocher-hint-at-more-ecb-rate-hiking-kurier-reports)). The Bank of Korea raised its policy rate to 3.25 per cent, and Tokyo core inflation reached a sixteen-month high. The US capped its proposed tariffs at deal rates, and S&P put France on a negative outlook ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/4e16110a-9604-4455-8557-4edece5df01a?syn-25a6b1a6=1)).

**Norway.** Mainland GDP grew 0.1 per cent in the third quarter against Norges Bank's 0.3 per cent ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/nasjonalregnskap-og-konjunkturer/nasjonalregnskap/statistikk/nasjonalregnskap/artikler/svak-vekst-i-fastlandsokonomien)), LFS unemployment rose to 4.7 per cent, registered unemployment held at 2.0 per cent ([NAV](https://www.nav.no/no/nav-og-samfunn/statistikk/arbeidssokere-og-stillinger-statistikk/nyheter/litt-faerre-arbeidsledige-i-november)) and retail volume rose 0.5 per cent in October. The differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, 1.80 percentage points at twelve months in government rates, about 3 basis points wider over the week, and 0.78 percentage point at ten years. None of the economists quoted expects a hike on 17 December; the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent. The budget agreement still lacks a majority.

**The week ahead.** Sunday brings the OPEC+ meeting on the 2027 production framework and the Spanish general election; on Monday the Storting finance committee's budget recommendation and Norges Bank's foreign exchange purchases for December. The euro-area flash HICP follows on Tuesday, where October was 4.0 per cent, Eiendom Norge's house prices on Thursday and the US employment report on Friday, the last major release before the Fed decision on 9 December, which the market prices as a hike with a probability of about 94 per cent.
