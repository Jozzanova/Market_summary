# Norges Bank, weekly report week 48 (20–27 November 2026)

**The Norwegian week brought mainland growth below Norges Bank's projection, a slight rise in unemployment in the labour force survey, stable registered unemployment and a modest rise in retail trade. Taken together the figures point to somewhat less pressure on capacity than the bank assumed in September, while household demand is holding up. None of the economists quoted expects a hike on 17 December. The krone weakened in the first half of the week and recovered in the second, ending little changed.**

## New data

{bars: Norway, week 48 releases, per cent from previous period | Mainland GDP Q3 0.1; Household consumption Q3 1.6; Public consumption Q3 0.3; Mainland investment Q3 −1.8; Retail volume October 0.5 | | SSB}

- **Growth below the bank's projection.** Mainland GDP rose 0.1 per cent in the third quarter, against 0.3 per cent in Monetary Policy Report 3/26; household consumption rose 1.6 per cent while mainland investment fell 1.8 per cent ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/nasjonalregnskap-og-konjunkturer/nasjonalregnskap/statistikk/nasjonalregnskap/artikler/svak-vekst-i-fastlandsokonomien)). Lower power production and fishing explain part of the shortfall.
- **Labour market.** Unemployment in the labour force survey rose 0.1 percentage point to 4.7 per cent in October ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/sysselsetting/statistikk/arbeidskraftundersokelsen/artikler/arbeidsledigheten-pa-4-7-prosent)). Registered unemployment was unchanged at 2.0 per cent in November, seasonally adjusted, with 200 fewer fully unemployed; NAV director Hege Nilssen said the labour market "har holdt seg godt" despite higher rates ([NAV](https://www.nav.no/no/nav-og-samfunn/statistikk/arbeidssokere-og-stillinger-statistikk/nyheter/litt-faerre-arbeidsledige-i-november)).
- **Retail trade.** Retail volume rose 0.5 per cent in October after a fall in September, driven by groceries ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/varehandel-og-tjenesteyting/varehandel/statistikk/varehandelsindeksen/artikler/detaljhandelen-noe-opp-i-oktober)). No consensus was reported for this release or for NAV's.
- **Credit.** Household credit growth was unchanged at 4.5 per cent over twelve months in October ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/bank-og-finansmarked/finansielle-indikatorer/statistikk/kredittindikator/artikler/innenlandsk-gjeld)).

*Reading.* Weaker output growth, a labour market that eases only gradually and steady consumption are broadly consistent with a policy rate held at 4.5 per cent; the week's data do not strengthen the case for a further hike.

## What others say

- **On the economy and the rate.** Kyrre M. Knudsen, chief economist at SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge, said the GDP figures are "positivt for alle som ønsker rentekutt", as lower growth can make it easier to approach the 2 per cent target, while noting that registered unemployment is still low ([NTB](https://sol.no/nyheter/positivt-for-alle-som-onsker-rentekutt/85473160)). Dane Cekov of SpareBank 1 Markets said that "alle tekniske indikatorer skrek at denne kronestyrkingen hadde gått for langt" and expects some further mild weakening towards year-end ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/mMRoX0/kronen-svekkes-hadde-gaatt-for-langt)).
- **Standing views.** Kjersti Haugland of DNB Carnegie expects 4.5 per cent well into next year but calls the krone "en joker" ([Dagbladet Børsen](https://borsen.dagbladet.no/nyheter/en-joker-kan-avgjore/85377056)). None of the economists quoted expects a hike on 17 December. No new market pricing was reported during the week; the last reported reading is about a 35 per cent probability of a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** Over the week EUR/NOK fell 0.13 per cent to 10.702, USD/NOK rose 0.40 per cent to 9.809 and I-44 was unchanged at 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone), after a high of 111.47 on Wednesday.

**Why.** In the first half of the week lower oil and gas prices and the sell-off in technology shares weighed on the krone: a small currency tends to weaken when investors reduce risk. From Thursday, the return of risk appetite and attacks in the Gulf that supported oil reversed the move. The differentials explain little.

**Differentials.** The policy rate differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points. The 12-month differential in government rates was 1.80 percentage points at Thursday's fix, about 3 basis points wider over the week, and 0.21 percentage point against the United States. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 14.5 basis points to 4.14 per cent, against 3 basis points for the German 10-year, so the long differential narrowed by about 11 basis points to 0.78 percentage point.
