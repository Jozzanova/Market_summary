# Markets, weekly report week 48 (20–27 November 2026)

**The week reversed much of the previous week's fall in equities. Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia's chief executive, with the US administration's support, set off the largest one-day rise in US equities since April 2025 on Monday; the recovery faltered on Tuesday and Wednesday and broadened again in Friday's shortened session. Government bonds lost their haven role as risk appetite returned, so US yields ended the week slightly higher, while yields in Norway and Japan fell. The krone ended the week little changed after weakening in the first half.**

## Equities

**United States.** The S&P 500 rose 5.60 per cent over the week, its best week since April 2025, the Nasdaq 6.68 per cent, the Dow 5.46 per cent and the Russell 2000 6.53 per cent ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/2026/11/27/stocks-jump-to-close-out-an-erratic-week-that-was-also-wall-streets-best-in-more-than-a-year/)). The path was uneven. On Monday the S&P 500 rose 4.73 per cent and Nvidia 31.5 per cent after its board said, citing input from the US government, that Huang's return "poses no national security risk" ([Nvidia](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm)). On Tuesday semiconductors gave up most of that gain, with the SOX down 7.88 per cent and Anthropic below its offering price ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/market-outlook/2026/11/24/tech-stocks-wane-a-day-after-a-huang-rally-as-anthropic-tumbles-below-its-initial-offering-price/)). On Wednesday the S&P 500 fell as much as 3.2 per cent during the day but closed 0.25 per cent lower. Markets were closed on Thursday, and on Friday the S&P 500 rose 1.60 per cent and the Nasdaq 2.31 per cent, with about four in five stocks higher, a broader rise than earlier in the week; Amazon rose 4 per cent as the holiday season began and Anthropic 11 per cent ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/2026/11/27/stocks-jump-to-close-out-an-erratic-week-that-was-also-wall-streets-best-in-more-than-a-year/)).

**AI and semiconductors.** The SOX rose 9.25 per cent over the week, NYSE FANG+ 7.70 per cent, the Magnificent Seven ETF 7.90 per cent and the AI ETF AIQ 8.74 per cent, all more than the S&P 500; the SOX is still 21.37 per cent lower over the month. Among sectors, materials (7.88 per cent), technology (7.48 per cent) and communication services (7.45 per cent) rose most, while energy (1.92 per cent), utilities and consumer staples (both about 2.4 per cent) lagged, the pattern of a week in which investors moved back into the shares they had sold. The FT notes that the administration's intervention at Nvidia has a cost of its own, as political involvement in single companies adds a risk that is hard to price ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/3318a827-bf20-4826-947d-9327381c3c10?syn-25a6b1a6=1)). The VIX fell 12.1 points to 23.8.

**Europe.** STOXX Europe 600 rose 2.91 per cent, the DAX 2.57 per cent, the FTSE 100 3.41 per cent and the CAC 40 2.88 per cent. Thursday's rise of 1.75 per cent in STOXX Europe 600, when the United States capped its proposed tariffs at the rates in existing trade deals, was the largest of the week ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/european-stocks-rally-tech-banks-083233000.html)). Euro-area banks rose 1.78 per cent, less than the market, after a fall on Wednesday when the ECB was reported to be discussing higher unremunerated reserve requirements, which would lower banks' interest income ([Reuters](https://longbridge.com/en/news/306829837)).

**Norway.** Oslo Børs rose 1.74 per cent, less than abroad given its small technology weight. Vår Energi agreed to buy ConocoPhillips' Norwegian business on Monday ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/z0Gj1r/vaar-energi-kjoeper-conocophillips-norske-virksomhet-i-avtale-verdt-43-milliarder-kroner)), Tomra fell 8.8 per cent on Tuesday after lowering its margin target, and Norse Atlantic was declared bankrupt on Friday after the lessors terminated the leases on all twelve aircraft; Kongsberg Gruppen rose 4.7 per cent on Friday ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/wdPMQ1/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)).

**Asia.** The Nikkei rose 4.03 per cent, the Hang Seng 1.27 per cent, the Shanghai Composite 2.06 per cent and KOSPI 0.94 per cent. KOSPI moved most during the week, up about 7 per cent on Tuesday morning and down about 6 per cent on Wednesday morning, as Samsung and SK hynix followed the swings in Nvidia.

## Fixed income and credit

The US 5-year yield rose 4.5 basis points over the week to 4.75 per cent and the 10-year 2.4 basis points to 4.94 per cent. Yields fell until Wednesday as investors sought safety during the intraday sell-off, and rose about 9 basis points on Friday, when equities rose and demand for havens eased ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/2026/11/27/stocks-jump-to-close-out-an-erratic-week-that-was-also-wall-streets-best-in-more-than-a-year/)). Core PCE inflation above expectations on Wednesday kept a Fed hike on 9 December priced: Fed funds futures imply 23.6 basis points, against 22 a week earlier. The German 10-year fell 3 basis points to 3.36 per cent, the Japanese 10-year about 17 basis points to 2.70 per cent through Thursday, and the Norwegian 10-year (live) 14.5 basis points to 4.14 per cent. The US 10-year breakeven was unchanged at 2.15 per cent.

## Commodities and currencies

**Oil and gas.** Brent's January contract, which expires at the end of the month, rose 3.10 per cent over the week to 95.44 dollars; the February contract, which most outlets now quote, ended Friday at 90.84 dollars ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/2026/11/27/stocks-jump-to-close-out-an-erratic-week-that-was-also-wall-streets-best-in-more-than-a-year/)). Oil fell on Monday as President Trump signalled more time for diplomacy with Iran and rose midweek after attacks on tankers and Gulf refineries; on Friday the February contract settled lower ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/oil-prices-rise-ahead-of-opec-meeting-as-middle-east-supply-risks-persist-63fbb550?mod=rss_worldnews)). TTF gas fell 5.94 per cent to 83.20 euros as weather forecasts turned milder.

**Metals.** Gold rose 3.53 per cent to 3,640 dollars and silver 5.20 per cent, recovering part of the previous week's losses as risk appetite returned and the forced selling eased, that is, investors no longer needed to sell metals to cover losses elsewhere ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/precious-metals-rally-as-ai-trade-revives-risk-appetite-86477a98?mod=rss_markets_main)).

**Currencies.** The dollar index rose 0.19 per cent to 104.01; EUR/USD was unchanged at 1.097 and USD/JPY eased to 159.3. The krone weakened in the first half of the week, with I-44 at 111.47 on Wednesday, as oil fell and the sell-off in technology shares reduced risk appetite, and recovered on Thursday and Friday. Over the week EUR/NOK fell 0.13 per cent to 10.702, USD/NOK rose 0.40 per cent to 9.809 and I-44 was unchanged at 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone).

## The week ahead

The OPEC+ ministerial meeting, which is to set the production framework for 2027, and the Spanish general election are on Sunday. The euro-area flash HICP for November follows on Tuesday, Jensen Huang appears before the House Foreign Affairs Committee on 1 December and the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December, and the US employment report for November is published on Friday 4 December, the last major release before the Fed decision on 9 December.
