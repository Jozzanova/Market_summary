# Macro, weekly report week 48 (20–27 November 2026)

**The week's data confirmed an economy in the United States that is still growing fast with core inflation still rising, and the market ended the week pricing a Fed hike on 9 December almost fully. In the euro area the account of the October meeting and several Governing Council members pointed to a hike on 17 December, the Bank of Korea raised its policy rate and Tokyo inflation strengthened the case for a Bank of Japan hike. No G10 central bank changed its policy rate. In Norway, mainland growth was weaker than Norges Bank expected.**

- **United States: strong growth, firm inflation.** Core PCE prices, the Fed's preferred measure, rose 3.3 per cent over twelve months in October, against 3.2 per cent expected, and third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/pce-inflation-report-october-2026.html)). The Beige Book gave a more mixed picture, with consumer spending broadly flat ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/beigebook202611-summary.htm)). Consumer confidence rose 11.4 points to 103.3, probably helped by lower petrol prices since October ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html)). The flash PMIs showed slower but solid growth with easing input costs in the United States and the euro area ([Reuters](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1123/1600272-euro-zone-growth-pmi/)).
- **Asia-Pacific.** Australian CPI rose 4.9 per cent and the trimmed mean 3.8 per cent ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/consumer-price-index-australia/latest-release)), while the market had by then priced out further RBA hikes after weak October employment. Tokyo core inflation, which excludes fresh food and is published three weeks before the national figures, reached a sixteen-month high in November ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/shares-footing-bruising-month-dollar-015346000.html)).

## Federal Reserve

- **Pricing.** Fed funds futures imply a Fed hike of 23.6 basis points on 9 December, a probability of about 94 per cent, and a further 17.4 basis points by 27 January; a week earlier the figures were about 22 and 16 basis points. On Tuesday, after Monday's recovery in equities, the December figure briefly reached 25.6 basis points, a fully priced quarter-point move.
- **Williams.** New York Fed President John Williams said inflation is "currently about 3-1/2 percent" ([New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/wil261123)) and that he "would not exclude... moving at consecutive meetings" ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/fed-lower-inflation-williams-says-210546185.html)). As vice chair of the FOMC his view is read as close to the leadership's. The blackout period before the December meeting begins today, so no further Fed speeches on policy are expected.

## European Central Bank

- **Account: support for a hike was already broad in October.** "A large number of members" said they could have supported a hike in October, and the hold was accepted on condition that the communication made clear that the direction of travel was upward ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/accounts/2026/html/ecb.mg261126~ccf28c8071.en.html)). Gediminas Šimkus doubted that one hike in December would be enough ([Reuters](https://1027wbow.com/2026/11/23/december-rate-hike-may-not-be-enough-but-not-in-a-rush-to-move-again-ecbs-simkus/)), Olaf Sleijpen said the direction for December is "clear" ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/ecb-policy-direction-clear-december-150810000.html)), and on Friday Vice President Boris Vujčić and Martin Kocher suggested that policy will need to be tightened further, according to the Austrian daily Kurier ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/vujcic-and-kocher-hint-at-more-ecb-rate-hiking-kurier-reports)). The last reported pricing is an 87 per cent probability of an ECB hike of 25 basis points on 17 December (LSEG data, 25 November).
- **Succession.** Euro-area leaders recommended Pablo Hernández de Cos as President from 8 February 2027 ([Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/26/european-council-puts-forward-pablo-hernandez-de-cos-as-next-president-of-the-european-central-bank/)), a choice investors read as continuity.

## Other central banks

- **Bank of Korea** raised its policy rate to 3.25 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/bok-rate-decision-korea-gdp-inflation.html)). **Bank of Japan** board member Naoki Tamura argued for timely hikes ([Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/bojs-tamura-signals-chance-of-fasterpace-interest-rate-hikes-5051855)); together with the Tokyo figures this supports a hike on 18 December. **Bank of England**: the Financial Policy Committee described AI financing as "increasingly complex, circular and opaque", meaning that the same firms increasingly lend to, invest in and buy from one another, so losses could spread in ways that are hard to trace ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2026/november-2026)).

## Assessment

The balance of risks still points to higher policy rates in the United States, the euro area and Japan. In the United States, firm core inflation and strong growth leave little room for the Fed to pause in December, and the November employment report on 4 December is the last major test. In the euro area, the account and the week's speeches suggest that only a clearly better outlook in the December staff projections would prevent a hike. The downside risks are the volatility of AI shares, which the Bank of England now names as a source of systemic risk, and the US tariff proposal, although its cap at the rates in existing trade deals has reduced the worst outcome. In Norway, weak mainland growth and a stable labour market point to somewhat less pressure on capacity than Norges Bank assumed.
