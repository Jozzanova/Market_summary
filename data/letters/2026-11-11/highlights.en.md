# Key points, Wednesday 11 November 2026

**Markets.** US equities fell on Tuesday: the S&P 500 lost 0.38 per cent to 7,521 and the Nasdaq 0.74 per cent, while STOXX Europe 600 rose 0.27 per cent and Oslo Børs 0.09 per cent. In Asia this morning the Nikkei is up 1.3 per cent, the KOSPI up 2.5 per cent and the Hang Seng down 0.8 per cent. The US 10-year yield rose about 12 basis points to 4.98 per cent; the latest official fixes, from Monday, have the euro-area AAA 10-year at 3.27 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year at 4.28 per cent, so they do not yet reflect Tuesday. The dollar index is 102.25, EUR/USD 1.117 (−0.5 per cent) and USD/JPY 158.7. Brent is 92.4 dollars, 1.0 per cent higher after a two-month low on Tuesday.

**Drivers.** US consumer prices rose 0.4 per cent in October and core prices 0.3 per cent, against expectations of 0.3 and 0.2 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/cpi-inflation-report-october-2026.html)). Fed officials responded firmly: Goolsbee called the report "borderline disturbing" ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/u-s-inflation-report-highlights-strain-of-rising-food-energy-and-housing-costs)), and Waller said a 50 basis point hike "should be considered" in December ([Reuters](https://investing.com/news/economy-news/investors-boost-odds-of-halfpoint-fed-hike-as-core-cpi-rise-meets-wallers-bar-5016055)). Fed funds futures now fully price a 25 basis point hike on 9 December. At the same London conference the Bank of England's Lombardelli and the ECB's Lane pointed to further tightening ([Bloomberg](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/nov/10/officials-from-major-central-banks-signal-more-rat/)). Among equities, Nvidia fell 5.5 per cent amid the inquiry into its chief executive.

**Norway.** The krone weakened: EUR/NOK is 10.605 (+0.5 per cent), USD/NOK 9.497 (+1.0 per cent), and I-44 rose 0.7 per cent to 109.57 at Tuesday's fix, 2.7 per cent weaker on the week. The larger move against the dollar suggests that the stronger dollar after the US print, together with Brent's low on Tuesday, weighed on the krone; no source attributes the move. The differential against the euro area is unchanged at 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate and about 1.0 point at ten years. Economists quoted after Tuesday's CPI expect an unchanged rate in December; no post-print market pricing has been reported. Norges Bank publishes its Financial Stability Report this morning.
