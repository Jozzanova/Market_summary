# Norges Bank, Wednesday 11 November 2026

**Summary.** No new Norwegian data or post-CPI economist comments were published in the last 24 hours, so the standing expectation is unchanged: those quoted after Tuesday's CPI expect the policy rate to stay at 4.50 per cent in December. The new information comes from abroad. US inflation was higher than expected, which strengthened the dollar and raised foreign rate expectations, and Brent touched a two-month low. Both weakened the krone, which is now at 10.61 against the euro. Norges Bank publishes its Financial Stability Report at 08:30 today.

## New data

There were no new Norwegian statistics since Tuesday's CPI ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/priser-og-prisindekser/konsumpriser/statistikk/konsumprisindeksen/artikler/stromprisene-trakk-opp-prisveksten-i-oktober)). Inflation was 3.6 per cent in October and CPI-ATE 2.7 per cent, against a Bloomberg consensus of 3.7 and 2.7. Core inflation was in line with Norges Bank's projection. On Thursday Statistics Norway publishes its investment survey for the fourth quarter, which gives the first estimate of petroleum investment in 2027, together with retail trade and job vacancies ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/kalender)).

## What others say

- **Marius Gonsholt Hov, Handelsbanken:** the print "endrer ikke noe på renteforventningene nå" (does not change rate expectations now). A hike before Christmas would have required "en ganske kraftig oppside" in this print and the November one ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).
- **Kyrre M. Knudsen, SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge:** "Dette er gode nyheter, fordi kjerneinflasjonen ble akkurat det Norges Bank hadde trodd" (good news, because core inflation was exactly what Norges Bank expected). He expects a hold and a signal that the peak has been reached ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/alt-blir-dyrere-kpi-pa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober/s/80-95-235224), [TV 2](https://www.tv2.no/nyheter/mener-prisveksten-kan-vaere-gode-nyheter/19412716/)).
- **Harald Magnus Andreassen, SpareBank 1 Markets, on the US figures:** "Momentet er for høyt. Problemet er at det gir seg ikke" (the momentum is too high, and it is not letting up) ([E24](https://e24.no/internasjonal-oekonomi/i/pM6qn1/prisveksten-i-usa-var-hoeyere-enn-ventet-i-oktober)).
- **Before the print:** Frank Jullum, Danske Bank, said the risk of a hike had "not been called off", and Kyrre Aamdal, DNB Carnegie, saw a further hike as more likely in 2027 than in December ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/9Bm1pq/prisveksten-norges-bank-er-bekymret)).

None of the economists quoted after the print expects a hike in December. No updated market pricing has been reported since the estimate of about 35 per cent on 5 November. Higher foreign rates pull in the direction of a firmer path, while a core print on the path pulls against it.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.605, 0.5 per cent higher than on Tuesday and 2.1 per cent higher on the week. USD/NOK rose 1.0 per cent to 9.497, and I-44 rose 0.7 per cent to 109.57 at Tuesday's fix.

**Why the krone weakened.** The move was twice as large against the dollar as against the euro. That points to the dollar's strength after the US print as the main factor, with lower oil as the second; no published source attributes the move.

**Differentials.**
- Against the euro area: 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate; 1.65 points between the Norwegian 3-year and the euro AAA 2-year; about 1.0 point at ten years (Monday's fixes).
- Against the United States: the Norwegian 10-year is about 60 basis points below the US 10-year, and the gap widened on Tuesday.

**Implications.** A firmer US path narrows the krone's rate advantage. A weaker krone lifts imported inflation over time, but I-44 is still 8.6 per cent stronger than at the start of the year.
