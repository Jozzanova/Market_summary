# Politics, Wednesday 11 November 2026

**The political news in the last 24 hours mostly lowered geopolitical risk premia rather than raising them. Both Washington and Beijing are signalling engagement ahead of Trump's visit to China next week. Russia and Ukraine have resumed US-mediated talks. On Iran the United States has toned down its rhetoric, although attacks on shipping in the Gulf continue. Against this, the White House is keeping its tariff policy unchanged.**

## United States

**Hegseth.** The House voted 211–204 to block an impeachment resolution against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth over the conduct of the Iran war ([TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/11/10/hegseth-impeachment-procedural-vote-house/)). Democrats can bring the matter back after 3 January.

**Tariffs.** On trade, White House official Jim Goyer said "we are just getting started" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/trump-tariffs-midterms-gary-cohn-inflation.html)). A Reuters/Ipsos poll finds 62 per cent support for repealing the tariffs. The statement matters for the inflation outlook: continued tariffs keep goods prices up while the Fed is tightening.

**Funding.** Federal funding expires on 11 December, two days after the Fed meeting ([CRA](https://cra.org/govaffairs/blog/2026/09/fy2027-sept-update/)).

## United States and China

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in New York that the aim is to de-risk, not to decouple. In his words, "the United States must produce more of what we consume, while China must consume more of what it produces" ([AP](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/business/treasury-secretary/3FCJH7SWIAZG3CV42VJI55YFNM/)). The trade truce expires in January. President Trump is attending the APEC meeting in Shenzhen on 18–19 November and has said he expects to meet both Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin ([Reuters](https://www.newstribune.com/news/2026/nov/09/trump-thinks-he-will-meet-xi-and-putin-at-china-apec-summit/)). An extension of the truce would reduce the risk of new tariff rounds and supply-chain disruption, particularly for semiconductors.

## Middle East and energy

**Iran.** Oil fell to its lowest since 31 August on Tuesday after US signals that the war against Iran is winding down; Secretary of State Rubio reportedly told G7 allies that sanctions and the naval blockade are working ([Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/2026/11/11/oil-hits-two-month-lows-as-us-eases-iran-fears)).

**Shipping.** A tanker was struck off Oman on Tuesday, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two tankers hit mines ([Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/oil-rises-from-twomonth-low-as-hormuz-shipping-attacks-offset-iran-truce-hopes-5017512)). The risk premium in oil is therefore lower but not gone. For Norway, a lower oil price means weaker terms of trade and a weaker krone.

## Europe and Russia

Russia and Ukraine held about four hours of talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, the first US-mediated round in nine months, with Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff as mediators ([AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/russia-and-ukraine-hold-nearly-4-hours-of-u-s-mediated-talks-in-abu-dhabi)). A large prisoner exchange was discussed. Rubio described the talks as "very productive" but said a solution is not imminent. The talks continue today.

**ECB succession.** Klaas Knot and Pablo Hernández de Cos remain the only candidates to succeed Christine Lagarde, who leaves on 7 February ([DPA](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/dutch-spanish-candidates-vie-succeed-174834620.html)).

## Emerging markets

Brazil's government will present measures in the coming days for a fiscal adjustment of 0.5 per cent of GDP in 2027, to reassure investors ([Reuters](https://www.933thedrive.com/2026/11/10/brazil-plans-measures-for-2027-fiscal-savings-source-says/)).

## Norway

No news from the negotiations on the 2027 budget has been reported since 6 November. The Labour Party has set a deadline of Friday 20 November for an agreement with Sp, SV, Rødt and MDG. Fuel taxes and the level of Norgespris are the main points of disagreement ([NTB](https://www.adressa.no/nyheter/innenriks/i/64r2we/fakta-om-budsjettsamtalene)). The government's proposal uses 608.4 billion kroner from the oil fund, 2.7 per cent of its value.
