# Macro, Wednesday 11 November 2026

**US core inflation rose 0.3 per cent in October, a third consecutive month at that pace and above expectations. Fed officials responded by opening for a larger hike in December. At the same conference in London, officials from the Bank of England and the ECB pointed to further tightening as well. The broad picture is that the major central banks are still in a tightening phase: energy and services prices are keeping inflation above target, and growth has so far held up.**

## Federal Reserve

**The October CPI.** Consumer prices rose 0.4 per cent on the month and core prices 0.3 per cent; the consensus was 0.3 and 0.2 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/cpi-inflation-report-october-2026.html)). The breakdown:
- Shelter rose 0.3 per cent and accounted for more than 30 per cent of the monthly rise.
- Energy rose 0.7 per cent and food 0.5 per cent.
- Used cars fell 1.2 per cent.
- BLS publishes no twelve-month rate this month, because October 2025 was not collected during the shutdown.

Core inflation has now run at 0.3 per cent a month for three months, which corresponds to an annual rate of about 3.5–4 per cent. That is the reason officials no longer treat the rise as transitory.

**Officials' reaction.**
- **Goolsbee:** called the report "borderline disturbing" and said "services inflation is not coming down" ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/u-s-inflation-report-highlights-strain-of-rising-food-energy-and-housing-costs)).
- **Waller:** "a 50-basis-point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting" ([Reuters](https://investing.com/news/economy-news/investors-boost-odds-of-halfpoint-fed-hike-as-core-cpi-rise-meets-wallers-bar-5016055)).
- **Logan:** wants at least another half-point of tightening and said it "may be possible to move more rapidly this winter" ([Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/feds-logan-calls-for-two-more-rate-hikes-possibly-rapidly-5016235)).
- **Paulson:** a voter this year, prefers "further gradual tightening" but would act faster if inflation expectations drift up ([Reuters](https://1027wbow.com/2026/11/10/feds-paulson-calls-for-gradual-rate-hikes-amid-ongoing-high-inflation/)).

**Pricing.** Fed funds futures price 27.6 basis points for 9 December and a further 17 basis points by January. Reuters puts the probability of 50 basis points at about 10 per cent.

## Bank of England and ECB

At the BOE Watchers Conference in London, Deputy Governor Clare Lombardelli said "there is likely to be a case for a further increase in rates in the near term". The ECB's chief economist Philip Lane said a hike would be "prudent" if the data do not change before December ([Bloomberg](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/nov/10/officials-from-major-central-banks-signal-more-rat/)). The think tank NIESR said Bank Rate should reach 5 per cent next year. Markets price about a 70 per cent probability of a hike to 4.25 per cent on 17 December. Euro-area inflation was 4.0 per cent in October and core 2.5 per cent ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/2-04112026-ap)). The higher headline rate leaves the ECB less room to wait, even though core inflation is closer to target.

## Bank of Japan

Reuters reports that Governor Ueda has all but locked in a hike to 1.5 per cent at the 17–18 December meeting ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/analysis-boj-locks-december-hike-053516965.html)). The yen is nonetheless at its weakest in more than a month against the dollar. The differential to US rates widened after the CPI print and outweighs the expected Japanese hike.

## Assessment

The risks to inflation are tilted to the upside in the United States, the United Kingdom and the euro area. Rising core rates and firm services prices suggest that the tightening so far has not been sufficient. Lower oil works in the opposite direction: Brent is 11 per cent lower than a week ago. If that persists, it will dampen headline inflation in the first half of next year. Growth risks are increasing, however. Higher short rates and a stronger dollar point to tighter financial conditions, a risk Governor Bowman highlighted on Tuesday ([Reuters](https://investing.com/news/economy-news/investors-boost-odds-of-halfpoint-fed-hike-as-core-cpi-rise-meets-wallers-bar-5016055)). For Norway, the international trend means higher foreign rates. All else equal, that narrows the krone's interest-rate advantage and can weaken the krone.
