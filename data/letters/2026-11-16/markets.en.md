# Markets, Monday 16 November 2026

**This report covers Friday's session and the weekend. Friday ended a week in which equities rose and Treasuries had their worst week since April 2025: semiconductor shares fell back on the uncertainty over Nvidia's leadership, while US yields rose further as the Fed's December hike became fully priced. This morning the chip sell-off has spread to Tokyo, Seoul and Taipei, and weak Japanese and Chinese activity data have added to it; oil is little changed despite a new Iranian drone attack on Bahrain.**

## Equities

**United States.** On Friday the S&P 500 fell 0.41 per cent and the Nasdaq Composite 0.91 per cent, while the Dow rose 0.10 per cent; over the week the three gained 2.1–2.5 per cent, the best week since early August ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The AI and semiconductor proxies fell markedly more than the market: SOX fell 3.43 per cent, NYSE FANG+ 1.00 per cent and the AI ETF AIQ 1.29 per cent, while the Magnificent Seven ETF fell only 0.29 per cent. AMD fell 8.8 per cent and Broadcom 4.8 per cent ([AP](https://www.wral.com/news/ap/35786-stocks-decline-in-asia-as-investor-concerns-over-nvidia-fallout-extend-into-another-week/)). Nvidia fell about 2 per cent after HSBC cut its rating to hold, arguing that "the uncertainties will prevail until a permanent succession plan is laid out", and lost about 13 per cent over the week ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/15/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). Over the weekend the FT reported that former chief executive Jensen Huang is discussing a return with allies ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/huang-discusses-comeback-as-nvidia-chief-with-allies-ft-says)). Consumer staples (+1.4 per cent) and communication services (+1.1 per cent) rose, and VIX rose 0.6 points to 19.3.

**Europe and Norway.** STOXX Europe 600 was unchanged on Friday and rose 2.4 per cent over the week; the DAX gained 3.6 per cent on the week. Oslo Børs rose 0.25 per cent, led by Equinor (+1.87 per cent, despite trading ex-dividend), while HydrogenPro fell 24 per cent ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/Qroo2P/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)); over the week the index rose 2.85 per cent, helped by higher oil prices early in the week ([DN](https://www.dn.no/bors/oslo-bors/hydrogenpro/bitcoin/oslo-bors-endte-opp-nar-tre-prosent-for-uken/2-1-2073730)).

**Asia this morning.** The Nikkei is down 2.1 per cent and KOSPI 3.2 per cent, as chip-equipment and memory shares follow Friday's US fall: Advantest fell about 10 per cent, Tokyo Electron 5.2 per cent and SK Hynix 5.5 per cent, and Taiwan's index fell about 4 per cent at midday ([AP](https://www.wral.com/news/ap/35786-stocks-decline-in-asia-as-investor-concerns-over-nvidia-fallout-extend-into-another-week/)). The Hang Seng is up 0.8 per cent, and the Shanghai Composite is down 0.4 per cent. New World Development fell 47 per cent in Hong Kong after announcing a rights issue at a deep discount ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/9daa3a26-c49c-4c94-91af-0d4c2116d2e4)).

## Fixed income and credit

The US 10-year yield rose 5 basis points on Friday to 5.15 per cent and the 5-year 4 basis points to 4.98 per cent; over the week the 5-year rose 33 basis points and the 10-year 30 basis points. Reuters attributes the move to firm inflation and a Fed that may "need to act a little bit quicker", in the words of a TD Securities strategist ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449075/us-treasuries-two-year-yields-headed-for-biggest-weekly-jump-in-two-years)). Fed funds futures price a Fed hike of about 26 basis points on 9 December and a further 20 basis points by 27 January. The German 10-year yield rose 7 basis points on Friday to 3.43 per cent and 14 basis points over the week. In Japan the 10-year yield is about 2.89 per cent after the GDP release ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/japan-q3-gdp-energy-oil-prices.html)). US high-yield spreads narrowed 28 basis points over the week to Thursday, so the equity volatility has not spread to credit.

## Commodities and currencies

**Energy.** Brent is 95.27 dollars this morning, 0.6 per cent below Friday's close and 2.5 per cent lower over last week. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they sent six drones at a US base in Bahrain on Sunday, and Bahrain said it intercepted several attacks ([Reuters](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/11/15/bahrain-iran-drone-attacks-tehran-us-base.html)); the price reaction has been limited, as the IEA's estimate that supply exceeds demand by 1.7 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter appears to weigh more ([Reuters](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/oil-prices-fall-on-expectations-of-coming-supply-surplus.html)). TTF gas fell 1.4 per cent on Friday to 90.95 euros.

**Metals.** Gold is 3,871 dollars, 0.4 per cent lower, after a fall of 4.6 per cent last week as real yields rose. Copper fell 1.2 per cent to the lowest in more than four months on the tech sell-off and the Chinese data ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/copper-sinks-to-four-month-low-on-tech-rout-china-growth-woes)).

**Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.44, EUR/USD 1.102 and USD/JPY 159.6; the yen has changed little despite the weak GDP figure. EUR/NOK is 10.553, 0.14 per cent below Friday's close, USD/NOK 9.576 and I-44 (a rise is a weaker krone) was 109.34 at Friday's fix. The krone is firm against the euro, supported by an oil price close to 95 dollars and a 12-month differential in government rates of 1.82 percentage points, but weaker against the dollar, against which the differential is only 0.13 percentage points. Bitcoin is about 64,600 dollars, 10 per cent lower than a week earlier.
