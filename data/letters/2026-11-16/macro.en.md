# Macro, Monday 16 November 2026

**No central bank met over Friday and the weekend, but the communication continued to point to further tightening in the United States and the United Kingdom. Boston Fed president Susan Collins argued for a gradual pace, which tempers the case for a half-point move in December, while Bank of England chief economist Huw Pill signalled support for another hike. This morning's data from Japan and China were weaker than expected and complicate the outlook for a Bank of Japan hike in December.**

## Federal Reserve

On Saturday Susan Collins said that "a steady, gradual path seems appropriate" at the moment, while adding that she is open-minded and noting that the policy path next year "might be a bit higher" than she had thought ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/feds-collins-says-gradual-rate-154619000.html)). On Friday Chicago Fed president Austan Goolsbee said inflation has spread to services and medical care and that the Fed "has definitely got work to do", while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the Fed "should understand that this is an energy supply shock" ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/us-treasury-chief-says-fed-140232568.html)). The futures market prices about 26 basis points for 9 December and 20 more by 27 January; according to CME data reported by Reuters the probability of 50 basis points in December is about 10 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449075/us-treasuries-two-year-yields-headed-for-biggest-weekly-jump-in-two-years)). Collins's remarks support the 25 basis point path rather than an acceleration.

## ECB and Bank of England

Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel said on Friday that a further ECB hike is "highly probable" if conditions remain as they are ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/nagel-says-highly-probable-that-ecb-will-have-to-act-again)). With euro-area inflation at 4.0 per cent in October and third-quarter growth of 0.4 per cent ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/2-13112026-ap)), the case for a December move rests on preventing high inflation from feeding into expectations. In an FT interview Huw Pill said the rise to 4 per cent was a significant step but that "we have further to go" ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/14/interest-rate-rises-inflation-bank-of-england-chief-economist)). UK consumer prices for October on Wednesday are the next test.

## Bank of Japan and China

Japan's GDP fell at an annualised rate of 0.1 per cent in the third quarter, against an expected rise of 0.3 per cent; net exports subtracted 0.6 percentage points as energy imports became more expensive ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/japan-q3-gdp-energy-oil-prices.html)). Weak consumption, partly due to heavy rain, contributed ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/4eb7ff18-1ba3-4944-ad48-bdc9e5ddc84e)). Markets still price about a 76 per cent chance of a Bank of Japan hike on 18 December (LSEG, same CNBC source): the weak yen keeps import prices high, while the contraction argues for caution.

{bars: China, October, per cent from a year earlier | Industrial output 4.8; Retail sales 0.4; Fixed-asset investment, Jan–Oct −8.1 | | NBS}

China's industrial output rose 4.8 per cent from a year earlier in October, against an expected 5.3 per cent, retail sales only 0.4 per cent and fixed-asset investment fell 8.1 per cent in the first ten months ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/china-retail-sales-october-industrial-output-investment.html)). The People's Bank of China nonetheless drained funds through its medium-term lending facility for the first time in five months ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/pboc-withdraws-medium-term-funds-in-warning-sign-to-easing-bets)), which suggests little appetite for near-term easing. Weaker Chinese demand weighs on metals prices and on exporters of capital goods.

## Assessment

The balance of risks is still tilted towards higher rates in the United States, the euro area and the United Kingdom, where inflation remains well above target. The new elements are on the growth side: the contraction in Japan, weaker Chinese demand and a fall in AI-related equities that could tighten financial conditions. Lower oil prices since October dampen the energy component of inflation, but not yet the core measures, so the central banks are likely to give more weight to the inflation outlook than to the growth risks at the December meetings.
