# Markets, Friday 13 November 2026

**Thursday's session was shaped less by Nvidia's leadership change, which equities largely absorbed, than by a further repricing of the Federal Reserve: Treasury yields rose about ten basis points as several Fed officials argued for more tightening, and the dollar held its gains. Oil rose markedly after President Trump played down the need for a deal with Iran, which supported the krone against the euro.**

## Equities

The S&P 500 fell 0.35 per cent, the Nasdaq 0.39 per cent and the Dow 0.36 per cent, after an opening fall of 0.8 per cent in the S&P 500 and 1.5 per cent in the Nasdaq ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). Nvidia confirmed in the morning that Jensen Huang had resigned and that chief financial officer Colette Kress would serve as interim chief executive; the shares touched a 52-week low and closed 4.3 per cent lower on volume of more than 370 million shares, about two and a half times the 30-day average (same source). Investors quoted by the FT expect demand for AI hardware to carry the company regardless ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/ecb2b4ed-1b61-4c8a-a2e7-a6f9c85a59ff)), and AMD and Broadcom rose on expectations that they could win business. The small-cap Russell 2000 fell 1.66 per cent, consistent with the rise in yields weighing most on rate-sensitive companies.

The AI proxies moved broadly in line with the market: SOX fell 0.54 per cent and SMH 1.03 per cent, while NYSE FANG+ and the Magnificent Seven ETF were close to unchanged; the robotics and AI ETF BOTZ fell 2.5 per cent. Energy was the only sector to rise markedly (+1.5 per cent), following oil; materials fell 1.3 per cent. The VIX rose 6.9 per cent to 18.7. Over the week the S&P 500 is still 3.3 per cent higher and SOX 16.6 per cent.

STOXX Europe 600 rose 0.14 per cent and the DAX 0.41 per cent, while euro-area banks fell 0.7 per cent. Mercedes-Benz said it would halt all future investment in Germany ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/f4a1fd87-9abc-4965-963a-a5349dbfdab7)). Oslo Børs was unchanged: Equinor rose 2.2 per cent and Aker BP 1.3 per cent with oil, while Kongsberg Gruppen fell 2.4 per cent and Okeanis 2 per cent despite record results ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/kMa45v/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)).

In Asia the KOSPI opened 2.8 per cent higher and the Nikkei 1.1 per cent as chip stocks recovered ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)), but the gains have narrowed: the Nikkei is up 0.83 per cent, while the KOSPI is down 0.62 per cent and the Hang Seng 0.93 per cent. US index futures point higher, with the Nasdaq 100 contract up 1.3 per cent.

## Fixed income and credit

Treasuries fell for a second session ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/treasury-yields-rise-as-fed-rate-hike-expectations-remain-strong-401fed89)): the 5-year yield rose 12 basis points to 4.95 per cent, the 10-year 10.5 to 5.10 per cent and the 30-year 8 to 5.43 per cent, so the curve flattened somewhat beyond five years. New York Fed President John Williams said inflation is "far too high" and that there is still work to do ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/fed-s-williams-says-inflation-far-too-high-still-work-to-do)). Fed funds futures price 27 basis points for 9 December, a fully priced hike with a small probability of a larger move, and about 20 basis points more by January. Japanese government bonds followed Treasuries lower this morning ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/japan-bonds-fall-as-treasury-rout-extends-on-fed-hike-bets)). In Europe the move was small: the German 10-year fell 1 basis point to 3.36 per cent and the 2-year rose 1 basis point. The Norwegian 10-year was 4.30 per cent at Wednesday's fix. US high-yield spreads were 3.30 per cent on Wednesday.

## Commodities and currencies

**Oil.** Brent settled 3.3 per cent higher at 95.82 dollars after President Trump wrote that "we don't need a deal with Iran" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)), which the FT reads as an end to the vice-president's push for a peace deal ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/3c1727bb-2966-4e5c-808b-873d14854fa2)). Brent is 95.6 dollars this morning. TTF gas rose 6.5 per cent to 92.3 euros; no source attributes the full move, but it coincides with the reduced prospect of an Iran settlement.

**Metals.** Gold is 3,914 dollars, up 0.75 per cent as buyers returned after a fall to the lowest level in more than a year ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/gold-holds-drop-to-near-one-year-low-as-rate-hike-bets-increase)). Copper edged up from a three-month low ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/copper-climbs-from-three-month-low-after-rough-week-for-metals)).

**Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.2 and EUR/USD 1.106 (−0.3 per cent). USD/JPY is 159.9 after crossing 160; Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said officials will continue to work with Washington and take appropriate action ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/japan-s-katayama-warns-of-fx-steps-after-one-sided-rapid-moves)). The krone strengthened against the euro, EUR/NOK 10.577 (−0.21 per cent), but weakened slightly against the dollar, USD/NOK 9.563 (+0.11 per cent); I-44 fell 0.39 per cent to 109.28 at Thursday's fix. Higher oil is the likely support against the euro. Bitcoin fell 7.6 per cent; no source explaining the move could be verified.
