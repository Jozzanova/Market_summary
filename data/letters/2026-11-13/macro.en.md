# Macro, Friday 13 November 2026

**None of the major central banks met in the last 24 hours, but the Federal Reserve's communication moved further toward tightening: three Reserve Bank presidents said more increases are likely to be needed, and the market has fully priced a hike on 9 December. The US data were mixed, with a small rise in jobless claims and a further fall in home sales, while UK growth in the third quarter matched expectations and keeps a December hike by the Bank of England in view. Today's US producer prices are the next test of the inflation picture.**

## Federal Reserve

New York Fed President John Williams said inflation is "far too high" and that the Fed still has work to do to get policy to the right place ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/fed-s-williams-says-inflation-far-too-high-still-work-to-do)). San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said more rate increases are likely to be needed to cool inflation ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/fed-s-daly-says-more-rate-hikes-likely-needed-to-cool-inflation)), and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack that more restrictive rates are needed ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/fed-s-hammack-says-more-restrictive-rates-are-needed)); she favours gradual increases and said she will keep an open mind about December ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The remarks confirm the direction but say little about the pace: futures price 27 basis points for December and about 20 more by January, so a half-point move remains a minority outcome. The Fed also published its 2027 calendar; the October 2027 meeting will be devoted to strategy issues, with no policy decision ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261112a.htm)).

## Bank of England and Bank of Japan

UK GDP rose 0.7 per cent in the third quarter, in line with the consensus, and 0.2 per cent in September alone, above an expected flat reading; information and communication grew 2.1 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/uk-gdp-third-quarter-2026.html)). Markets price about a 73 per cent chance of a December hike (LSEG data, same source). Activity that is holding up with inflation at 3.5 per cent leaves the Bank little reason to pause. In Japan, the yen near 160 per dollar has prompted renewed warnings from the Ministry of Finance ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/japan-s-katayama-warns-of-fx-steps-after-one-sided-rapid-moves)); a weaker yen adds to import prices and supports the case for a Bank of Japan hike in December.

## Data of note

- **Labour market:** initial jobless claims rose 9,000 to 200,000 in the week to 7 November, against an expected 195,000; continuing claims fell to just under 1.7 million ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The level remains low and does not by itself alter the picture of a firm labour market.
- **Housing:** existing home sales fell 5.5 per cent in October to an annual rate of 3.75 million, the lowest since 2010, with mortgage rates the highest since 2000 apart from one month in 2023 (same source; [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/1e08661e-1db0-4ff7-8236-77254bb05790)). Housing is the clearest channel through which tighter policy is already working.
- **Prices:** the latest figures on file are CPI inflation of 3.7 per cent and PCE inflation of 3.6 per cent in September, with core PCE at 3.1 per cent. Today's producer prices are expected to rise 0.4 per cent on the month and 6.6 per cent on the year (Dow Jones poll, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).
- **Elsewhere:** Peru raised its policy rate for the first time in nearly four years on food and energy prices ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/peru-raises-interest-rates-for-first-time-in-nearly-four-years)).

## Assessment

The balance of risks still points toward higher policy rates in the United States, the United Kingdom and Japan. The new element is that Fed officials across the spectrum now share the view that further increases are needed, while the debate concerns pace. The risk to that view comes from activity: housing is weakening and claims are edging up, and a disorderly fall in AI-related equities after Nvidia's leadership change would tighten financial conditions further. Higher oil after the Iran comments works the other way, by keeping headline inflation high.
