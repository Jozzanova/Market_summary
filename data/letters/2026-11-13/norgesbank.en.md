# Norges Bank, Friday 13 November 2026

**Summary.** The new Norwegian information in the last 24 hours is Statistics Norway's investment survey, which lowered this year's petroleum investment estimate, and a public debate on the credibility of the inflation target, in which professor Kjetil Storesletten argued for a policy rate of 5–6 per cent. No bank economist changed view; those quoted after the October CPI expect a hold in December, and the latest reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike. The krone strengthened against the euro as oil rose.

## New data

**Investment survey.** Statistics Norway now estimates petroleum investment at 266 billion kroner in 2026, 9 billion lower than in the previous survey and below 272.9 billion in 2025, and gives a first estimate of 237 billion for 2027 ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/Qrlp88/jekker-ned-aarets-oljeinvesteringer)). The 2027 figure will rise as new development plans are submitted, and project costs have increased by about 35 billion kroner since last autumn, much of it to be spent in 2027. First estimates are routinely revised up, so the figures point to a gradual decline in petroleum investment rather than a sharp fall; the impulse to the mainland economy from the sector is likely to weaken somewhat in 2027.

**Today:** the balance of payments for the third quarter at 08:00 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/en/kalender)).

## What others say

- **Kjetil Storesletten, professor at the University of Minnesota,** has argued that the tightening came too late and is too weak, and that the policy rate should be 5–6 per cent; he warns that high household inflation expectations can feed into wage demands ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/rente-og-inflasjon-eksperter-frykter-tapt-tillit-til-norges-bank/s/80-95-240062)).
- **Torfinn Harding, professor at Kristiania University College:** "Folk har ikke lenger tillit til inflasjonsmålet" (people no longer trust the inflation target) (same source). The article notes that households in the Expectations Survey expect about 5 per cent annual inflation over five years, against just over 2.5 per cent for economists and the social partners.
- **The standing bank views** are unchanged: Marius Gonsholt Hov of Handelsbanken expects a hold this year, and Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects a hold in December and a signal that the peak has been reached ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).

None of the bank economists quoted expects a hike in December. The case for tightening is argued from outside the banks and from abroad, where the Fed is fully priced to hike on 9 December and the Bank of England at about 73 per cent; the Expectations Survey on 19 November will show whether the gap in household expectations persists.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.577, 0.21 per cent lower than on Thursday, and USD/NOK 9.563, 0.11 per cent higher. I-44 fell 0.39 per cent to 109.28 at Thursday's fix and is 1.41 per cent weaker on the week.

**Why.** The krone strengthened against the euro as Brent rose 3.3 per cent after President Trump's comments on Iran ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)), the usual terms-of-trade channel; the firm dollar on Fed expectations held it back against the dollar.

**Differentials.**
- Against the euro area: 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate; 1.84 points at twelve months in government rates (the Norwegian 12-month bill against the euro AAA 1-year rate, a stand-in for the swap differential), 3 basis points narrower; 0.96 points at ten years (Wednesday's fixes).
- Against the United States: 0.21 points at twelve months in government rates, 20 basis points narrower as US short rates rose.
