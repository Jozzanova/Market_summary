# Macro, Thursday 12 November 2026

**There were no policy decisions among the major central banks in the last 24 hours. The day's information came from speeches and data that point the same way as earlier in the week: price pressures remain high in the United States and Japan, and markets expect further tightening. In the United States a December hike is fully priced; in Japan board member Toichiro Asada argued for a cautious pace of rate rises while producer prices accelerated. In Norway, Norges Bank's Financial Stability Report described a robust system but a higher risk of serious events.**

## Federal Reserve

No Fed officials were reported speaking on Wednesday, a federal holiday. The standing guidance comes from Tuesday, when Governor Waller said he favoured a hike in December and that a half-point move should be considered after the October consumer price index ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/fed-governor-waller-sees-december-hike-likely.html)), and Dallas Fed President Logan backed at least two further quarter-point increases ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/fed-s-waller-backs-december-rate-hike-says-open-to-larger-move)). Futures price 26 basis points for 9 December and a further 17 basis points by January. The bar for a half-point move therefore lies in the data still to come; the market currently treats it as a minority outcome. Separately, the Fed is debating when New York Fed President Williams's term should end ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/retirement-rules-spark-exit-date-debate-for-ny-fed-s-williams)).

## Bank of Japan

Board member Toichiro Asada, who has argued for keeping rates low longer than the majority, said the bank should be cautious about the pace of hikes because higher rates could weigh on the underlying price trend ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/boj-s-dovish-member-calls-for-caution-on-pace-of-rate-increases)). The same morning, corporate goods prices accelerated for the first time in three months, which supports the case for a December hike ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/japan-producer-prices-pick-up-backing-case-for-another-boj-hike)). With USD/JPY at 159.75, a weak yen adds to import prices and raises the cost of waiting.

## Europe

Germany's Council of Economic Experts more than doubled its growth forecast for 2026, citing government spending and robust exports ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/german-economic-advisers-more-than-double-2026-growth-forecast)); Bundesbank President Nagel said the economy is recovering faster than expected a few months ago ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/german-economy-on-the-mend-though-reforms-needed-nagel-says)). Stronger German activity reduces the downside risk to the ECB's inflation outlook. In the United Kingdom the RICS survey showed the weakest housing expectations in three years as mortgage rates weigh on demand ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/b9eb728e-847d-44f8-a2cc-bb139bc7220f)); third-quarter GDP is published at 08:00.

## China

The People's Bank of China reiterated an accommodative stance but noted that loan rates are already low by international standards, which suggests caution about further cuts ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/china-s-loan-interest-rates-are-already-low-central-bank-says), [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/chinas-central-bank-signals-easing-restraint-as-growth-holds-up-15c19e61)).

## Norway

Norges Bank's Financial Stability Report judged the system resilient but the risk of serious events higher, and kept the countercyclical buffer at 2.5 per cent ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/news/Press-releases/2026/2026-11-11-fsr2/)). Statistics Norway publishes its investment survey for the fourth quarter today, with the first estimate of petroleum investment for 2027 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/kalender)).

## US prices

Consumer prices rose 0.4 per cent in October and core prices 0.3 per cent ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)). On the Fed's own measure, PCE inflation was 3.6 per cent in September and core PCE 3.1 per cent, well above the 2 per cent target.

## Assessment

The balance of risks has shifted further towards tighter policy. In the United States, firm core inflation and strong activity ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/51fb0811-982a-4c7b-8d87-f85cd9008f3b)) make a December hike the base case; the main downside risk is a correction in AI-related equities, which could tighten financial conditions on its own. The episode at Nvidia illustrates how concentrated that risk is. Oil at 91 dollars is lower than a week ago, which eases headline inflation at the margin, but the Strait of Hormuz remains a source of upside risk.
