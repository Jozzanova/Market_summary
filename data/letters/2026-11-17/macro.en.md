# Macro, Tuesday 17 November 2026

**There were no policy decisions on Monday, and the central bank communication of the day confirmed the direction rather than changed it: Fed officials discussed the size of the steps rather than whether to raise rates, and the Reserve Bank of Australia's minutes and its governor showed that the board sees the inflation risks as skewed to the upside. The data pointed to weaker growth outside the United States, in China and Japan, while Canadian inflation held at 3.1 per cent. Today's US retail sales for October are the last major US activity figure before the FOMC minutes on Wednesday.**

## Federal Reserve

Cleveland Fed president Beth Hammack said she favours small and steady increases in interest rates, but that the Fed may need to consider larger moves if inflation pressures worsen ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/fed-s-hammack-reiterates-preference-for-small-incremental-hikes)). Chair Kevin Warsh said nothing on the rate path in his remarks on AI and productivity; he said of productivity growth that "in recent quarters, it's been reasonably strong" and that the effects of AI come "with important implications for monetary policy down the road" ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20261116a.htm)). Treasury yields fell slightly as he refrained from giving hints about rates ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/-63dc4bbf)). Vice Chair for Supervision Michael Barr noted that the equity premium is "at low levels relative to historical norms" ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20261116a.htm)).

Fed funds futures price a Fed hike of about 24 basis points on 9 December and a further 19 basis points by 27 January, little changed from Friday. The pricing thus continues to imply about two hikes by January; Governor Waller's suggestion last week that 50 basis points "should be considered" in December if inflation stays high ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/fed-governor-waller-sees-december-hike-likely.html)) is reflected only in a small probability of a larger step.

## Other central banks

- **Reserve Bank of Australia.** The minutes of the 2–3 November meeting show that the hike to 4.85 per cent was decided by six votes to three. The majority pointed to inflation well above target, greater capacity pressures after a higher staff estimate of the NAIRU and the possibility that financial conditions are not restrictive enough; the minority gave more weight to the risk of a sharper downturn, with unemployment rising faster than expected ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/monetary-policy/rba-board-minutes/2026/2026-11-03.html)). Governor Michele Bullock said today that "the risks on inflation are still skewed to the upside" and that "maybe 3 per cent, 3.5 per cent is the new normal for inflation" ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/sp-gov-2026-11-17.html)). Futures had priced no further hike on 8 December.
- **Bank of Japan.** Board member Hajime Takata called for a swift increase in rates to a "good" position ([Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipgFBVV95cUxNQXYydzdHVVhRTmFVV1ZhQ2pQSzN4SDFXTFVMMHpWeTlNWS1OZlNPRFhkVmhVS3kyZktET0pKampiajdMbG9RRUFBMFphTDRvRmJYVC1YY0lyUHhfNFlyQWVFQ1pBTlh2akFSNW5TY3FzZkZkeFdMUzk2NG0tdEV1dDRUaUtjWmhPWHNReTY5Vk1CdnlxNW93RW9ISHlVbnA5NmlGSjhR?oc=5)), while the third-quarter contraction in GDP casts further doubt on a December hike, in the FT's reading ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/5dfa6759-407c-4166-b221-802a5f5b936a)). Former governor Haruhiko Kuroda said persistent inflation risks could lead to a policy rate above neutral ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/former-boj-chief-kuroda-sees-chance-of-rate-rising-above-neutral)).
- **ECB.** No member of the Governing Council spoke on policy on Monday. The last reported market pricing is a probability of about 72 per cent of an ECB hike on 17 December. Spain is pressing for an early decision on the next ECB president and continues to put forward Pablo Hernández de Cos ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/spain-hopes-for-quick-deal-on-de-cos-as-next-ecb-president)).

## Data

- **China.** Activity in October was weaker than expected: industrial production rose 4.8 per cent from a year earlier (poll 5.3 per cent), retail sales only 0.4 per cent (poll 0.8 per cent), and fixed-asset investment fell 8.1 per cent in the first ten months, with property investment down 21.6 per cent ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/chinas-factory-output-retail-sales-020559490.html)). Weaker Chinese demand weighs on metal prices and adds to the downward pressure on oil.
- **Canada.** Consumer prices rose 3.1 per cent from a year earlier in October, unchanged from September, and 0.2 per cent on the month; excluding energy the rate fell to 2.0 per cent ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/261116/dq261116a-eng.htm)). The decline in the measure excluding energy suggests that the underlying pressure is easing, which supports a cautious Bank of Canada on 9 December.
- **United States.** The Empire State survey showed that manufacturing in New York expanded modestly in November, after a strong October ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/new-york-manufacturing-activity-moderates-from-october-surge-4d9db965)). For prices, CPI inflation was 3.7 per cent in September and the Fed's preferred measure, the PCE price index, rose 3.6 per cent, with core PCE at 3.1 per cent. Today's retail sales for October are expected to rise little; RBC expects 0.1 per cent for the headline and 0.3 per cent for the control group ([RBC](https://www.rbc.com/en/economics/us-week-ahead/retail-sales-set-to-hold-up-under-price-pressure/)).
- **Euro area.** Industrial production picked up in September after a weak summer marked by high energy costs ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/eurozone-industrial-production-picked-up-in-september-40e4d56a)).

## Assessment

The balance of risks has shifted slightly over the past week. The inflation outlook in the United States, the euro area, the United Kingdom and Australia is still the main concern of the central banks, and the pricing of the Fed's December hike has not moved. At the same time, the fall in equities concentrated in AI-related shares, the weak data from China and Japan and the decline in oil prices point to weaker growth ahead. Lower oil prices would, if they persist, reduce headline inflation over the coming months and ease the pressure on central banks to tighten beyond what is priced; the 4 basis point fall in the US ten-year breakeven to 2.20 per cent on Monday is consistent with this reading. The FOMC minutes on Wednesday and the Trump–Xi–Putin meeting on Thursday are the next tests.
