# Politics, Tuesday 17 November 2026

**US policy towards China was the political factor with the clearest market effect on Monday: new restrictions on Chinese access to advanced chips through data centres in third countries added to the fall in semiconductor shares, two days before President Trump meets Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen. In the Middle East, the US vice president signalled that Washington does not want a prolonged conflict with Iran, which appears to have lowered the risk premium in oil.**

## United States and China

- **Chip export rules.** The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule requiring a licence for advanced chips shipped to overseas data centres that provide significant computing power to companies headquartered in restricted countries such as China; it also adds licence requirements for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil and takes effect in 90 days ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6222897-bis-limits-remote-computing-exports/amp/)). The market channel is direct: Chinese AI shares and Asian chip producers fell this morning ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/chip-stocks-slide-as-us-sets-new-curbs-on-china-s-compute-access)), and the rule adds to the uncertainty over Nvidia's sales ahead of tonight's results.
- **The Shenzhen meeting.** Trump meets Xi and Putin on Thursday on the sidelines of the APEC summit; Russia aims for a joint statement ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/e567ba7d-c53a-47a2-a361-3b8a107fcdef)). The WSJ reports that Trump's advisers are divided over China policy ahead of the summit ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/trumps-advisers-are-at-war-over-china-ahead-of-xi-summit-90995398)), and China looks unlikely to fulfil its pledge to buy US farm products in 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/china-s-17-billion-crop-pledge-falters-ahead-of-trump-xi-summit)). The outcome matters for tariffs, export controls and, through Russia, for the oil and gas market.
- **China and Canada.** Prime Minister Mark Carney secured further Chinese tariff relief on about 400 million dollars of Canadian exports, including pork and seafood, in return for an extension of Canadian steel and aluminium remissions ([Prime Minister of Canada](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/11/17/prime-minister-carney-advances-canadas-strategic-partnership-china)).
- **Nvidia.** President Trump backed calls for Jensen Huang to be reinstated as chief executive, while the board is looking for a new one ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/trump-backs-calls-for-huang-to-be-reinstated-as-nvidia-ceo)).

## Middle East and energy

Vice President JD Vance said the United States is "not going to allow this to become a 10- or 15-year commitment, or frankly, even a two-year commitment" ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/jd-vance/vance-says-no-daylight-trump-iran-war-rcna609681)). Traders appear to read this as a lower risk of escalation, and Brent fell 3.6 per cent ([Bloomberg](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/oil_retreats_as_traders_weigh_supply_glut-16-nov-2026-185096-article/)), even though Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed a drone attack on a US base in Bahrain on Sunday and mine hits on two tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, which Reuters could not verify ([Reuters](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/15/bahrain-iran-drone-attacks-tehran-us-base.html)).

## Europe

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said in his City of London speech that "the hard Brexit we were given is not good enough for Britain" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/17/uk-seeks-closer-ties-with-eu-after-brexit-fail)), but that any major change in the relationship would "take time" and would not be implemented before the next election ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/new-uk-relationship-with-europe-will-take-time-burnham-says)). The EU–UK summit is in Brussels on Friday. The limited near-term scope suggests little effect on sterling for now.

## Norway

The budget negotiations between Labour and the Centre Party, the Socialist Left, Red and the Greens continue towards Labour's deadline at 23:00 on Friday, with the possibility of extra time until Monday 23 November ([NTB](https://www.adressa.no/nyheter/innenriks/i/w3ydVn/finanspolitikere-har-med-sovepose-klar-for-lange-budsjettforhandlinger)). The main disputes are fuel taxes, the road-use tax, the fixed electricity price (norgespris) and taxes on the lowest incomes ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/nyheter/statsbudsjettet-2027-1.18034953)); no report on the state of the talks on Monday was found. The Greens will raise the Supreme Court ruling on Breidablikk, Tyrving and Yggdrasil in the talks and demand that production stop ([NTB](https://www.adressa.no/nyheter/innenriks/i/z0GgVw/mdg-krever-full-oljestans-etter-klimadom)), while Energy Minister Terje Aasland says oil policy is unchanged ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/M5gz2r/klimasoeksmaalet-tillatelser-maa-behandles-paa-nytt)). The market channel is fiscal: an agreement that raises spending or lowers energy taxes would add to demand and, in Norges Bank's framework, to the inflation outlook.
