# Norges Bank, Tuesday 17 November 2026

**The trade surplus in October was the largest since 2022, driven by gas exports at high prices, but the figures say little about the rate path. No economist published a new view on Norges Bank on Monday; those quoted since the October CPI expect a hold on 17 December. The krone weakened this morning as oil and equities fell.**

## New data

**External trade.** The trade surplus rose to 121.4 billion kroner in October, 17.4 per cent more than in September and the highest since 2022 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/utenriksokonomi/utenrikshandel/statistikk/utenrikshandel-med-varer/artikler/hoyeste-handelsoverskudd-siden-2022)). Gas exports rose 32.7 per cent to 105.3 billion kroner, at an average price of nearly 10 kroner per standard cubic metre, twice the average of the past three years, while crude oil exports fell 7.0 per cent, as volumes were the lowest since March 2025. Mainland exports rose 1.2 per cent on the month but were 3.5 per cent lower than a year earlier ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/utenriksokonomi/utenrikshandel/statistikk/utenrikshandel-med-varer)). Imports reached a nominal monthly record, lifted by data-centre equipment and about 25,000 cars bought ahead of the VAT change on 1 January. The car purchases bring household demand forward, which will weigh on goods consumption early next year.

**Electricity prices.** Households paid 201.6 øre per kWh including grid rent and taxes in the third quarter, 44.7 per cent more than a year earlier; after government support, the price was 6.7 per cent lower than a year earlier ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/energi-og-industri/energi/statistikk/elektrisitetspriser)). The support thus continues to dampen the effect of high energy prices on CPI.

## What others say

- **No new comments.** No bank economist commented on Norges Bank on Monday that could be found.
- **Standing views.** After the October CPI (3.6 per cent, against Norges Bank's 3.8 per cent), Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge said "Jeg forventer at Norges Bank holder renten uendret i desember", and Harald Magnus Andreassen of SpareBank 1 Markets that inflation is now on the track Norges Bank assumed ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)). Marius Gonsholt Hov of Handelsbanken said a hike before Christmas would require "en ganske kraftig oppside" ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)). Frank Jullum of Danske Bank believes the peak has been reached, while Allan Monks of JP Morgan expects a further hike in the first quarter ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/eG3QBa/tror-rentetoppen-er-naadd-balanserer-paa-en-knivsegg)).
- **Market pricing.** The last reported figure is still about 35 per cent for a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November); Handelsbanken's later note says money markets price "little chance" of a hike ([Pound Sterling Live](https://www.poundsterlinglive.com/news/23869-pound-to-krone-handelsbanken-krone-rally-over)). No newer figure was found.

Apart from JP Morgan, none of the economists quoted expects a further hike. Thursday's Expectations Survey is the next test of whether inflation expectations support that view.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.617 this morning, 0.7 per cent above Monday's close, and USD/NOK 9.646; I-44 was 109.55 at Monday's fix, 0.2 per cent weaker than on Friday.

**Why.** The weakening coincides with a fall of 3.6 per cent in the oil price and a broad fall in equities. Lower oil prices worsen the terms of trade, and equity falls tend to lead Norwegian life and pension insurers to adjust their currency hedges, as Kjetil Olsen of Nordea has pointed out ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/Pg6kg0/kronen-svekkes-etter-global-boerssmell)). No source attributes this morning's move specifically.

**Differentials.** Against the euro area: 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, 1.82 percentage points at twelve months in government rates (the stand-in for the swap differential) and 0.95 percentage points at ten years. Against the United States the 12-month differential is only 0.13 percentage points.
