# Markets, Tuesday 17 November 2026

**The sell-off in AI and semiconductor shares broadened on Monday and took the broad US indices down by about 2 per cent, as new US restrictions on Chinese access to advanced chips added to the uncertainty over Nvidia ahead of tonight's results. Unlike last week, rates did not add to the pressure: Treasury yields edged lower and oil fell markedly on the prospect of a supply surplus, while the dollar held near its highest level since April 2025.**

## Equities

**United States.** The S&P 500 fell 1.92 per cent, the Nasdaq Composite 2.49 per cent, its largest one-day fall since 5 November, and the Dow 1.41 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The AI and semiconductor proxies again fell markedly more than the market: SOX fell 5.28 per cent, NYSE FANG+ 3.33 per cent, the AI ETF AIQ 3.22 per cent and the Magnificent Seven ETF 2.50 per cent. Nvidia fell 4.1 per cent ahead of its results tonight, the first under interim chief executive Colette Kress; Micron fell 7.6 per cent, Intel 7.1 per cent, AMD 5.6 per cent and Broadcom 3.9 per cent ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/xJPJWn/markant-tek-fall-paa-wall-street-nvidia-fortsetter-nedgangen)). The Commerce Department's new licensing requirement for advanced AI chips shipped to data centres in Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand appears to have added to the pressure on the sector. Only consumer staples (+0.27 per cent) and utilities (+0.18 per cent) rose, and VIX rose 3.6 points to 22.9. Ben Emons of FedWatch Advisors wrote that "the tape feels increasingly fragile as Nvidia's AI premium continues to unwind" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).

**Europe and Norway.** STOXX Europe 600 fell 0.28 per cent, ending a run of five gains that had taken it to record levels; technology and basic resources (−2.5 per cent, as metal prices fell) weighed, while health care rose 0.8 per cent and Novo Nordisk 2.4 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/european-stocks-ease-as-tech-and-miners-drag-ce785edfdd89ff2c)). The DAX fell 0.57 per cent and the FTSE 100 0.19 per cent, where Anglo American fell 4.2 per cent ([DPA](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/world-indices/articles/european-stocks-close-weak-cautious-190339591.html)). Oslo Børs fell 0.41 per cent, turning lower late in the session as the oil price fell: Equinor fell 2.94 per cent and Aker BP 0.88 per cent, while DNB rose 0.95 per cent and Polight 12.2 per cent on its first mass-production order ([E24](https://e24.no/bors/nyheter/a/306258)).

**Asia this morning.** The chip sell-off continued after the US restrictions: KOSPI is down 1.8 per cent, the Nikkei 1.0 per cent and the Hang Seng 0.8 per cent, while the Shanghai Composite is unchanged; Taiwan fell 1.4 per cent early in the session and the Hang Seng Tech index about 2 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449311/asia-shares-slide-on-tech-weakness-ahead-of-nvidia-earnings)). US equity futures are a little higher, by 0.1–0.35 per cent, while European futures point about 0.6 per cent lower.

## Fixed income and credit

Treasury yields fell slightly as investors moved out of equities: the 10-year fell 3 basis points to 5.12 per cent and the 5-year 5 basis points to 4.93 per cent, after a rise of 26 and 28 basis points respectively last week. Fed funds futures still price a Fed hike of about 24 basis points on 9 December and a further 19 basis points by 27 January, little changed from Friday; Reuters reports that markets treat the December hike as a certainty, with a slim chance of 50 basis points ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449311/asia-shares-slide-on-tech-weakness-ahead-of-nvidia-earnings)). The US 10-year breakeven fell 4 basis points to 2.20 per cent, in line with the lower oil price. The German 10-year yield rose 2 basis points to 3.45 per cent and the 2-year 3 basis points to 3.16 per cent, reflecting continued expectations of ECB tightening. The Japanese 10-year yield rose 3 basis points to 2.93 per cent.

## Commodities and currencies

**Energy.** Brent fell 3.6 per cent on Monday to 92.44 dollars and is 91.74 dollars this morning. The IEA's estimate that supply now exceeds demand, high Saudi output and lower Gulf selling prices outweighed the tensions with Iran; Vice President JD Vance said the United States would not let the conflict become "a two-year commitment", which traders appear to read as a lower risk of escalation ([Bloomberg](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/oil_retreats_as_traders_weigh_supply_glut-16-nov-2026-185096-article/)). TTF gas fell 6.8 per cent to 84.75 euros; no source found attributes the move.

**Metals.** Gold is 3,877 dollars, 0.6 per cent higher this morning, after a fall of 3.8 per cent over the past week as real yields and the dollar rose. Aluminium fell 2.2 per cent, and copper is at its lowest in more than four months ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449311/asia-shares-slide-on-tech-weakness-ahead-of-nvidia-earnings)).

**Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.52, after touching 103.69 overnight, the highest since April 2025; EUR/USD is 1.1008, after a low of 1.0985, and USD/JPY 159.15, close to the 160 level at which traders watch for Japanese intervention ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449311/asia-shares-slide-on-tech-weakness-ahead-of-nvidia-earnings)). The New Zealand dollar is at its lowest since 2020 as the interest rate gap widens ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/new-zealand-dollar-drops-to-six-year-low-against-greenback)). EUR/NOK is 10.617 this morning, 0.7 per cent above Monday's close, and USD/NOK 9.646; I-44 (a rise is a weaker krone) was 109.55 at Monday's fix, 0.2 per cent weaker than on Friday. The weakening coincides with the fall in oil prices and in global equities; Nordea has earlier pointed out that equity falls lead life and pension insurers to adjust their currency hedges, which tends to weaken the krone ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/Pg6kg0/kronen-svekkes-etter-global-boerssmell)). The 12-month differential in government rates against the euro area is 1.82 percentage points and against the United States only 0.13 percentage points.

## Today

US retail sales for October follow at 14:30; RBC expects 0.1 per cent for the headline and 0.3 per cent for the control group ([RBC](https://www.rbc.com/en/economics/us-week-ahead/retail-sales-set-to-hold-up-under-price-pressure/)). Nvidia reports after the US close, with consensus revenue of 109.1 billion dollars and earnings per share of 2.47 dollars (LSEG) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-earnings-jensen-huang-resignation.html)); UBS cites an implied share-price move of about 7.8 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449311/asia-shares-slide-on-tech-weakness-ahead-of-nvidia-earnings)).
