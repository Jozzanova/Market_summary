# Politics, Tuesday 24 November 2026

**Politics moved markets on Monday through two channels: the US administration's intervention at Nvidia, which removed the most immediate uncertainty over the largest company in the index but opened a new round of congressional scrutiny, and Washington's signal that it will give diplomacy with Iran more time, which lowered the risk premium in oil despite weekend attacks in the Gulf. In Norway, the budget talks remain unresolved, and the fall in the oil fund's value has narrowed the fiscal room.**

## United States

- **Nvidia.** The board reinstated Jensen Huang after concluding, "based on input from senior levels of the United States government", that his return "poses no national security risk"; it set up a national security and export control committee, and its own investigation into reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort will continue ([Nvidia](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm)). President Trump said he had made the deal and that it "was a lot easier to convince them yesterday than it was a week and a half ago" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/trump-nvidia-jensen-huang.html)). Lawmakers of both parties questioned the decision; Huang is due to be interviewed behind closed doors by three House committees on 1 December ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/congress-criticism-jensen-huang-nvidia-reinstated-ceo-trump-rcna610926)). The Commerce Department added the Singapore company Megaspeed, under investigation for possibly diverting Nvidia chips to China, to its Entity List, and Singapore's Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said the city-state will not be "used and abused as a conduit" to circumvent US export controls ([The Edge Singapore](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/news/singapore-news/singapore-wont-be-conduit-skirt-us-chip-curbs-pm-says)). *Market channel:* governance and regulatory risk for the AI sector is reduced but not removed.
- **Vance and Venezuelan oil.** The incoming chair of the Senate Homeland Security committee, Maggie Hassan, has asked Vice President Vance for his communications with a Vitol trader over a Venezuelan oil contract; Vance's spokesman said the alleged meeting "never happened" ([Reuters](https://kelo.com/2026/11/23/exclusive-jd-vances-ties-to-prized-venezuelan-oil-contract-under-investigation-by-us-senate-panel/)).
- **Funding.** Federal funding expires on 11 December; appropriators expect another short-term extension into the new year ([Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/politics/2026/11/22/will-there-be-a-government-shutdown/)).

## Middle East and energy

- **Iran.** Iran attacked US facilities in Bahrain over the weekend, and two tankers were struck near Oman ([CNBC](https://cnbc.com/2026/11/22/saudi-houthi-bahrain-iran-attacks-araghchi-talks.html)); Trump said the Islamabad memorandum was null and void, while Iran's foreign minister said the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within two hours once the United States meets its commitments ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/23/iran-vows-every-means-to-counter-us-blockade-of-hormuz-after-trump-threat)). On Monday Trump told Fox News he may resume the war next year if talks fail ([Bloomberg](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/oil_falls_as_trump_signals_iran_talks-23-nov-2026-185152-article/)). US officials suspect Iran of cyberattacks on as many as 50 ships carrying oil and other hazardous cargo ([Reuters](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/us-eyes-iran-possible-culprit-001800486.html)). *Market channel:* the market read the absence of escalation as a reason to reduce hedges against a supply disruption, and Brent fell about 3.6 per cent.
- **Russia and Ukraine.** Ukrainian drones struck the Novoshakhtinsk refinery and a Transneft pumping station in Tatarstan overnight to Monday ([Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/11/23/ukrainian-drones-strike-two-russian-oil-refineries-and-pumping-station-in-overnight-attacks)). *Market channel:* lower Russian refining capacity supports diesel margins, though diesel prices eased on Monday.
- **Cobalt.** The Democratic Republic of Congo will suspend all cobalt exports from 30 November until further notice to address oversupply, with a review after four months ([Reuters](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/2026/congo-halts-cobalt-exports-again-to-tackle-oversupply-support-prices-industry-regulator-says)).

## Asia and emerging markets

- **China.** The securities regulator lowered the minimum margin ratio for buying shares on credit from 100 to 80 per cent from 30 November, which allows investors to borrow more against their holdings ([Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261122/e9adb9d894474d1cbbea6ae00e78b5b5/c.html)).
- **South Korea.** Seoul scrambled fighter jets after Russian military aircraft entered its air defence identification zone, the second incident in a week ([AP](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/world/2026/11/23/south-korea-says-several-russian-aircraft-flew-into-its-air-defense-zone/)); KOSPI is nevertheless up 7 per cent this morning.
- **Brazil.** The Chamber of Deputies approved President Lula's fiscal adjustment early on Tuesday, after the real touched 6 per dollar on Monday, its weakest since April 2025 ([AFP](https://unitel.bo/noticias/agencias/congreso-de-brasil-aprueba-ajuste-fiscal-de-lula-en-medio-de-presion-sobre-el-real-IF25020686)).

## Europe

- **Spain.** The general election is on Sunday; Bloomberg describes housing costs as a central issue for voters despite strong growth ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-24/spain-election-2026-angry-generation-feels-left-behind-as-economy-booms)). *Market channel:* the fiscal outlook and the Spanish spread.

## Norway

- **Budget.** Talks between Labour and Sp, SV, Rødt and MDG went into their fourth day of overtime on Monday without agreement and continue today; the finance committee's recommendation is due on 30 November ([NTB](https://radioh.no/ny-runde-med-budsjettforhandlinger-tirsdag/)).
- **The oil fund and the fiscal rule.** The fund was about 21,635 billion kroner on Monday evening after losing up to 1,800 billion in a week; at that value the proposed oil revenue spending of 608.4 billion kroner is 2.81 per cent of the fund, and the 3 per cent guideline would be exceeded if the fund fell below 20,280 billion before the new year ([NRK](https://nrk.no/artikkel/oljefondet-har-krympet-over-1000-milliarder-blast-bort-1533745)). *Market channel:* a lasting fall in the fund would constrain the fiscal stance in future budgets, which matters for demand, the rate outlook and the krone.
