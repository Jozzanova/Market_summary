# Norges Bank, Tuesday 24 November 2026

**No new Norwegian key figures bearing on the policy rate were published on Monday, and no new comments by economists on the 17 December decision were found; the standing view among those quoted is a hold. The day's Norwegian discussion concerned the fall in the oil fund and what it means for fiscal room. The krone weakened further as oil fell, even though global risk appetite improved markedly.**

## New data

No new key figures. Statistics Norway publishes the credit indicator for October at 08:00 today; in September, twelve-month growth in household credit was 4.5 per cent, down 0.1 percentage point from August ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/bank-og-finansmarked/finansielle-indikatorer/statistikk/kredittindikator)). The labour force survey follows on Wednesday and the national accounts for the third quarter on Thursday; no consensus figures have been reported.

## What others say

- **On fiscal policy and the oil fund.** Harald Magnus Andreassen, chief economist at SpareBank 1 Markets, said that "vi sitter jo på en liten vulkan" when the fund's return finances a quarter of the state budget, and that a lasting fall "vil ha noe å si for hvordan dette ser ut når neste budsjett skal lages". Kjetil Olsen, chief economist at Nordea Markets, does not expect the current budget talks to be affected, but said "jeg tror tiden for ekstravaganse er litt over" ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/j3dEmL/sitter-paa-en-liten-vulkan)). According to NRK, the fund was worth about 21,635 billion kroner on Monday evening, so that the proposed spending of oil revenue corresponds to 2.81 per cent of the fund ([NRK](https://nrk.no/artikkel/oljefondet-har-krympet-over-1000-milliarder-blast-bort-1533745)). For monetary policy, the relevance is that a tighter fiscal stance in later years would dampen demand and, other things equal, reduce the need for a high policy rate.
- **On the rate path.** No economist published a new view. The views on file are unchanged: Handelsbanken's forecast is that the policy rate peaked at 4.50 per cent, with a final hike not ruled out ([Estate Nyheter](https://www.estatenyheter.no/inflasjon-norges-bank-obligasjonsrenter/norges-bank-forberedt-pa-a-sette-renten-opp/522335)), SpareBank 1 Markets and SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expect a hold ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)), Danske Bank believes the peak has been reached, and JP Morgan expects a further hike in the first quarter ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/eG3QBa/tror-rentetoppen-er-naadd-balanserer-paa-en-knivsegg)). None of the economists quoted expects a hike in December.
- **Market pricing.** No new figure was found; the last reported reading is about a 35 per cent probability of a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.720 this morning and USD/NOK 9.826, up 0.21 and 0.78 per cent; I-44 rose 0.22 per cent at Monday's fix to 110.98 (a rise is a weaker krone). The krone has weakened about 1 per cent against the euro over the past week.

**Why.** Monday's weakening went against the usual pattern: global equities rose markedly, which normally supports the krone as currency-hedged Norwegian investors buy back kroner. The likely explanation is oil: Brent fell about 3.6 per cent, which lowers the value of Norway's exports and the expected flow of petroleum revenue, and the dollar strengthened as the market priced a Fed hike in December more fully. No source attributes the move explicitly.

**Differentials.** The policy rate differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points. The 12-month differential in government rates was 1.84 percentage points at Friday's fix, about 6 basis points wider over the day. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 5 basis points on Monday to 4.23 per cent, more than the German 10-year, so the long differential against Germany narrowed to about 0.86 percentage point. The short differentials have not moved against the krone, which supports the reading that oil, not rates, drove the weakening.
