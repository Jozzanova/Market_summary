# Macro, Tuesday 24 November 2026

**The November flash surveys showed activity still growing in the United States and the euro area but at a slower pace, with easing input costs, while officials at the Federal Reserve, the ECB and the Bank of England argued that inflation still calls for tighter policy. As equities recovered and the demand for safe assets eased, the market moved to price a Fed hike on 9 December as close to certain; lower oil prices helped long-dated yields elsewhere, notably in Japan this morning.**

## Federal Reserve

- **Pricing of a December hike is now complete.** Fed funds futures imply an increase of 25.6 basis points on 9 December, a fully priced quarter-point Fed hike, and a further 16 basis points by 27 January; on Monday morning the December figure was 22.5 basis points. CME FedWatch showed about a 98 per cent probability of a Fed hike in December, up from about 88 per cent on Friday ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/treasury-yields-bonds-fed.html)). The repricing reflects the reversal of last week's demand for safe assets as equities recovered, and the tone of Fed speakers, rather than new inflation data.
- **Williams.** New York Fed President John Williams said that inflation is "currently about 3-1/2 percent" and that policy must ensure that shocks "do not lead to broader or persistent inflationary dynamics" ([New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/wil261123)). He told reporters that he "would not exclude... moving at consecutive meetings at all if that was what was needed" ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/fed-lower-inflation-williams-says-210546185.html)). As vice chair of the FOMC, his view is usually read as close to the leadership's, which is likely to have supported the pricing beyond December.
- **Bowman.** The Fed's Inspector General is examining whether Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman breached the blackout rules by speaking at an invitation-only Bank of America dinner hours after the June meeting ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/2c1fcdc0-eb16-4cee-9041-736527c2ae83)). No market effect could be identified.
- **Inflation expectations.** The US 10-year breakeven inflation rate fell 2 basis points to 2.13 per cent and the 5-year inflation expectation five years ahead 1 basis point to 2.11 per cent, as oil fell. Long-run expectations thus remain anchored close to the target, which gives the Fed room to treat a hike as insurance rather than the start of a long cycle.

## Other central banks

- **ECB.** Governing Council member Gediminas Šimkus said he is "not convinced that a 25-basis-point rate hike in December will be sufficient to fully control inflation" and sees "early warning signs" of second-round effects, but no "major pressure to act quickly again after December" ([Reuters](https://1027wbow.com/2026/11/23/december-rate-hike-may-not-be-enough-but-not-in-a-rush-to-move-again-ecbs-simkus/)). Reuters describes an ECB hike on 17 December as all but certain, with one or two more priced for 2027. On Wednesday the Governing Council discusses raising banks' minimum reserve requirement from 1 to 2 per cent, which would reduce the interest the Eurosystem pays on reserves; a decision may be deferred to December.
- **Bank of England.** Chief economist Huw Pill said in Vienna that "we do need to take action now" to keep inflation on course for target, noting that inflation has been below target in "only three of the last 62 months" ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/boes-pill-urges-action-maintain-price-stability/)).
- **Bank of Japan.** Japanese government bond yields fell after a three-day holiday, the 10-year by about 7 basis points, as lower oil prices eased inflation concerns ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/japanese-bonds-rally-across-curve-as-domestic-stocks-surge)). A Reuters poll has most economists expecting a hike to 1.50 per cent on 18 December.
- **Reserve Bank of Australia.** Australian bonds are on course for their sixth daily gain, with the 10-year yield at its lowest since July, as the rise in unemployment to 4.9 per cent has led money markets to price an RBA cut by June 2027 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/australia-bonds-set-for-longest-rally-since-june-on-rba-cut-bets)). Australia is thus the first of the economies that tightened this autumn where the market expects the next move to be down.
- **Bank of Canada.** Governor Tiff Macklem speaks to the Economic Club of Canada in Toronto today on the outlook and the challenges for policy, ahead of the decision on 9 December.

## Data

{bars: Flash PMI, November, composite index | US 54.6; Euro area 51.7; UK 52.3 | | S&P Global}

- **Euro area: steady growth, an unusually wide gap between Germany and France.** The composite PMI was unchanged at 51.7, below the 51.9 expected; services fell to a five-month low of 51.2 while manufacturing rose to 52.9. German output grew at its fastest pace since March 2022, while France saw its sharpest contraction in six months ([Reuters](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1123/1600272-euro-zone-growth-pmi/)). Input-cost inflation eased to a three-month low and output prices rose at the slowest pace since August; this tempers, but does not remove, the case for the December hike, since headline inflation was 4.0 per cent in October.
- **United States: slower but still solid.** The composite PMI fell to 54.6 from 55.9, a four-month low, with services at 54.7 and manufacturing at 54.5; S&P Global's Chris Williamson said the surveys are consistent with annualised GDP growth of about 3 per cent in the fourth quarter, and that price gauges remain consistent with inflation well above 2 per cent ([Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/us-business-growth-cooled-in-november-but-stayed-in-expansion)).
- **United Kingdom.** The composite PMI fell to 52.3 from 52.9, with services at 52.0.
- **Today and this week.** The Conference Board's consumer confidence index is due at 16:00 Oslo time; most responses were collected before last week's steepest equity declines. On Wednesday come the second estimate of third-quarter GDP and October personal income and outlays, for which the Dow Jones consensus is a monthly rise of 0.3 per cent in the PCE price index after 0.5 per cent in September ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/treasury-yields-bonds-fed.html)), and the Beige Book.

## Assessment

Monday did not change the picture of the cycle, but it changed the balance of considerations. Last week's equity fall had raised the possibility that tighter financial conditions would do part of the Fed's work; with half of that loss recovered in a day and Williams open to consecutive moves, the market has returned to pricing a hike in December and a further one by January. The fall in oil works the other way: lower energy prices reduce headline inflation in the coming months and have already lowered long-run inflation expectations somewhat. The PMIs point to slowing but positive growth on both sides of the Atlantic with easing cost pressure, which is consistent with a short tightening cycle rather than a long one. Wednesday's PCE figures remain the main test before the Fed's blackout begins on Saturday.
