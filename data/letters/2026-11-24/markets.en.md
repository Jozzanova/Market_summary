# Markets, Tuesday 24 November 2026

**Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as chief executive turned last week's sale of AI and semiconductor shares into the largest one-day recovery in US equities since April 2025, and the recovery has spread to Asia this morning. As demand for safe assets eased, short-dated Treasury yields rose and the market moved to price a Fed hike on 9 December fully, while long-dated yields changed little as oil fell on signs that Washington will give diplomacy with Iran more time. The krone weakened as oil fell.**

## Equities

- **United States: half of last week's loss recovered in a day.** The S&P 500 rose 4.73 per cent, the Nasdaq Composite 6.51 per cent and the Dow 3.01 per cent, the best day since April 2025, after seven consecutive declines; the S&P 500 had fallen 3.70 per cent on Friday alone ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/stocks-rebound-ai-tech-selloff-nvidia-rcna610833)). The VIX fell 9.3 points to 26.6. Every sector except energy and consumer staples rose: technology rose 8.97 per cent, while energy fell 1.06 per cent as oil fell.
- **AI and semiconductors led by a wide margin.** Nvidia rose 31.5 per cent and added about 950 billion dollars in market value, the largest one-day gain for any company, after its board unanimously reinstated Huang and stated, on the basis of input from the US government, that his return "poses no national security risk" ([Nvidia](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm)). The semiconductor index SOX rose 14.03 per cent, NYSE FANG+ 7.42 per cent and the Magnificent Seven ETF 7.62 per cent, against 4.73 per cent for the S&P 500; Micron rose 19 per cent, Dell 20 per cent and Arm 16 per cent, while Anthropic fell 1.4 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-nvidia-shares.html)). The move is largely a reversal: SOX is still 11 per cent lower over the month, and Nvidia is still about 30 per cent below its October record. CrowdStrike fell about 10 per cent after agreeing to buy Zscaler for 43.9 billion dollars ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-monday-november-23-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-surge-after-nvidia-reinstates-jensen-huang-182039397.html)).
- **Europe and Norway rose less.** STOXX Europe 600 rose 1.81 per cent, ending five days of declines, the DAX 2.15 per cent and the FTSE 100 1.30 per cent; STMicroelectronics rose nearly 8 per cent and Infineon about 7 per cent ([RTTNews](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/world-indices/articles/european-markets-close-strong-nvidia-191824403.html)). Oslo Børs rose 0.50 per cent. Nordic Semiconductor rose 7.5 per cent on the Nvidia news, while Vår Energi fell 3.3 per cent after agreeing to buy ConocoPhillips' Norwegian business, including the operatorship of Greater Ekofisk, for an enterprise value of 4.4 billion dollars ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/z0Gj1r/vaar-energi-kjoeper-conocophillips-norske-virksomhet-i-avtale-verdt-43-milliarder-kroner)); analysts called the deal strategically sound but pointed to the possibility of a share issue to finance it ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/oljegigant-spretter-opp-etter-milliardnyhet/s/80-95-272900)). The smaller weight of technology and the fall in oil prices explain most of the gap to the United States.
- **Asia this morning: chip makers at the daily limit in Taipei.** KOSPI is up 7.00 per cent and the Nikkei 225 3.97 per cent after Monday's holiday; Taiwan's index is up more than 9 per cent, with TSMC close to the 10 per cent daily limit, SK hynix up 8.6 per cent and Tokyo Electron 10.5 per cent ([AP](https://www.wral.com/news/ap/feec0-asian-stocks-rally-after-wall-street-rebound-as-jensen-huang-returns-as-nvidia-s-ceo/)). The Hang Seng is up 0.16 per cent and the Shanghai Composite 1.59 per cent, where AI hardware carries less weight. US equity futures are about 0.2 per cent higher ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).

## Fixed income and credit

- **Short yields up, long yields little changed.** The 2-year Treasury yield rose more than 7 basis points to 4.69 per cent and the 5-year 2.7 basis points to 4.73 per cent, while the 10-year changed little at 4.92 per cent and the 30-year edged down to 5.29 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/treasury-yields-bonds-fed.html)). Investors who had bought Treasuries as a haven last week sold them as equities recovered, and that weighed most on short maturities, which carry the expected policy path; lower oil prices supported the long end by reducing expected inflation. CME FedWatch shows about a 98 per cent probability of a Fed hike on 9 December, up from about 88 per cent on Friday. Treasuries gained in Asian trading as oil fell further ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/treasuries-advance-as-cheaper-oil-alleviates-inflation-concerns)).
- **Europe and Norway.** The German 2-year yield was unchanged at 3.08 per cent and the 10-year fell 2 basis points to 3.37 per cent. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 5 basis points to 4.23 per cent and the 5-year 4.5 basis points to 4.35 per cent. Japanese government bond yields fell after the holiday, the 10-year by about 7 basis points ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/japanese-bonds-rally-across-curve-as-domestic-stocks-surge)).

## Commodities and currencies

- **Oil fell markedly.** Brent fell about 3.6 per cent on Monday and is 86.18 dollars this morning, about a fifth lower this month ([AP](https://www.wral.com/news/ap/feec0-asian-stocks-rally-after-wall-street-rebound-as-jensen-huang-returns-as-nvidia-s-ceo/)). President Trump told Fox News he may resume the war with Iran next year if talks fail, which the market read as more time for diplomacy; traders who had bought oil to hedge against an escalation over the weekend sold those positions when none came, and the IEA expects a supply surplus of about 2 million barrels a day this quarter ([Bloomberg](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/oil_falls_as_trump_signals_iran_talks-23-nov-2026-185152-article/)). TTF gas rose 1.45 per cent to 89.74 euros on colder forecasts.
- **Metals.** Gold rebounded on Monday as risk appetite returned ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/gold-holds-gain-after-nvidia-s-rehiring-of-huang-sparks-rally)) and is 3,633 dollars this morning, 0.5 per cent lower. Copper fell 2.22 per cent and aluminium 4.57 per cent as investors remained cautious about demand ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/copper-resumes-decline-as-investors-look-beyond-drama-at-nvidia)). Shares in Chinese cobalt producers rose after the Democratic Republic of Congo said it will suspend cobalt exports from 30 November ([Reuters](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/2026/congo-halts-cobalt-exports-again-to-tackle-oversupply-support-prices-industry-regulator-says)).
- **Currencies.** EUR/USD fell 0.54 per cent to 1.0913 and the dollar index is 104.28, as higher US short yields supported the dollar; USD/JPY is 158.99. The krone weakened: EUR/NOK rose 0.21 per cent to 10.720 and USD/NOK 0.78 per cent to 9.826, and I-44 rose 0.22 per cent to 110.98 at Monday's fix (a rise is a weaker krone). The fall in oil worsens Norway's terms of trade and appears to have outweighed the support from higher risk appetite.
