# Markets letter, Monday 9 November 2026

**Summary.** Asian equities recovered part of last week's losses this morning, led by the semiconductor-heavy markets: the Nikkei 225 rose 3.2 per cent and the KOSPI 1.0 per cent, and US equity futures pointed about 0.5 per cent higher. The rebound came despite news that Nvidia's board is investigating chief executive Jensen Huang after reports he was the target of Chinese intelligence. Oil was steady near 98 dollars after President Trump said renewed strikes on Iran were "highly unlikely" if talks progress, while gold rose 1.4 per cent. Europe and the United States open after the weekend; the week's main data are consumer prices in Norway and the United States on Tuesday.

## Equities

- **United States.** Prices on the page are Friday's closes: the S&P 500 at 7,377 and the Nasdaq Composite at 24,579, down 2.7 and 3.4 per cent over the week. Futures on the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 traded about 0.5 per cent higher in Asian hours ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/investinglive-asia-pacific-market-news-asia-shrugs-off-nvidia-scandal-oil-steadies/)). The AI complex remains the weak spot: the semiconductor index (SOX) is 12.2 per cent lower over the week and 28 per cent over the month despite Friday's 4.2 per cent rebound; NYSE FANG+ is down 4.3 per cent over the week and the AI ETFs between 3.6 and 5.6 per cent, against 2.7 per cent for the S&P 500. Nvidia shares trade for the first time on the investigation today; Huang has admitted to an affair with an executive US officials reportedly believe was recruited by Chinese intelligence, and remains chief executive ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nvidias-board-to-investigate-reports-that-jensen-huang-was-spying-target-c630325e)).
- **Europe.** The STOXX Europe 600 fell 1.0 per cent on Friday and the DAX 1.9 per cent, but both were roughly unchanged over the week.
- **Norway.** The benchmark index closed Friday at 2,053, 4.3 per cent lower over the week but 21.7 per cent higher this year. Nordea's Joachim Bernhardsen says the question for this week is whether the AI sell-off reverses ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/qOnMOg/venter-paa-signaler-kan-snu-opp-ned-paa-hele-regnestykket)).
- **Asia.** The Nikkei rose 3.2 per cent to 59,276, recovering about half of Friday's fall; SK Hynix gained about 4.6 per cent and TSMC about 5.6 per cent, while SoftBank fell ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/investinglive-asia-pacific-market-news-asia-shrugs-off-nvidia-scandal-oil-steadies/)). Many indices had entered bear-market territory last week ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/b8644327-86e7-4d1c-a71f-3fb1d552723b)). The Hang Seng rose 0.2 per cent and Shanghai 0.5 per cent. Foreign funds bought close to 5 billion dollars of Indian equities last week as money rotated out of the AI trade ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-09/foreign-funds-pile-into-india-as-ai-trade-falters)).

## Fixed income and credit

- The US 10-year yield ended Friday at 4.86 per cent and the 5-year at 4.65 per cent; the 10-year note future eased 0.1 per cent in Asian trade. Markets price about an 80 per cent probability of a Federal Reserve hike in December ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/investinglive-asia-pacific-market-news-asia-shrugs-off-nvidia-scandal-oil-steadies/)).
- At Thursday's fixes the euro-area AAA 10-year yield was 3.29 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year 4.31 per cent. US high-yield spreads stood at 348 basis points, 22 basis points wider over the week.

## Commodities and currencies

- **Oil.** Brent dipped to about 96.7 dollars before recovering to 98.3 dollars, little changed. Trump told CNN that renewed strikes were "highly unlikely" if talks show progress and meets Qatar's prime minister today ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/08/politics/trump-interview-on-iran)); Iran's navy said a tanker hit mines east of the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday and that the strait stays closed ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/irans-army-says-violating-oil-tanker-hit-mines-in-hormuz-strait)). Brent is 16.4 per cent lower than a week ago.
- **Metals.** Gold rose 1.4 per cent to 4,133 dollars, silver 2.6 per cent and copper 2.1 per cent. China extended its suspension of a 24 per cent tariff on US goods to January ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/china-extends-pause-on-24-us-tariffs-to-january-keeps-10-rate)).
- **FX.** The dollar index is at 102.1 and USD/JPY at 158.2. Japan confirmed it intervened on three days over the summer ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/japan-says-three-day-yen-intervention-lifted-yen-in-summer)).
- **The krone.** EUR/NOK is at 10.54 and USD/NOK at 9.44 this morning, close to Friday's closing levels. The brief's one-day changes of 0.9 and 1.5 per cent compare with an earlier Friday fixing and mainly reflect the weakening during Friday's equity sell-off. Over the week EUR/NOK is 2.5 per cent higher, and the I-44 index stood at 108.79 on Friday, 2.0 per cent weaker over the week but 9.2 per cent stronger this year.
