# Macro letter, Sunday 8 November 2026

**Summary.** No central bank changed policy over the weekend. ECB President Christine Lagarde said in an interview published on Saturday that inflation is likely to remain high in the near term, driven by energy, and declined to commit to a December move. In the United States, Friday's strong employment report has left markets pricing a December Federal Reserve hike at about 80 per cent, with Tuesday's October consumer price report the last major input before the meeting. In Norway, October CPI on the same day is the first test of Norges Bank's hold on Thursday.

## ECB

In an interview with Yomiuri Shimbun, Lagarde noted that headline inflation was 4 per cent in October, with energy inflation at 20.8 per cent, while core inflation held at 2.5 per cent and services edged up to 3.3 per cent. She saw no clear second-round effects yet, but warned that "the higher they are, and the longer they remain high, the greater the risk to our medium-term target." On December she said: "I cannot tell you today that we will for sure do this in December." She attributed part of the global rise in long-term yields to competition for capital between sovereigns and AI-related firms, and urged highly indebted governments to respect the fiscal rules ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/inter/date/2026/html/ecb.in261107~e7b86fc4ce.en.html)). The deposit rate is 2.50 per cent. Euro-area finance ministers discuss the two candidates to succeed her, Klaas Knot and Pablo Hernández de Cos, on Monday.

## Federal Reserve

October payrolls rose 165,000 and unemployment fell to 4.1 per cent. The Bloomberg median for Tuesday's CPI is +0.3 per cent on the month for the headline, after +0.6 per cent in September, and +0.2 per cent for core. No year-on-year rate will be published because data for October 2025 were not collected during that year's government shutdown. Headline CPI has risen at an annualised 4.4 per cent over the past three months, and producer prices are running hotter, with September PPI at 6.3 per cent year on year ([Marc to Market](https://www.marctomarket.com/2026/11/week-ahead-feds-focus-on-accumulation.html)). Bloomberg expects price growth to have slowed in October after two uncomfortable months ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-07/us-inflation-set-to-simmer-down-some-after-recent-pickup)). The 2-year Treasury yield was 4.46 per cent at Thursday's fix, 99 basis points higher this year.

## Bank of England and Bank of Japan

Markets price about a 55 per cent chance of another Bank of England hike on 17 December; third-quarter GDP on Thursday is expected at +0.4 per cent. Swaps price about a 52 per cent chance of a Bank of Japan hike in December, down from just over 80 per cent a week earlier, after the sell-off in Japanese equities ([Marc to Market](https://www.marctomarket.com/2026/11/week-ahead-feds-focus-on-accumulation.html)).

## Other data

- **China.** October trade and inflation data are due in the coming days; no figures had been published at the time of writing. The People's Bank has raised the foreign-exchange reserve requirement, which is read as a signal against one-way yuan appreciation.
- **Norway.** October CPI is due Tuesday at 08:00; in September CPI was 3.4 per cent and CPI-ATE 3.0 per cent ([Statistics Norway](https://www.ssb.no/en/kalender)). No published consensus could be verified.

## Assessment

The balance of risks still leans towards tighter policy outside Norway. In the United States, a CPI print in line with consensus would probably not dislodge December pricing, while a third strong monthly reading would reinforce it. In the euro area, Lagarde's emphasis on the duration of energy-driven inflation keeps a December move open, though the 16 per cent fall in Brent last week, if sustained, would ease that pressure. Financial conditions have tightened through equities and credit, which argues for caution, but none of the major central banks has yet signalled that market turbulence alters its course.
