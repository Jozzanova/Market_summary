# Norges Bank, Sunday 8 November 2026

**Summary.** There was no Norges Bank news over the weekend. The Bank's news page lists nothing after Thursday's decision to keep the policy rate at 4.50 per cent, and no new comments from economists or the press on the rate outlook could be found for Saturday or Sunday. The standing view is a hold with a readiness to hike if needed; Tuesday's October CPI and the weaker krone are the next tests.

## What Norges Bank said

The latest items on the Bank's news page are the 5 November press release and two speeches the same day ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/news/)). The message stands: "inflation is too high", it "will likely be necessary to keep the policy rate elevated for a time", and the Committee is "prepared to raise the policy rate if needed" ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/topics/monetary-policy/Monetary-policy-meetings/2026/november-2026/)).

## What economists and the market say

No new analyst comments were found for the weekend. The latest views are Friday's: Nordea's Kjetil Olsen said the krone is still about 1 per cent stronger than Norges Bank assumed and that a weaker currency would not automatically mean a higher rate ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/Pg6kg0/kronen-svekkes-etter-global-boerssmell)). After Thursday's decision DNB Carnegie and Handelsbanken saw the peak as probably reached, while Danske Bank called December "a knife-edge" ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/p1o20V/sjefoekonom-de-holder-muligheten-aapne-for-en-heving-til)).

## The krone and rates

EUR/NOK ended the week at 10.54, 1.9 per cent higher, and the I-44 index at 108.79, 2.0 per cent weaker over the week, which narrows the gap to the Bank's krone assumption. At Thursday's fixes the 3-year government bond yielded 4.60 per cent and the 10-year 4.31 per cent, 17 and 13 basis points lower over the week, so the bond market did not price a firmer path despite the weaker currency. Abroad, the Federal Reserve, the Bank of England, the Riksbank and the RBA lean towards tightening, which supports interest-rate differentials against the krone; lower oil prices work the other way.
