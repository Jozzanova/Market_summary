# Markets letter, Sunday 8 November 2026

**Summary.** Markets are closed today, and the prices below are Friday's closes. The week ended with the S&P 500 down 2.7 per cent and the Nasdaq Composite down 3.4 per cent after the post-midterm unwinding of the AI trade, while Europe was broadly flat and Oslo fell 4.3 per cent. Weekend news was limited: ECB President Lagarde warned that inflation is likely to stay high in the near term, Bloomberg reports that the AI slump is reviving interest in other Asian trades, and there was no decision from Washington on Iran. The week ahead is dominated by consumer prices in Norway and the United States on Tuesday.

## Equities

- **United States.** The S&P 500 closed the week at 7,377 and the Nasdaq at 24,579; the Dow lost 0.9 per cent. The damage was concentrated in the AI complex. The Philadelphia semiconductor index (SOX) fell 12.2 per cent over the week despite Friday's 4.2 per cent rebound and is 28 per cent lower than a month ago, though still 33 per cent higher this year. NYSE FANG+ fell 4.3 per cent and the AI ETFs between 3.6 and 5.6 per cent, all behind the market, while the Magnificent Seven ETF held up better at −1.4 per cent. The technology sector lost 8.2 per cent and energy 12.0 per cent; communication services gained 4.9 per cent. The VIX ended at 25.6, 32 per cent higher over the week.
- **Europe.** The STOXX Europe 600 rose 0.2 per cent over the week and the CAC 40 2.1 per cent; the FTSE 100 fell 0.5 per cent and the DAX was unchanged.
- **Norway.** The benchmark index ended at 2,053, 4.3 per cent lower over the week, weighed by energy and shipping as oil fell, but 21.7 per cent higher this year.
- **Asia.** The Nikkei 225 fell 9.3 per cent over the week and the KOSPI 11.7 per cent, the markets most exposed to semiconductors, while the Hang Seng rose 5.1 per cent. Bloomberg reports that the unravelling of the AI trade is reviving demand for out-of-favour Chinese and Indian stocks ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-08/ai-slump-revives-asia-s-forgotten-trades-from-china-to-india)). India holds its one-hour Diwali trading session today ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-07/why-india-s-stock-markets-hold-a-session-on-diwali-explainer)).

## Fixed income and credit

- The US 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.86 per cent, 7 basis points lower over the week, and the 5-year at 4.65 per cent. Friday's strong employment report lifted the probability of a December Federal Reserve hike to about 80 per cent ([Marc to Market](https://www.marctomarket.com/2026/11/week-ahead-feds-focus-on-accumulation.html)).
- At Thursday's fixes the euro-area AAA 10-year yield was 3.29 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year 4.31 per cent, 8 and 13 basis points lower over the week. US high-yield spreads widened 22 basis points over the week to 348 basis points. Meta's 25 billion dollar bond sale on election day was a reminder of the scale of AI-related borrowing ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-07/meta-s-bond-sale-shows-new-reality-for-tech-debt-credit-weekly)); Lagarde linked higher long-term yields partly to competition for capital between governments and AI firms ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/inter/date/2026/html/ecb.in261107~e7b86fc4ce.en.html)).

## Commodities and currencies

- Brent ended at 98.3 dollars a barrel, 16.4 per cent lower over the week, on hopes of a US–Iran settlement. Tehran says the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until its conditions are met, while Qatar says its mediation "remains active" ([HNGN](https://www.hngn.com/articles/273722/20261107/trump-says-iran-decision-coming-soon-advisers-split.htm)). Dutch gas fell 17.7 per cent over the week to 85.6 euros; gold rose 4.5 per cent to 4,075 dollars.
- The dollar index rose 0.4 per cent over the week to 102.3, and USD/JPY ended at 158.4, near the top of its range.
- **The krone** weakened over the week: EUR/NOK rose 1.9 per cent to 10.54 and USD/NOK 2.3 per cent to 9.45. The import-weighted I-44 index ended at 108.79, 2.0 per cent weaker over the week but 9.2 per cent stronger this year. The combination of falling equities, lower oil and a firmer dollar has worked against the currency.
