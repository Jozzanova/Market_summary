# Markets, Friday 27 November 2026

**With US markets closed for Thanksgiving, European equities recovered much of the week's losses as technology and bank shares rebounded and the US trade office signalled that new tariffs would respect the caps in existing trade deals. Industrial and precious metals rose sharply, and the krone strengthened as risk appetite returned. In Asia this morning equities have extended the recovery, while Treasury yields rose when the cash market reopened, as lower demand for safe assets met an unchanged expectation of a Fed hike on 9 December.**

## Equities

- **Europe: a broad rebound.** STOXX Europe 600 rose 1.75 per cent, the Euro Stoxx 50 2.04 per cent, the DAX 2.08 per cent, the CAC 40 1.61 per cent and the FTSE 100 1.75 per cent. All major sectors rose, with miners, autos and defence up more than 3 per cent; Imperial Brands rose 13.3 per cent after Altria said it was considering an all-cash offer, while Soitec rose 7 per cent and Infineon 3.6 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/european-stocks-rally-tech-banks-083233000.html)). Reuters attributes the move to the US Trade Representative's plan to cap the new tariffs at the rates in existing deals, 15 per cent for the EU, to the nomination of Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next ECB President, read as a sign of continuity, and to a recovery in technology after two days of selling. Euro Stoxx Banks rose 2.48 per cent, recovering part of Wednesday's fall, although BAWAG fell 5.7 per cent and Commerzbank 2.6 per cent on company news. Trading was thinner than usual with New York closed.
- **Norway: higher with the rest of Europe.** The Oslo Børs benchmark index rose 1.46 per cent, with DNB up 1.53 per cent, Kongsberg Gruppen 2.93 per cent, Norsk Hydro 2.54 per cent and Aker BP 1.79 per cent, while Equinor fell 0.23 per cent ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/yz3adx/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)). Thor Medical fell 19.4 per cent after pausing production at Herøya and lowering its outlook, and Norse Atlantic fell a further 13 per cent after warning that it could go bankrupt without short-term financing. Hydro's gain is consistent with the rise in metal prices.
- **United States: closed, futures higher.** US markets were closed on Thursday and trade a shortened session today, closing at 13:00 New York time. Stock futures rose during Thursday and the Asian session ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The AI and semiconductor proxies have no new closes since Wednesday, when the SOX ended the week to date 9.1 per cent lower. Reuters reports that S&P placed Nvidia's credit rating on watch for a possible downgrade, and that, according to the Financial Times, the company has told partners to stop shipping servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/shares-footing-bruising-month-dollar-015346000.html)).
- **Asia this morning: the recovery continues.** The Nikkei 225 is up 1.37 per cent, KOSPI 2.49 per cent, the Hang Seng 1.10 per cent and the Shanghai Composite 0.33 per cent. Reuters notes that the Nikkei is still down 8.4 per cent for November, and links the steadier tone to easing concern about AI and holiday-thin trading ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/shares-footing-bruising-month-dollar-015346000.html)). China's State Council reaffirmed its support for the capital market in a rare midday readout ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/china-s-cabinet-stresses-market-support-in-midday-release)), and South Korean authorities will meet financial industry leaders on Monday to assess market conditions ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/south-korea-reportedly-mulls-expanding-stock-stabilization-fund)).

## Fixed income and credit

- **Treasury yields higher on the reopening.** The US 10-year yield is 4.91 per cent this morning, 6.4 basis points above Wednesday's close, and the 30-year 5.29 per cent, up 6 basis points; Reuters reports the 2-year 3 basis points higher at about 4.67 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/shares-footing-bruising-month-dollar-015346000.html)). Bloomberg attributes the move to lower demand for safe assets as Asian equities rose ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/treasuries-retreat-as-gains-in-equities-sap-demand-for-havens)): the safe-haven buying that pulled yields down on Wednesday is being partly reversed. Fed funds futures imply a Fed hike of 23.9 basis points on 9 December, a probability of about 96 per cent, and a further 17.8 basis points by 27 January.
- **Europe and Norway.** The German 10-year yield rose 2 basis points to 3.32 per cent and the 2-year 3 basis points to 3.06 per cent on Thursday, as investors moved back into equities. The ECB account of the October meeting, which showed that a large number of members could have supported a hike then, came in the afternoon and does not appear to have moved yields much. The Norwegian 10-year (live) rose 6 basis points to 4.18 per cent and the 5-year 7.5 basis points to 4.29 per cent, more than in Germany, partly reversing a fall of about 16 basis points over the week.

## Commodities and currencies

- **Metals sharply higher.** Gold rose 3.49 per cent to 3,659 dollars, silver 9.01 per cent, platinum 6.35 per cent and copper 5.64 per cent on Thursday (prints from a holiday-shortened session). Bloomberg links copper's rebound from an eleven-month low to the return of risk appetite in technology shares, and gold's rise to the recovery in technology stocks and a weaker dollar, which outweighed the prospect of Fed hikes ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/copper-rebounds-from-11-month-low-on-return-of-risk-appetite)). CNBC cautions, quoting Trade Nation's David Morrison, against reading much into moves made in thin holiday trading ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).
- **Oil supported by the Gulf.** Brent for January delivery is about 96 dollars this morning, 0.4 per cent lower, but 3.9 per cent higher over the week ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/shares-footing-bruising-month-dollar-015346000.html)); the dashboard's front-month series, now the next contract, is 92.32 dollars, up 1.28 per cent. The support comes from a drone attack in Kuwait that killed two Italian technicians, which Kuwait and Italy blame on Iran, Iranian claims that two tankers struck mines near the Strait of Hormuz, and a Houthi attack on a Saudi refinery ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-military-ai-weapons-oil/)). TTF gas rose 0.90 per cent to 88.09 euros per megawatt hour.
- **Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.75 and EUR/USD 1.0973, both little changed; Reuters notes that the dollar is on course for a third monthly gain, supported by the expected Fed hike and safe-haven demand. USD/JPY is 159.30. The krone strengthened: EUR/NOK fell 0.64 per cent to 10.680 and USD/NOK 0.46 per cent to 9.734, and I-44 fell 0.56 per cent at Thursday's fix to 110.84 (a fall is a stronger krone). The return of risk appetite and higher oil prices appear to explain the move: the krone, as a small and less liquid currency, tends to weaken when investors seek safety and to recover when equities rise. The New Zealand dollar edged up after the election left a hung parliament.
