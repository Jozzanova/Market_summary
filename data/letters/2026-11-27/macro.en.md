# Macro, Friday 27 November 2026

**The account of the ECB's October meeting shows that a large number of Governing Council members could already then have supported a hike, which strengthens the case for a move on 17 December; the same day euro-area leaders recommended Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next ECB President. Tokyo inflation has risen further, supporting a Bank of Japan hike in December, while growth in mainland Norway slowed more than Norges Bank expected. The Bank of England warned that the financing of AI investment is becoming harder to see through.**

## European Central Bank

- **Account: hike support was already broad in October.** All members agreed with Chief Economist Philip Lane's proposal to keep the deposit facility rate at 2.50 per cent, but "a large number of members" said the data since September had confirmed the case for further tightening and that they could have supported a hike at the meeting ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/accounts/2026/html/ecb.mg261126~ccf28c8071.en.html)). Their argument was that energy prices had moved above the levels in the adverse scenario, that a durable resolution of the conflict in the Middle East had become less likely, and that "the only issue was timing". The hold was accepted on condition that the communication made clear that the direction of travel was upward unless the inflation outlook improved significantly. Members also noted that the deposit rate still lies within staff estimates of the neutral rate, the level at which policy neither restrains nor stimulates the economy, which implies that rates are not yet restrictive.
- **Pricing.** The last reported market pricing is an 87 per cent probability of an ECB hike of 25 basis points on 17 December (LSEG data, 25 November); no newer figure was found. The account makes a hike more likely unless the December staff projections show a clearly better inflation outlook. Euro-area inflation was 4 per cent in October ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/pablo-hernandez-de-cos-ecb-president.html)).
- **Succession.** Euro-area leaders recommended Hernández de Cos, former governor of the Banco de España and since 2025 general manager of the BIS, for an eight-year term from 8 February 2027; the European Parliament and the Governing Council are consulted before the formal appointment ([Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/26/european-council-puts-forward-pablo-hernandez-de-cos-as-next-president-of-the-european-central-bank/)). Laurence Boone and Markus Brunnermeier were put forward for the Executive Board. CNBC describes him as moderately dovish but pragmatic: he supported large hikes during the 2022 energy shock, and Christian Kopf of Union Investment said his views "may well be regime-dependent" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/pablo-hernandez-de-cos-ecb-president.html)). Reuters reports that investors read the choice as continuity ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/european-stocks-rally-tech-banks-083233000.html)).

## Other central banks

- **Federal Reserve: pricing unchanged over the holiday.** There were no Fed communications on Thursday. Fed funds futures imply a hike of 23.9 basis points on 9 December, a probability of about 96 per cent, and a further 17.8 basis points by 27 January; Reuters cites a 99 per cent probability ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/shares-footing-bruising-month-dollar-015346000.html)).
- **Bank of Japan: Tokyo inflation supports a December hike.** Core inflation in Tokyo, which excludes fresh food, rose to a sixteen-month high in November, which Reuters says strengthens the case for a BoJ hike as soon as next month ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/shares-footing-bruising-month-dollar-015346000.html)). Short-term Japanese government bond yields rose on the release ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/short-term-jgb-yields-rise-on-elevated-tokyo-inflation-data-713a900b?mod=rss_markets_main)). Tokyo's figures are published three weeks before the national ones and are read as a leading indicator.
- **Bank of England: financial stability.** The Financial Policy Committee judged that geopolitical conflict, concern about sovereign debt and volatile valuations linked to AI raise the chance that several risks crystallise at once, and described AI financing as "increasingly complex, circular and opaque", that is, the same firms increasingly appear as lenders, investors and customers of one another, so that losses could spread in ways that are hard to trace ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2026/november-2026)). The countercyclical capital buffer was kept at 2 per cent, and the review of the capital framework will lower Tier 1 requirements for the major banks by about 50 basis points of risk-weighted assets.

- **Norway: weak growth with strong consumption.** Mainland GDP grew 0.1 per cent in the third quarter, below Norges Bank's projection of 0.3 per cent; lower power production and fishing subtracted 0.1 percentage point ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/nasjonalregnskap-og-konjunkturer/nasjonalregnskap/statistikk/nasjonalregnskap/artikler/svak-vekst-i-fastlandsokonomien)). Household consumption rose 1.6 per cent, while mainland investment fell 1.8 per cent and petroleum investment 8.0 per cent. Statistics Norway's Pål Sletten said that growth "har flatet ut gjennom året", and is now somewhat weaker than normal. The quarterly trade surplus of 307 billion kroner was the largest outside the 2022 energy crisis, as high oil and gas prices raise export revenue.
- **China.** Industrial profits rose sharply in October, supported by strong foreign demand for technology products ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/chinas-industrial-profit-surged-despite-headwinds-0296ec7c?mod=rss_socialeconomyfeed)), while households continue to reduce consumer debt ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/8c5e0298-58f0-47e9-a843-513178d9fa2e?syn-25a6b1a6=1)), a split between external strength and weak domestic demand.

## Assessment

The balance of risks still points to higher policy rates in the United States, the euro area and Japan. The ECB account shows that the majority was close to acting in October, and with inflation at 4 per cent and energy prices above the adverse scenario, the December projections would need to show a clearly better outlook to prevent a hike. In Japan, rising core inflation in Tokyo makes a December hike more likely. In Norway, the weak mainland growth eases capacity pressures somewhat, but strong household consumption suggests that demand is holding up. The main downside risks are tighter financial conditions after the fall in AI shares, which the Bank of England now names as a source of systemic risk, and the conflict in the Gulf, which keeps energy prices high and volatile.
