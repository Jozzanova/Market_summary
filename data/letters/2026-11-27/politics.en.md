# Politics, Friday 27 November 2026

**The political news with a market effect came from Europe and the Gulf: euro-area leaders agreed on the next ECB President but not on the EU's long-term budget, and the US tariff proposal now appears capped at the rates in existing trade deals. In the Gulf, a deadly drone attack in Kuwait and Iranian claims of mine strikes on tankers keep a risk premium in oil. In Norway the budget still lacks a majority ahead of Monday's committee deadline.**

## United States

- **Tariffs capped at deal rates.** The US Trade Representative plans to cap the proposed new tariffs at the lower rates set in trade deals, 15 per cent for the EU ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/european-stocks-rally-tech-banks-083233000.html)); the measures would not take effect before a public hearing on 25 January ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural)). Market channel: a ceiling on the rate removes the worst outcome for European exporters, which Reuters cites as one reason for Thursday's rise in European equities.
- **Nvidia and the administration.** President Donald Trump said Jensen Huang is looking forward to a "second term" as Nvidia's chief executive ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/trump-jensen-huang-ceo-second-term-at-nvidia)). Huang appears before the House Foreign Affairs Committee on 1 December and the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December.

## Europe

- **ECB President agreed, budget not.** Euro-area leaders recommended Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde from 8 February 2027 ([Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/26/european-council-puts-forward-pablo-hernandez-de-cos-as-next-president-of-the-european-central-bank/)). The summit ended after almost fourteen hours without agreement on the 2028–2034 budget; President Emmanuel Macron said France would not conclude the talks after 31 December, citing the presidential election in spring 2027, and the proposed 45 billion euro loan to Ukraine was postponed to December ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/26/eu-summit-leaders-ready-to-spar-over-new-budget-and-ukraine-aid)). Market channel: the nomination removes uncertainty about the ECB's direction, while a budget dispute tied to the French election calendar keeps attention on French spreads.
- **Defence.** The EU is preparing tens of billions of euros in new loans and grants to replace US military capabilities ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/eu-plans-new-funds-to-help-replace-us-war-fighting-capabilities)); defence shares rose more than 3 per cent on Thursday.
- **Spain votes on Sunday.** The snap general election on 29 November is followed for its effect on Spanish spreads ([Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirwFBVV95cUxOaFZGRnlyUS1kZHlNY28tLXpFdGFiMGhPZTNCTGtkaDNaZ0t6YVdqU3VGeTA0YWVIdHp)).

## Middle East and energy

- **Attacks in Kuwait and the Strait of Hormuz.** Two Italian technicians were killed in a drone attack on a Kuwaiti military facility that Kuwait and Italy attribute to Iran, which denies involvement; European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said "we must step up pressure on Iran" ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-military-ai-weapons-oil/)). Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed that two tankers struck mines near Khasab, which could not be independently confirmed, and a Houthi attack on the Jazan refinery killed a worker. Kpler data cited by CBS show weekly transits through the strait falling from 217 to 147 in November. Market channel: fewer transits and the threat to tankers raise the risk premium in oil and in shipping costs; Brent is about 3.9 per cent higher over the week.

## Asia and elsewhere

- **New Zealand: hung parliament.** Official results left the National-led coalition without a majority and dependent on the new Opportunity Party ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/bc5d39a0-6610-481b-9138-5afdd22cf406?syn-25a6b1a6=1)); the New Zealand dollar nonetheless edged higher ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/new-zealand-dollar-gains-as-traders-look-past-hung-parliament)).
- **Japan and Taiwan.** Japan's upper house rejected a no-confidence motion against Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's education minister ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/japan-s-upper-house-rejects-motion-against-scandal-hit-minister)). Taiwan holds local elections on Saturday ([Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/politics/202611030029)).
- **Brazil.** President Lula named the banker Luiz Carlos Trabuco as finance minister after a sell-off in Brazilian assets ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/94f93408-476b-479c-be0e-d7f7942f9491?syn-25a6b1a6=1)), a choice read as an attempt to reassure investors about fiscal policy.

## Norway

- **Budget without a majority.** The agreement between Labour, the Centre Party, the Socialist Left and Red still lacks the Greens' votes; acting Green leader Ingrid Liland said "det er fortsatt tid til å finne en rødgrønn enighet", while Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre pointed to the days left for dialogue ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/artikkel/rodgront-budsjettkaos-derfor-mener-de-mdg-bor-snu-1533986)). The finance committee's recommendation is due on Monday. Market channel: limited, since the deal changes the use of oil revenue only marginally, but a government crisis would add uncertainty to fiscal policy in 2027.
