# Norges Bank, Friday 27 November 2026

**Mainland GDP grew 0.1 per cent in the third quarter, below Norges Bank's projection of 0.3 per cent, but household consumption rose markedly. The one economist quoted on the figures, Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge, reads them as easing pressure in the economy; none of the economists on file expects a hike on 17 December. The krone strengthened on Thursday as risk appetite returned. Retail trade and registered unemployment are published at 08:00.**

## New data

{bars: Mainland Norway, Q3 2026, per cent from previous quarter | Mainland GDP 0.1; Household consumption 1.6; Public consumption 0.3; Mainland investment −1.8; Employment 0.2 | | SSB}

- **Weak growth, strong consumption.** Mainland GDP rose 0.1 per cent from the second quarter; excluding fishing and electricity supply it rose 0.2 per cent ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/nasjonalregnskap-og-konjunkturer/nasjonalregnskap/statistikk/nasjonalregnskap/artikler/svak-vekst-i-fastlandsokonomien)). Household consumption rose 1.6 per cent, goods 2.2 per cent, after a weak first half, while mainland investment fell 1.8 per cent, investment in manufacturing 6.1 per cent and petroleum investment 8.0 per cent; housing investment rose 0.5 per cent but remains 17.4 per cent below the first quarter of 2023. Employment rose 0.2 per cent and hours worked 0.1 per cent. Monetary Policy Report 3/26 projected 0.3 per cent; no market consensus was reported.
- **Revisions and wages.** With the new base year, mainland growth was revised up 0.2 percentage point in the first quarter and down by the same amount in the second. Wages per hour worked rose 3.6 per cent in the first three quarters, less than in 2025. The trade surplus of 307 billion kroner was the largest outside 2022, owing to high oil and gas prices.
- **Reading.** Growth below trend points to somewhat less pressure on capacity than Norges Bank assumed. Against that, the rise in consumption suggests that household demand is recovering despite the policy rate at 4.5 per cent, and the shortfall is partly explained by temporary factors in power and fishing.

## What others say

- **On the economy and the rate.** Kyrre M. Knudsen, chief economist at SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge, told NTB that "lavere vekst [kan] gjøre det lettere å nærme seg inflasjonsmålet på 2 prosent" and that the figures are "positivt for alle som ønsker rentekutt", while noting that registered unemployment is still low and job growth fairly good ([NTB](https://sol.no/nyheter/positivt-for-alle-som-onsker-rentekutt/85473160)). No other bank comments on the release were found.
- **Standing views.** The views on file are unchanged: Knudsen expects the rate to be kept in December, and Kjersti Haugland of DNB Carnegie expects 4.5 per cent well into next year but calls the krone "en joker" ([Dagbladet Børsen](https://borsen.dagbladet.no/nyheter/en-joker-kan-avgjore/85377056)). None of the economists quoted expects a hike on 17 December. The last reported market reading is about a 35 per cent probability of a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November); no newer figure was found.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK fell 0.64 per cent to 10.680 and USD/NOK 0.46 per cent to 9.734; I-44 fell 0.56 per cent at Thursday's fix to 110.84 (a fall is a stronger krone), still 0.59 per cent higher than a week ago.

**Why.** The krone strengthened as European equities rose and the price of oil was supported by the attacks in the Gulf: a small currency tends to gain when risk appetite returns and when the terms of trade improve. The weak GDP figure did not appear to weigh on the krone.

**Differentials.** The policy rate differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points. The 12-month differential in government rates was 1.83 percentage points at Wednesday's fix, little changed over the week. The Norwegian 10-year (live) rose 6 basis points on Thursday to 4.18 per cent, against 2 basis points in Germany, so the differential to the German 10-year is about 0.86 percentage point; the Norwegian 10-year is about 16 basis points lower over the week.
