# Markets, Saturday 7 November 2026

**Summary.** Wall Street steadied on Friday after Thursday's sharp fall, led by a rebound in semiconductor shares, while Europe and Oslo closed lower as they caught up with the US sell-off. The S&P 500 rose 0.3 per cent and the Nasdaq Composite 1.1 per cent, but both ended the week lower, by 2.7 and 3.4 per cent. The October employment report was much stronger than expected, with payrolls up 165,000, and lifted the probability of a Federal Reserve hike in December to about 78 per cent. Treasury yields edged up, Brent was unchanged near 98 dollars after a 16 per cent fall over the week, and the krone weakened further: EUR/NOK ended the week at 10.54 and USD/NOK at 9.45. Markets are closed today.

## Equities

- **United States.** The S&P 500 closed at 7,377 (+0.3 per cent), the Nasdaq at 24,579 (+1.1 per cent) and the Dow at 51,286 (−0.5 per cent). The rebound was narrow and concentrated in the AI complex: the Philadelphia semiconductor index (SOX) rose 4.2 per cent, though it remains 12 per cent lower over the week and 28 per cent lower over a month. NYSE FANG+ gained 0.7 per cent, the Magnificent Seven ETF 1.0 per cent and the AI ETFs between 0.9 and 1.3 per cent, broadly in line with or slightly ahead of the Nasdaq. Technology rose 1.5 per cent while consumer staples fell 1.3 per cent. Press coverage described the recovery as modest relative to the scale of the week's losses, which Bloomberg put at 5.7 trillion dollars globally ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/stocks/u-s-stocks-mixed-after-jobs-data-muted-ai-rebound-945344f6), [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/088ed252-ea9c-4b61-b2d1-37295fd0f7c2), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/ai-s-wild-week-has-wall-street-hunting-for-fresh-escape-routes)). The VIX fell 9 per cent to 25.6, still a third higher than a week ago.
- **Europe.** The STOXX Europe 600 closed 1.0 per cent lower at 648, the DAX 1.9 per cent, the FTSE 100 1.0 per cent and the CAC 40 0.8 per cent, as Europe absorbed Thursday's US decline. Over the week the STOXX 600 was little changed (+0.2 per cent) and the Euro Stoxx 50 rose 0.6 per cent ([Business Recorder/Reuters](https://brecorder.com/news/40447852/risk-off-mood-sweeps-european-stocks-as-ai-concerns-deepen)).
- **Norway.** The benchmark index fell 1.3 per cent to 2,053 and is 4.3 per cent lower over the week, though still 22 per cent higher this year. Frontline was among the largest decliners and fell 18 per cent over the week, while Norwegian rose ([Finansavisen](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwAFBVV95cUxPSmhZMzhhbE5tcmZxc0NsRGl3VXloSi1ZQnREZ3l3eklfU2Z1azdCWVpYTnVvbERxNzlOaUJFaUZLVUpuZ3lWUE0xeHdpdUFRWTZqR0d4dWxLLXJRU1lMdzZRd2d5UEVFUnVaU2haM18xYWsyZVRzamxDd3ZnNXdaM0h4UGEwWjl2UTg0cEIxU3huWGRNdW1aUkxGS3hBMElrc2tDaHpCUDk1ZjlyODh6czlpalBncjFtSHFmc1ZFakU?oc=5), [E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/rMv53w/boersene-skjelver-oslo-i-ekstrem-sweet-spot)). A technical error at the exchange briefly showed the index up 66,000 per cent at the open.
- **Asia.** There is no trading today. On Friday the Nikkei 225 fell 6.0 per cent and the KOSPI 6.0 per cent, as reported yesterday; the emerging-market ETF rose 2.2 per cent in New York. India holds a special Diwali session on Sunday ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-07/why-india-s-stock-markets-hold-a-session-on-diwali-explainer)).

## Fixed income and credit

- The US 10-year yield rose 2 basis points to 4.86 per cent and the 5-year 4 basis points to 4.65 per cent after the employment report; over the week the 10-year is 7 basis points lower. Futures now place the last hike of the cycle in April rather than June ([Business Recorder/Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40447788/fed-seen-sticking-to-december-rate-hike-after-jobs-data)).
- At Thursday's fixes the 2s10s curve was 38 basis points, the euro-area AAA 10-year 3.29 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year 4.31 per cent. US high-yield spreads widened 16 basis points to 348 basis points, 22 basis points wider over the week.

## Commodities and currencies

- Brent ended at 98.3 dollars, unchanged on the day and 16 per cent lower over the week as hedge funds cut bullish bets on signs of US–Iran de-escalation ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/hedge-funds-fled-bullish-oil-bets-ahead-of-election-day-drop)). Dutch gas fell 5.6 per cent; gold eased 0.9 per cent to 4,075 dollars.
- The dollar index rose 0.7 per cent to 102.3; the Australian dollar fell 0.9 per cent.
- **The krone** weakened further: EUR/NOK rose 0.9 per cent to 10.54 and USD/NOK 1.7 per cent to 9.45. The import-weighted I-44 index was 108.79 at Friday's fix, 1.0 per cent weaker on the day and 2.0 per cent over the week, but 9 per cent stronger than at the start of the year. Nordea's Kjetil Olsen attributes much of the move to currency hedging by pension funds when equity prices fall, which he called "almost mechanical" ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/Pg6kg0/kronen-svekkes-etter-global-boerssmell)).
