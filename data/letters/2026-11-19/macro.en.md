# Macro, Thursday 19 November 2026

**The Fed's communication tilted further towards tightening: the October minutes show almost all participants expecting further increases, and Governor Waller argued for 50 basis points in December. Elsewhere the picture is more mixed. UK inflation rose but came in below the Bank of England's forecast, which lowered the probability of a December hike, and Australian unemployment rose to its highest since 2021, which has led markets to price out further RBA hikes.**

## Federal Reserve

**Minutes.** The minutes of the 27–28 October meeting record the 10–2 decision to hold the target range at 3.75–4.00 per cent, with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack preferring an increase. Most participants supported the pause to learn more about AI-related investment demand, financing conditions and the effects of the September increase, and a few called it a close call. Almost all participants judged that "further action to raise the target range" would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks, and a couple said that "an increase of more than 25 basis points could warrant consideration at the next meeting" if inflation data were unfavourable. The vast majority saw upside risks to inflation from energy prices, supply constraints and AI-related demand ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm)).

**Waller.** Governor Christopher Waller said in a CNBC interview that a 50 basis point hike is "probably necessary" on 8–9 December, as inflation is "headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out"; a quarter-point increase would suffice only with a signal of a further move in January, which Chair Kevin Warsh has opposed ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/federal-reserve-governor-christopher-waller.html)). CME FedWatch puts the probability of a 25 basis point Fed hike on 9 December at 95 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/treasury-yields-bonds-fed.html)); no published figure for the probability of 50 basis points was found.

## Other central banks

- **Bank of England.** After the inflation figures, markets saw an 80 per cent chance of a Bank of England hike on 17 December, down from 90 per cent, while still expecting three to four quarter-point increases by the end of 2027 ([Reuters](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1118/1599633-uk-inflation-figures/)).
- **ECB.** Policymakers will discuss raising the minimum reserve requirement from 1 to 2 per cent at their non-monetary meeting on 25 November, with a decision possibly deferred to 17 December, according to sources ([Reuters](https://www.1049thewolf.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-ecb-to-discuss-raising-reserve-requirement-next-week-sources-say/)). No newer report on the pricing of the 17 December meeting was found; the last one, on 4 November, was about 72 per cent for a hike.
- **Bank of Japan.** Governor Kazuo Ueda told parliament that it would be appropriate to continue raising the policy rate ([Reuters](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/348857/Bank-of-Japan-governor-says-central-bank-should-keep-raising-rates)), and this morning said he expects consumer spending to return to a rising trend despite the recent contraction ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/boj-s-ueda-unfazed-by-consumption-drop-stays-open-to-rate-hike)).
- **Reserve Bank of Australia.** Unemployment rose 0.1 percentage point to 4.9 per cent in October and employment fell by 43,500 ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/employment-and-unemployment/labour-force-australia/latest-release)); traders have begun to price RBA rate cuts ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/australian-unemployment-jumps-to-4-9-following-rba-rate-hikes)). After five increases, the labour market now appears to be responding.

## Data

- **United Kingdom.** Consumer prices rose 3.6 per cent in the twelve months to October, up from 3.5 per cent and in line with the Reuters poll, but below the Bank of England's forecast of 3.7 per cent; core inflation was unchanged at 2.8 per cent and services inflation was 3.7 per cent, against 3.8 per cent expected ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/october2026), [Reuters](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1118/1599633-uk-inflation-figures/)). The rise is mainly energy; stable core and softer services inflation suggest less second-round pressure than the Bank feared.
- **Euro area.** HICP inflation was confirmed at 4.0 per cent in October, up from 3.8 per cent ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/2-18112026-ap)).
- **United States.** Housing starts rose to an annual rate of 1.309 million in October from 1.191 million, while permits fell 7.4 per cent to 1.295 million ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/pdf/newresconst_202610.pdf)). Moody's expects growth of 2.3 per cent this year, slowing in 2027 and 2028 as higher interest rates and energy prices weigh ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/moodys-says-u-s-economy-grew-more-than-expected-416c91a5)).

## Assessment

The balance of risks has shifted slightly towards tighter US policy and slightly away from it elsewhere. In the United States, the minutes show that a pause in October was a close call, and the debate has moved to the size of the December increase. In the United Kingdom and Australia, the latest figures point to less pressure than feared. The fall in oil prices, about 20 per cent this month, works in the other direction for headline inflation and, if sustained, would ease the energy contribution that drove the minutes' upside risks. For Norway, lower oil prices and a weaker krone pull in opposite directions for imported inflation.
