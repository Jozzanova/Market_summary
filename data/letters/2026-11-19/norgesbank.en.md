# Norges Bank, Thursday 19 November 2026

**The krone weakened on Wednesday as oil prices fell further and Oslo Børs declined for a third day. Statistics Norway's labour cost index showed annual growth of 4 to 5 per cent in most industries in the third quarter. No new comments on Norges Bank were found; the economists quoted still expect a hold on 17 December, and today's Expectations Survey is the next input.**

## New data

**Labour costs.** Labour costs in manufacturing rose 4.7 per cent from a year earlier in the third quarter and 0.8 per cent from the second quarter; construction rose 4.2 per cent, retail trade 4.5 per cent and public administration 4.7 per cent, with a range from about 3.1 per cent in business services to 5.0 per cent in finance ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/lonn-og-arbeidskraftkostnader/statistikk/arbeidskraftkostnadsindeks)). Growth of this size is somewhat above the 4.3 per cent wage growth economists expected for 2026 in the third-quarter Expectations Survey, which suggests that domestic cost pressure is not yet easing markedly. No press comment on the release was found.

**Expectations Survey.** The fourth-quarter survey is published at 10:00 ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/aktuelt/kalender/26-11-19-forventningsundersokelsen/)). In the third quarter, economists expected inflation of 2.7 per cent two years ahead and 2.4 per cent five years ahead, while business leaders expected 4.6 and 5.4 per cent ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/contentassets/8724efe42e7a41328218ef734a16d92e/expectations-survey-q3-2026.pdf)). Whether October's fall in inflation to 3.6 per cent has lowered the longer-term expectations is the question.

## What others say

- **No new comments.** No bank economist commented on Norges Bank on Wednesday that could be found. NHO said on Monday that the wage settlements do not need to stand in the way of Norges Bank ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/yzvere/trenger-ikke-aa-spenne-bein-paa-norges-bank)).
- **Standing views.** Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects the rate to be held in December with a signal that the peak has been reached ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)); Marius Gonsholt Hov of Handelsbanken said a hike before Christmas would require "ganske kraftig oppside" in the data ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)); Frank Jullum of Danske Bank believes the peak has been reached, but said a December hike would be "helt klart aktuelt igjen" after an inflation surprise of 0.3 percentage points ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/eG3QBa/tror-rentetoppen-er-naadd-balanserer-paa-en-knivsegg)). None of the economists quoted expects a hike in December; Allan Monks of JP Morgan expects one in the first quarter.
- **Market pricing.** No new figure was found; the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.658 this morning, 0.76 per cent higher than at the previous close, and USD/NOK 9.720, 1.00 per cent higher; I-44 was 109.92 at Wednesday's fix, up 0.27 per cent (a rise is a weaker krone).

**Why.** Brent fell for a further day on rising US inventories ([E24](https://e24.no/bors/nyheter/a/306284?pinnedEntry=306284)), which weakens the terms of trade, Oslo Børs fell 1.05 per cent, and the dollar rose to a 19-month high after the Fed minutes and Waller's remarks. All three point to a weaker krone; no Norwegian source attributes the move specifically.

**Differentials.** Against the euro area the differential is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, 1.80 percentage points at twelve months in government rates (the stand-in for the swap differential), 1 basis point higher than in the previous fix, and 0.97 percentage points at ten years. The Norwegian 10-year yield (live) fell 4.7 basis points to 4.36 per cent, while the German 10-year rose 1 basis point, so the long differential narrowed; the krone weakened nonetheless, which points to oil and risk appetite rather than rates as the driver.
