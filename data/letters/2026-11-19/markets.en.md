# Markets, Thursday 19 November 2026

**The session was shaped by the Fed and by commodities rather than by Nvidia's results. The October minutes and Governor Waller's case for a 50 basis point hike pushed the short end of the US curve up, while a further fall in oil and the sharpest drop in copper since April pulled long yields, commodity shares and the krone down. Equities were mixed: technology recovered slightly, but health insurers, energy and European miners fell markedly.**

## Equities

**United States.** The S&P 500 fell 0.23 per cent, its fifth consecutive fall and the longest losing run since June ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)), and the Dow 1.11 per cent, while the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.42 per cent. Nvidia fell 2.9 per cent although revenue of 115.8 billion dollars was well above the consensus of 109.1 billion; doubts about the return on AI investment appear to have outweighed the figures ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trading-day-markets-mixed-in-a-nvidia-anthropic-minutes-swirl-ce785edddf8bf621)). Humana fell 13 per cent and UnitedHealth about 10 per cent after the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services proposed wider audits of Medicare Advantage plans ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/trading-day-markets-mixed-nvidia-221528504.html)). Among the sector ETFs, energy fell 3.27 per cent and health care 2.20 per cent, while consumer discretionary rose 1.79 per cent. The AI proxies did roughly in line with the Nasdaq: SOX rose 0.10 per cent, NYSE FANG+ 0.65 per cent and the Magnificent Seven ETF 0.62 per cent. VIX fell 0.9 points to 24.6.

**Europe and Norway.** STOXX Europe 600 fell 1.02 per cent to its lowest close in almost two weeks. Basic resources fell 4.7 per cent, the most since May, with Glencore and Aurubis down more than 5 per cent as copper reached a seven-month low, and oil and gas shares fell 2.1 per cent; Deutsche Bank fell a further 3.1 per cent ([Reuters](https://lufkindailynews.com/news_reuters/business/european-stocks-hit-two-week-low-on-weak-miners-caution-ahead-of-anthropic-ipo/article_7b8eb40e-039b-5bde-8cf3-40d8fa4999fd.html)). Oslo Børs fell 1.05 per cent, a third day of decline, with the weakness deepening after the US open; Panoro Energy, Vow and Arribatec fell markedly after quarterly figures ([E24](https://e24.no/bors/nyheter/a/306283?pinnedEntry=306283)).

**Asia this morning.** The Hang Seng is down 1.89 per cent, with Tencent down more than 6 per cent after its results, and New World Development has lost more than half its value this week after announcing a heavily discounted rights issue ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/new-world-shares-tank-54-to-head-for-worst-week-on-rights-issue)). The Nikkei is up 0.34 per cent, while KOSPI is down 0.24 per cent and the Shanghai Composite 0.44 per cent.

## Fixed income and credit

The US 10-year yield fell 3.7 basis points to 5.07 per cent and the 30-year 4.6 basis points to 5.41 per cent, while the 2-year rose about one basis point to 4.80 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/treasury-yields-bonds-fed.html)); the 5-year fell 1.6 basis points to 4.88 per cent. The flattening reflects two forces: the minutes and Waller's remarks raised expected policy rates, while lower oil prices reduced the inflation compensation demanded at long maturities. CME FedWatch puts the probability of a 25 basis point Fed hike on 9 December at 95 per cent. The Norwegian 10-year yield (live) fell 4.7 basis points to 4.36 per cent and the 5-year 5.1 basis points, in line with the US long end and the lower oil price.

## Commodities and currencies

**Energy.** Brent settled 1.19 dollars lower at 91.95 dollars and WTI 3.2 per cent lower at 83.26 dollars, its lowest since August, after US crude inventories rose by 3.4 million barrels against expectations of little change; attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz limited the fall ([Reuters](https://www.myjoyonline.com/oil-prices-settle-lower-as-traders-weigh-surplus-supply-outlook/)). The front-month quote is 89.24 dollars this morning, 2.95 per cent below Wednesday's settlement; no report of a fall of that size overnight was found, so the quote should be read with caution. TTF gas fell 5.3 per cent to 80.85 euros; no source attributes the move.

**Metals.** Copper fell 5.7 per cent on the London Metal Exchange as AI-related positions were unwound ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/copper-prices-industrial-metal-dollar-lme)) and has steadied this morning ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/copper-rebounds-after-rout-as-physical-tightness-persists)). Gold fell 3 per cent to a 14-month low and is 3,724 dollars this morning ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trading-day-markets-mixed-in-a-nvidia-anthropic-minutes-swirl-ce785edddf8bf621)).

**Currencies.** The dollar rose to a 19-month high on Wednesday, with sterling the weakest G10 currency; the dollar index is 103.92 this morning, EUR/USD 1.0967 and USD/JPY 159.57. The krone weakened: EUR/NOK is 10.658, 0.76 per cent higher, and USD/NOK 9.720, 1.00 per cent higher, while I-44 (a rise is a weaker krone) was 109.92 at Wednesday's fix. Lower oil prices, a fall on Oslo Børs and a firmer dollar all point the same way; no Norwegian source attributes the move.
