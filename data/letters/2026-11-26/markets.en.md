# Markets, Thursday 26 November 2026

**A sharp intraday sell-off in US technology shares was largely reversed before the close, and Asian markets have recovered this morning as investors look past the proposed US tariffs. Firm US data, with core PCE inflation above expectations and GDP growth revised up, confirmed the pricing of a Fed hike in December without pushing yields higher: government bond yields fell as investors sought safety during the sell-off. Oil rose on Houthi attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure, and the krone was little changed. US markets are closed today for Thanksgiving.**

## Equities

- **United States: a large intraday swing, a small net decline.** The S&P 500 fell 0.25 per cent after being down as much as 3.2 per cent during the day, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62 per cent after a fall of as much as 4.6 per cent; the Dow rose 0.33 per cent and the Russell 2000 0.71 per cent ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-november-25-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-pce-inflation-data-091428237.html)). Materials rose 2.07 per cent and real estate 0.92 per cent, while technology fell 0.83 per cent. The VIX rose 1.9 points to 28.3, so the recovery in prices did not bring the price of protection down.
- **AI and semiconductors: losses again, but smaller.** The semiconductor index SOX fell 0.65 per cent and is down 9.1 per cent over the week; NYSE FANG+ fell 0.76 per cent and the Magnificent Seven ETF 1.05 per cent. Nvidia fell about 4 per cent, giving back more of Monday's gain on Jensen Huang's reinstatement, and Micron, Sandisk and Oracle fell 4 to 5 per cent ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Business/stocks-tumble-tech-sell-off-resumes-nvidia-falls/story?id=138442070)). Nomura's Charlie McElligott argued that Huang's return does not offset tighter financial conditions, that is, the higher cost of funding for the AI investment programmes ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-november-25-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-pce-inflation-data-091428237.html)).
- **Europe lower, led by banks.** STOXX Europe 600 fell 1.06 per cent, its largest decline in three weeks, the Euro Stoxx 50 1.82 per cent and the DAX 1.26 per cent; banks fell about 3 per cent and technology declined for the ninth time in ten sessions ([Reuters](https://longbridge.com/en/news/306829837)). The ECB Governing Council discussed on Wednesday raising banks' unremunerated minimum reserve ratio from 1 to 2 per cent of deposits, which Reuters estimated would cost banks about 4.4 billion euros a year, because the extra reserves earn no interest ([Reuters](https://1027wbow.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-ecb-to-discuss-raising-reserve-requirement-next-week-sources-say/)); this is a likely contributor to the fall in bank shares, although no major outlet ties the two explicitly. Oslo Børs was unchanged (−0.02 per cent): Norse Atlantic fell 85 per cent after saying its financial situation is critical, and Vår Energi 2.6 per cent after a share placement of 10.5 billion kroner at a discount, while Equinor rose 1.05 per cent with oil ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/dJOVqJ/nedgang-paa-oslo-boers)).
- **Asia this morning: a broad recovery.** The Nikkei 225 is up 1.53 per cent, KOSPI 1.70 per cent and the Hang Seng 1.86 per cent, while the Shanghai Composite is unchanged; SoftBank Group is up 11 per cent and SK hynix 4 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/stock-markets-today-.html)). Reuters reports that Taiwan's index is up 2.6 per cent and that investors are looking past the new US tariffs ([Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/asian-stocks-rally-as-taiwan-south-korea-rebound-nvidia-risks-linger-5052021)). The Bank of Korea raised its policy rate in the morning without unsettling equities.

## Fixed income and credit

- **Yields fell despite firm data.** The US 10-year yield fell 5.6 basis points to 4.85 per cent and the 5-year 5.2 basis points to 4.66 per cent; Reuters reports that the 10-year fell as much as 10 basis points intraday to a two-and-a-half-month low of 4.80 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/dollar-steady-as-sliding-yields-temper-safehaven-bid-5051883)). Demand for Treasuries as a safe asset during the equity sell-off thus outweighed core PCE inflation above expectations. Fed funds futures imply a Fed hike of 23.6 basis points on 9 December, against 23.9 on Wednesday morning, and a further 17 basis points by 27 January.
- **Europe and Norway.** The German 10-year yield fell 3 basis points to 3.30 per cent and the 2-year 3 basis points to 3.03 per cent. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 4 basis points to 4.12 per cent and the 5-year 5 basis points to 4.22 per cent. US high-yield spreads widened 9 basis points to 3.72 percentage points at Tuesday's fix, 29 basis points wider than a week earlier.

## Commodities and currencies

- **Oil higher on attacks on Saudi infrastructure.** The January Brent contract settled 3.5 per cent higher at 94.61 dollars on Wednesday and WTI rose 2 per cent, after the Houthis claimed attacks on the Ras Tanura refinery and Riyadh; short-covering before the holiday, when traders who had bet on lower prices bought back contracts, added to the move ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/oil-wti-brent-hormuz-middle-east.html)). The front-month series shows 91.15 dollars this morning; the apparent fall overnight reflects the change to the next contract month as January Brent expires. TTF gas fell 4.76 per cent to 87.30 euros on milder weather forecasts and higher Norwegian flows ([Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/european-natural-gas-prices-drop-amid-forecasts-for-milder-temperatures-5050476)).
- **Metals recover this morning.** Gold is 3,621 dollars, up 2.42 per cent, silver up 5.19 per cent and copper up 3.00 per cent, after gold fell on Wednesday following the PCE figures. Bloomberg links the rebound in copper from an eleven-month low to the gains in technology shares ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/copper-rebounds-from-lowest-this-year-as-volatility-hits-metals)).
- **Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.82, EUR/USD 1.0963 and USD/JPY 159.18. The krone was little changed this morning: EUR/NOK is 10.741 and USD/NOK 9.800, while I-44 rose 0.39 per cent at Wednesday's fix to 111.47 (a rise is a weaker krone), 1.41 per cent higher than a week ago. Lower risk appetite during Wednesday's sell-off appears to have weighed on the krone more than the higher oil price supported it.
