# Norges Bank, Thursday 26 November 2026

**Unemployment in the labour force survey edged up to 4.7 per cent, and the budget agreement raises the use of oil revenue slightly. No economist published a new view on the 17 December decision in the past 24 hours; the standing view among those quoted is a hold. The krone has weakened about 1.4 per cent over the week by the import-weighted index, and E24's sources describe the move as a correction after a strong autumn. The national accounts for the third quarter are published at 08:00.**

## New data

- **Unemployment slightly higher.** Unemployment in the labour force survey was 4.7 per cent in October (trend), up 0.1 percentage point from September, with 144,000 unemployed; the employment rate was unchanged at 69.7 per cent ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/sysselsetting/statistikk/arbeidskraftundersokelsen)). Statistics Norway notes that the rate has stayed between 4.6 and 4.7 per cent since the turn of the year, with unemployment rising among women and falling among men, and that preliminary figures show the number of jobs falling by just over 6,000 in October, mainly in health and education ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/sysselsetting/statistikk/arbeidskraftundersokelsen/artikler/arbeidsledigheten-pa-4-7-prosent)). No consensus was reported. The figures point to a labour market that is easing only gradually, which on its own does little to change the case for keeping the policy rate at 4.5 per cent.
- **National accounts today.** Norges Bank's Monetary Policy Report 3/26 projected mainland GDP growth of 0.3 per cent in the third quarter, the same as in the second quarter ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/contentassets/fc580519ec63404980e5daada0ca7790/ppr-3-26.pdf)). No consensus has been reported for this release or for Friday's retail trade and registered unemployment.
- **Budget.** The deal between Labour, the Centre Party, the Socialist Left and Red raises the use of oil revenue by 7.4 billion kroner to 615.8 billion, about 2.75 per cent of the fund, but lacks a majority as the Greens stayed out ([NTB](https://sol.no/nyheter/hjelper-ikke-norges-bank/85469395)). A slightly more expansionary budget adds marginally to demand; the change is small relative to the economy.

## What others say

- **On the rate path.** No new comments were found from 24 to 26 November, and no new market pricing was reported. The views on file are unchanged: Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects the rate to be kept unchanged in December, and Harald Magnus Andreassen of SpareBank 1 Markets said after the October CPI that inflation is on the track Norges Bank assumed ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)); Kjersti Haugland of DNB Carnegie expects 4.5 per cent well into next year but calls the krone "en joker" ([Dagbladet Børsen](https://borsen.dagbladet.no/nyheter/en-joker-kan-avgjore/85377056)). None of the economists quoted expects a hike in December. The last reported market reading is about a 35 per cent probability of a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).
- **On the krone.** Dane Cekov of SpareBank 1 Markets told E24 that "alle tekniske indikatorer skrek at denne kronestyrkingen hadde gått for langt", and expects some further mild weakening towards year-end; E24 also points to lower oil and gas prices and to the dollar's role as a safe haven during the turbulence in AI stocks ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/mMRoX0/kronen-svekkes-hadde-gaatt-for-langt)). A weaker krone raises import prices with a lag, so if the move persists it would weaken one of the arguments economists have used for a hold.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.741 this morning, little changed, and USD/NOK 9.800; I-44 rose 0.39 per cent at Wednesday's fix to 111.47 (a rise is a weaker krone), 1.41 per cent higher than a week ago.

**Why.** Brent rose 3.5 per cent on Wednesday, which would normally support the krone, while TTF gas fell 4.8 per cent and the sharp intraday fall in US technology shares lowered risk appetite, which tends to weigh on smaller currencies. Over the week, lower oil and gas prices than in October and the dollar's safe-haven role appear to have mattered most; the rate differential has not moved against the krone.

**Differentials.** The policy rate differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points. The 12-month differential in government rates was 1.83 percentage points at Tuesday's fix, about 3 basis points wider than a week earlier. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 4 basis points on Wednesday to 4.12 per cent, against 3 basis points in Germany, so the long differential is about 0.82 percentage point; the Norwegian 10-year has fallen 23.5 basis points over the week.
