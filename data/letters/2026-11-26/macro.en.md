# Macro, Thursday 26 November 2026

**US data on Wednesday showed an economy that is still growing fast and core inflation that is still rising, which confirms the case for a Fed hike on 9 December; the Beige Book gave a more mixed picture of activity. Outside the United States, the Bank of Korea raised its policy rate, a Bank of Japan board member argued for timely hikes and Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey noted that lower energy prices would lower the inflation peak, so the tightening cycle continues but its pace differs across countries.**

## Federal Reserve

- **Core PCE above expectations.** The PCE price index rose 0.3 per cent in October and 3.8 per cent over twelve months, as expected, while the core index rose 0.3 per cent on the month and 3.3 per cent over twelve months, against 3.2 per cent expected and the highest in about three years ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/pce-inflation-report-october-2026.html)). Consumer prices (CPI) rose 3.7 per cent over twelve months in September. The rise in the core measure, which excludes food and energy, shows that price pressures are broadening rather than reflecting energy alone.
- **Growth revised up.** Third-quarter GDP growth was revised to 4.3 per cent at an annual rate from 3.4 per cent, against 3.5 per cent expected, and final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 5.8 per cent; initial jobless claims fell to 189,000, against 200,000 expected ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/pce-inflation-report-october-2026.html)). Personal income rose 0.7 per cent and spending 0.3 per cent.
- **Beige Book less strong.** Eight of twelve districts reported growth "in the slight to moderate range" and four no change; consumer spending was roughly flat, employment rose slightly, and "input cost growth generally outpaced increases in selling prices" ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/beigebook202611-summary.htm)). The last point suggests that margins are being squeezed, which could pass through to consumer prices in coming months.
- **Pricing.** Fed funds futures imply a Fed hike of 23.6 basis points on 9 December, a probability of about 94 per cent of a quarter-point move, and a further 17 basis points by 27 January. CNBC reports that the probability of a third hike in 2027 fell to about one in three from even odds ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/pce-inflation-report-october-2026.html)).

## Other central banks

- **Bank of Korea: hike and higher growth forecasts.** The Bank of Korea raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to 3.25 per cent, its third hike this year, and raised its GDP growth forecast for 2027 to 4.1 per cent from 2.9 per cent; Governor Shin Hyun-song said further policy response is "warranted" until inflation converges to target ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/bok-rate-decision-korea-gdp-inflation.html)). Investment in AI chip production lies behind the stronger growth.
- **Bank of Japan: Tamura argues for timely hikes.** Board member Naoki Tamura said the BoJ "must respond swiftly to signs of an upward deviation in prices", that the policy rate of 1.25 per cent is "still some way" from neutral, and that recent price developments suggest further upside risks ([Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/bojs-tamura-signals-chance-of-fasterpace-interest-rate-hikes-5051855)). Reuters reports that markets widely expect a BoJ hike to 1.5 per cent on 18 December.
- **Bank of England: Bailey and Mann.** Governor Andrew Bailey told the Treasury Committee that "if the current prices were to stay where they are, the peak of inflation mechanically would be lower", and that the labour market has stopped loosening, while external member Catherine Mann argued that "the optimal response is to hike more and sooner"; Reuters reports that markets price about an 80 per cent chance of a BoE hike on 17 December ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/boes-bailey-sees-lower-inflation-145022000.html)). The Financial Stability Report is published today.
- **ECB.** Markets price an 87 per cent chance of an ECB hike of 25 basis points on 17 December (LSEG data), and German GDP growth for the third quarter was revised down to 0.1 per cent ([Reuters](https://longbridge.com/en/news/306829837)). The Governing Council discussed raising banks' minimum reserve ratio; no decision has been announced.

Together with October inflation of 4.9 per cent, this weakens the case for the RBA cut that money markets have priced for 2027.

## Assessment

The balance of risks still points to higher policy rates. In the United States, strong growth and rising core inflation leave little doubt about a December hike; the question for markets is the path in 2027, where the Beige Book's signs of flat consumer spending and squeezed margins argue for caution. In Korea, investment linked to AI is lifting growth and inflation, and in Japan price pressures are broadening, while in the United Kingdom lower energy prices could limit the need for further hikes. The fall in AI shares and the proposed US tariffs are the main downside risks to growth.
