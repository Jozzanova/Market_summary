# Markets, Monday 23 November 2026

**This report covers Friday's session and the weekend. Friday ended the worst week for US equities since the spring of 2025, led by a renewed fall in semiconductor shares, while Treasuries again served as a haven. Over the weekend Asian authorities introduced support measures, and US equity futures recovered a good part of Friday's loss in Asian trading; equities in Seoul nevertheless fell further, while government bond futures gave back some of last week's gains as demand for safe assets eased.**

## Equities

- **United States: a seventh day of declines.** The S&P 500 fell 3.70 per cent on Friday, the Nasdaq Composite 5.28 per cent and the Dow 1.93 per cent; the S&P 500 and the Dow closed more than 10 per cent below their records ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/sp-500-correction-market-sell-off.html)). The VIX rose 7.5 points to 35.9.
- **AI and semiconductors fell most.** The semiconductor index SOX fell 10.73 per cent, NYSE FANG+ 6.41 per cent, the Magnificent Seven ETF 5.18 per cent and the AI ETF AIQ 6.25 per cent, against 3.70 per cent for the S&P 500. Nvidia fell 14 per cent after Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining its accounting, and Anthropic fell 8.5 per cent on its second trading day ([Bloomberg](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/us-stock-futures-rally-after-ai-rout,-havens-fall:-markets-wrap/92421262)). Technology fell 7.27 per cent while consumer staples rose 0.67 per cent, as investors moved towards less cyclical earnings.
- **Europe and Norway.** STOXX Europe 600 fell 2.00 per cent and the DAX 2.60 per cent, while the FTSE 100 fell 1.02 per cent. Oslo Børs fell 1.50 per cent, its fifth daily decline, with Nordic Semiconductor down 6.7 per cent ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/ez8RGy/nedgang-paa-oslo-boers)).
- **Asia this morning.** Tokyo is closed for a public holiday. KOSPI is down 3.22 per cent, with SK hynix and Samsung down about 4 per cent ([AFP](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261123-asian-tech-back-in-the-firing-line-as-traders-track-wall-st-rout)); the Hang Seng is down 0.64 per cent and the Shanghai Composite 0.96 per cent, while Taiwan's market rose 2.1 per cent after its worst week since 1991. S&P 500 futures are 2.2 per cent higher and Nasdaq 100 futures 2.9 per cent higher ([Bloomberg](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/us-stock-futures-rally-after-ai-rout,-havens-fall:-markets-wrap/92421262)). Over the weekend China eased margin requirements for buying shares with borrowed money, Taiwan widened its curbs on short selling and South Korea prepared a stabilisation fund it last used in 2008.

## Fixed income and credit

- **A haven on Friday, some reversal this morning.** The US 5-year yield fell 16.5 basis points to 4.70 per cent and the 10-year 14 basis points to 4.91 per cent; the 30-year fell 10 basis points. The 5-year, which carries expectations of the policy rate over the next few years, fell most. Treasury futures fell about 0.2 per cent this morning, ending five days of gains. Fed funds futures price a Fed hike of 22.5 basis points on 9 December, about 90 per cent, and 17 more by January.
- **Europe and Norway.** The German 10-year yield fell 6 basis points to 3.39 per cent and the 2-year 6 basis points to 3.08 per cent. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 6.5 basis points to 4.28 per cent and the 5-year 8.4 basis points to 4.39 per cent.

## Commodities and currencies

- **Oil and gas.** Brent's January contract fell about 0.3 per cent to 92.29 dollars this morning after settling at 92.57 dollars on Friday; the equity fall weighed, while a Houthi claim of an attack on a Saudi refinery and attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz limited the decline ([Reuters](https://www.asiaone.com/money/oil-prices-fall-amid-equity-selloff-though-iran-war-attacks-limit-losses)). TTF gas rose 4.7 per cent on Friday to 88.45 euros, and European gas rose further this morning as colder temperature forecasts threaten inventories ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/european-gas-surges-as-cold-snap-threatens-tight-inventories)).
- **Metals.** Gold is 2.7 per cent higher at 3,610 dollars after its largest weekly fall in eight months, as buyers returned and the market pared bets on a December Fed hike. Copper fell below 12,000 dollars a tonne in London.
- **Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.77, EUR/USD 1.0985 and USD/JPY 159.00; the yen gave back part of Friday's gain as demand for havens eased. The krone is slightly firmer this morning: EUR/NOK is 10.703 and USD/NOK 9.744, against 10.716 and 9.770 at Friday's close. I-44 rose 0.50 per cent at Friday's fix to 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone), in line with lower risk appetite.
