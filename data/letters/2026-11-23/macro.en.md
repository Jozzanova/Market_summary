# Macro, Monday 23 November 2026

**This report covers Friday and the weekend. No central bank met, and no new speeches by Federal Reserve or ECB officials were found for the weekend. The equity fall has so far changed little in the pricing of a Fed hike on 9 December, but it adds a second consideration to the decision: tighter financial conditions do part of the work of a higher policy rate. The week brings US growth and PCE inflation figures before Thanksgiving, and Norway's national accounts.**

## Federal Reserve

- **Pricing broadly unchanged.** Fed funds futures price a hike of 22.5 basis points on 9 December, a probability of about 90 per cent, and a further 17 basis points by 27 January; a week earlier the December figure was about 26 basis points. The pricing beyond December has fallen more than the December pricing itself, which suggests that the market sees the equity fall as a reason for a shorter tightening cycle rather than for a pause next month. Gold's rebound this morning was attributed in part to the market paring bets on a December hike ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/gold-rebounds-as-traders-pare-bets-on-near-term-fed-rate-hikes)).
- **Friday's speeches.** Governor Lisa Cook said it was not clear that a single increase would be enough to return inflation to target, citing estimates of 3.8 per cent headline and 3.2 per cent core PCE inflation for October ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20261120a.htm)).
- **Inflation expectations.** The US 10-year breakeven inflation rate fell 1 basis point on Friday to 2.15 per cent, and the 5-year inflation expectation five years ahead rose 2 basis points to 2.12 per cent; long-run expectations thus remain close to the Fed's target despite PCE inflation of 3.6 per cent in September.

## Other central banks

- **ECB.** No new communication was found over the weekend. The German 2-year yield fell 6 basis points on Friday to 3.08 per cent, in line with lower global yields; no updated figure for the pricing of the 17 December meeting was available.
- **Bank of England.** Chief economist Huw Pill speaks today, and the Financial Stability Report follows on Thursday; after October inflation of 3.6 per cent, below the bank's forecast, markets priced about an 80 per cent chance of a Bank of England hike on 17 December (Reuters, 18 November).
- **Reserve Bank of Australia.** AMP's chief economist Shane Oliver said the risk of a recession in Australia is "high and rising" after this year's rate increases ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/australia-recession-risk-is-high-and-rising-amp-s-oliver-says)).

## Assessment

The balance of risks has shifted. Until last week, the central question for the Fed was whether one hike would be enough to bring inflation down from well above 3 per cent; the equity fall now raises the risk that lower household wealth and tighter credit conditions slow demand faster than expected. That would argue for a shorter tightening cycle, but it does not obviously change the December decision, since core inflation is still well above target and long-run expectations have risen little. Wednesday's PCE figures are the main test: a core reading at or above the 3.2 per cent Cook cited would support the market's expectation of a hike, while weaker activity data would strengthen the case for stopping there.
