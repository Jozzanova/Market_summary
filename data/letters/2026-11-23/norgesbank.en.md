# Norges Bank, Monday 23 November 2026

**No new Norwegian data were published on Friday or over the weekend that bear on the policy rate, and no new comments by economists on the 17 December decision were found. The standing view is unchanged: none of the economists quoted expects a hike in December. The krone weakened further on Friday as global equities fell, but is slightly firmer this morning.**

## New data

No new key figures. The week brings the credit indicator on Tuesday, the labour force survey on Wednesday, the national accounts for the third quarter on Thursday and retail trade and registered unemployment on Friday; the national accounts will show how far higher rates have dampened mainland activity.

## What others say

- **On the markets.** Joachim Bernhardsen, chief strategist at Nordea, described Friday's fall in equities as a form of capitulation: "Det er litt oppvask nå", and pointed out that high interest rates make funding harder for technology companies that rely on the debt markets ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/pML5zo/ny-uke-med-markedsuro-det-er-litt-oppvask-naa)).
- **On the rate path.** No economist published a new view over the weekend. Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects a hold in December, Harald Magnus Andreassen of SpareBank 1 Markets expects no hike this year or next ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)), and Frank Jullum of Danske Bank believes the peak has been reached, while Allan Monks of JP Morgan expects a further hike in the first quarter ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/eG3QBa/tror-rentetoppen-er-naadd-balanserer-paa-en-knivsegg)). None of them expects a hike in December.
- **Market pricing.** No new figure was found; the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.703 this morning and USD/NOK 9.744, slightly below Friday's close of 10.716 and 9.770; I-44 rose 0.50 per cent at Friday's fix to 110.74 (a rise is a weaker krone). Over the past week the krone has weakened about 1.5 per cent against the euro.

**Why.** Friday's weakening coincided with a broad fall in global equities and lower risk appetite: when equity prices fall, Norwegian life insurers and pension funds that hedge the currency risk on their foreign assets become over-hedged and sell kroner to adjust, which tends to weaken the krone mechanically, as Nordea's chief economist Kjetil Olsen explained after a similar episode earlier this month ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/Pg6kg0/kronen-svekkes-etter-global-boerssmell)). Brent changed little on Friday, so the terms of trade do not explain the move. The slight recovery this morning coincides with higher US equity futures.

**Differentials.** The policy rate differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points. The 12-month differential in government rates was 1.78 percentage points at Thursday's fix, little changed over the week. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 6.5 basis points on Friday to 4.28 per cent, slightly more than the German 10-year, so the long differential against Germany narrowed to about 0.89 percentage point. The differentials have changed too little to explain the krone's weakening.
