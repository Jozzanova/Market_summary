# Macro, Wednesday 18 November 2026

**No central bank changed rates on Tuesday, but the day's data and speeches leaned towards more tightening. In the United States, Fed officials argued about the pace of further hikes rather than their need, and retail sales were firmer underneath than the headline. In the United Kingdom unemployment fell, which keeps a December Bank of England hike in play ahead of today's inflation figures, and in Australia wage growth was the fastest in almost three years.**

## Federal Reserve

St. Louis Fed president Alberto Musalem said that "the economy will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027", and that the real policy rate should be 2 to 2.5 per cent by then, about a percentage point above its current level; he described a scenario with no further hikes as "unlikely but still plausible" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/alberto-musalem-fed-interest-rates.html)). Musalem does not vote on the FOMC until 2028. New York Fed president John Williams, who votes, urged measured action ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/fed-s-williams-says-no-need-for-dramatic-rate-move)), and Cleveland Fed president Beth Hammack proposed principles for market interventions ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/fed-s-hammack-proposes-principles-for-market-interventions)). The pricing was little affected: fed funds futures imply about 24 basis points of Fed tightening on 9 December and a further 19 basis points by 27 January, and CME FedWatch put the probability of a December hike at 95.4 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/wall-street-ends-lower-ahead-of-nvidia-results-focus-turns-to-anthropic-debut-ce785edcdc81fe2d)). A lawyer for Governor Lisa Cook said she will not take part in the White House hearing on whether to remove her unless a senior economic adviser is taken off the panel ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/feds-lisa-cook-rebuffs-white-house-hearing-citing-biased-panel-e6eac42b)). The minutes of the October meeting are published tonight.

## Other central banks

- **Bank of England.** Unemployment fell to 4.6 per cent in July to September, 0.3 percentage points lower than in the previous quarter, while regular pay growth eased to 3.5 per cent, and to 3.1 per cent in the private sector ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/uklabourmarket/november2026)). The ONS warns that sample rotation affects the recent movements. David Rees of Schroders called the figures a "clear warning shot" for the Monetary Policy Committee, while Jack Meaning of Barclays expects a hold in December, citing the noise in the survey and the weakness in other labour market indicators ([Pound Sterling Live](https://www.poundsterlinglive.com/gbp-live-today/23885-pound-to-euro-and-dollar-uk-jobs-data-december-hike)). Today's October CPI is the main test before 17 December.
- **ECB.** Chief economist Philip Lane spoke on monetary policy communication without guidance on the December meeting ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/key/date/2026/html/ecb.sp261117~7f5d3b1a0e.en.pdf)). The last reported pricing is still a probability of about 72 per cent of an ECB hike on 17 December.
- **Bank of Japan.** Governor Kazuo Ueda said the planned cut in the sales tax on food is likely to support growth by raising households' spending power ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/boj-s-ueda-says-food-sales-tax-cut-likely-to-help-spur-growth)). Demand at this morning's 20-year government bond auction was weaker than the 12-month average ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/japan-20-year-bond-sale-demand-is-weaker-than-12-month-average)), a reminder that the long end remains sensitive to fiscal policy.
- **People's Bank of China.** The daily yuan fixing was set at the strongest level since 2022 as the APEC meeting began ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/pboc-sets-strongest-yuan-fix-since-2022-to-allow-further-gains)).

## Data

- **US retail sales.** Sales rose 0.2 per cent in October, after an unrevised 0.8 per cent in September; the headline is not statistically different from zero. Excluding autos sales rose 0.6 per cent and excluding autos and gasoline 0.5 per cent, while auto dealers fell 1.6 per cent and nonstore retailers rose 1.2 per cent ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/retail/marts/www/marts_current.pdf)). The figures are nominal: with gasoline prices high, part of the rise is prices, but the core categories suggest that household demand held up into the fourth quarter. That supports the Fed's case for further tightening.
- **Australia.** The wage price index rose 1.1 per cent in the third quarter, the fastest quarterly increase since December 2023, and 3.5 per cent from a year earlier; private-sector wages rose 1.0 per cent ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/wage-price-index-australia/sep-2026)). The acceleration strengthens the case made by the majority at the RBA's November meeting, although futures had priced no further hike on 8 December.
- **United States, other.** Industrial production weakened, import prices rose on petroleum, and homebuilder sentiment fell to its lowest since April 2012 ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/wall-street-ends-lower-ahead-of-nvidia-results-focus-turns-to-anthropic-debut-ce785edcdc81fe2d)).

## Assessment

The balance of the day's information tilts the risks towards more tightening in the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia: domestic demand and wages are holding up while inflation remains above target. The counterweight is in asset prices. A fourth day of equity losses and a VIX above 25 tighten financial conditions, and if the fall in AI-related shares spreads to investment plans, as Musalem himself noted, demand could slow faster than the central banks assume. For now, markets treat a Fed hike in December as all but certain; the October minutes and the UK CPI today are the next inputs.
