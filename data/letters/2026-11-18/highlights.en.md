# Key points, Wednesday 18 November 2026

**Equities.** Equities fell for a fourth day, with the weakness moving from chips to software, banks and Europe: the S&P 500 fell 1.03 per cent, the Nasdaq 0.74 per cent and the Dow 1.65 per cent, while SOX rose 0.34 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/wall-street-ends-lower-ahead-of-nvidia-results-focus-turns-to-anthropic-debut-ce785edcdc81fe2d)). STOXX Europe 600 fell 1.75 per cent, its largest fall since March, with technology down 3.9 per cent and Deutsche Bank 6 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/european-stocks-mark-worst-day-in-eight-months-on-ai-jitters-deutsche-bank-slides-ce785edcdd8df723)); Oslo Børs fell 0.34 per cent ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/8PmPEr/nedgang-paa-oslo-boers)). Nvidia's revenue of 115.8 billion dollars beat expectations and the shares rose 3 to 5 per cent after hours; this morning KOSPI is up 1.2 per cent and the Nikkei 0.4 per cent, while the Hang Seng is down 1.1 per cent.

**Government bonds.** The US 10-year yield fell 2 basis points to 5.10 per cent and the 5-year 3.5 basis points to 4.90 per cent. Fed funds futures still price a Fed hike of about 24 basis points on 9 December and a further 19 basis points by 27 January. The German 10-year yield fell 3 basis points to 3.42 per cent, while the Norwegian 10-year (live) rose 2 basis points to 4.41 per cent.

**Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.62, EUR/USD 1.0991 and USD/JPY 159.15. The krone is slightly stronger this morning: EUR/NOK is 10.590, 0.25 per cent below Tuesday's close, and USD/NOK 9.637; I-44 was 109.63 at Tuesday's fix, little changed, as a firmer oil price offset the fall in equities.

**Energy.** Brent rose slightly on Tuesday as tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz offset the prospect of a supply surplus ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449308/oil-prices-caught-between-rising-mideast-exports-and-tanker-attacks)), and is 92.48 dollars this morning. TTF gas rose 0.8 per cent to 85.40 euros.

**Drivers.** St. Louis Fed president Alberto Musalem said several hikes will likely be needed before spring 2027 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/alberto-musalem-fed-interest-rates.html)); US retail sales rose 0.2 per cent in October, 0.6 per cent excluding autos ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/retail/marts/www/marts_current.pdf)); UK unemployment fell to 4.6 per cent ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/uklabourmarket/november2026)); Australian wages rose 1.1 per cent in the quarter ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/wage-price-index-australia/sep-2026)); the United States struck Iran's Qeshm Island after Iranian attacks on Bahrain, and Kuwait reported drone attacks ([AFP](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261118-stocks-drop-as-booming-nvidia-results-drowned-by-spy-scandal)).

**Norway.** The differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, 1.79 percentage points at twelve months in government rates and 0.96 percentage points at ten years. There were no new Norwegian key figures or comments on Norges Bank on Tuesday; the economists quoted still expect a hold on 17 December, and the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike. Thursday's Expectations Survey is the next input.
