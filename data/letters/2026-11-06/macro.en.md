# Macro letter, Friday 6 November 2026

**Summary.** The tightening cycle broadened this week. The Bank of England raised Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4.00 per cent, its first increase since 2023, and the Riksbank and the Reserve Bank of Australia also raised rates. Norges Bank, the Federal Reserve and the ECB held, each with language that keeps further tightening on the table. The common thread is inflation that has been pushed up by energy prices and has proved slower to recede than expected, set against labour markets that are cooling only gradually. Thursday's fall in US equities, which spread to Asia this morning with the Nikkei 225 down close to 6 per cent, and the decline in Treasury yields have, at the margin, lowered the market-implied probability of a Federal Reserve hike in December to about 70 per cent.

## Norges Bank

The Committee kept the policy rate at 4.50 per cent on Thursday, following the increase in September. The statement notes that inflation remains above target, with CPI at 3.4 per cent and CPI-ATE at 3.0 per cent in September, and that "it will likely be necessary to keep the policy rate elevated for a time". The Committee added that it "is prepared to raise the policy rate if warranted by the inflation outlook". It observed that the krone has appreciated and is stronger than assumed in September, which, other things equal, dampens imported inflation. The next decision, on 17 December, comes with a new Monetary Policy Report and a new rate path. The October CPI on 10 November is the main domestic release before then. ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/news/Press-releases/2026/2026-11-05-rate/))

## Bank of England

The Monetary Policy Committee raised Bank Rate to 4.00 per cent by eight votes to one, with the dissenter preferring a 50 basis point increase. The November Monetary Policy Report projects inflation of about 5 per cent by mid-2027, driven by energy. Governor Bailey said he "could see the case for a further increase". Gilt yields rose modestly on the day and sterling was little changed after reaching a post-Brexit high on Wednesday. ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/november-2026), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/interest-rates-inflation-uk.html))

## Federal Reserve

The FOMC held the target range at 3.75 to 4.00 per cent on 28 October with two dissents in favour of a hike. Thursday's data were firm: initial claims fell to 189,000 against 197,000 expected, third-quarter productivity rose 3.6 per cent at an annual rate with unit labour costs down 0.7 per cent, and announced job cuts in October were 71 per cent lower than a year earlier. Even so, the fall in equities and in yields reduced the market-implied probability of a December hike to about 70 per cent from 83 per cent. Today's employment report, with consensus payroll growth of 59,000 to 65,000 and unemployment at 4.2 per cent, is the last major labour-market release before the meeting. ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm), [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-11-05/gold-holds-gains-silver-slips-wall-street-plunges-fed-hike-bets-ease-kitco))

## ECB and the Nordics

The ECB held the deposit facility rate at 2.50 per cent on 29 October, after the increase in September, with euro-area inflation at 3.8 per cent in September. President Lagarde announced that she will leave office on 7 February 2027, which adds a leadership transition to the December and February meetings. The Riksbank raised its policy rate to 2.00 per cent on 4 November and said further increases are likely over the coming year. ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/29/ecb-holds-rates-at-25-as-lagarde-announces-early-exit-with-inflation-near-double-the-targe), [Riksbank](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/nyheter--pressmeddelanden/pressmeddelanden/2026/261104_nr1-upp/prm-4-november-2026-styrrantan-hojs-till-2-procent.pdf))

## Assessment

Policy rates are being raised or held at restrictive levels across the advanced economies in response to an energy-driven inflation impulse, while growth is slowing and asset prices have become more volatile. The December meetings of the Federal Reserve, the ECB, the Bank of England, Norges Bank and the Bank of Japan fall within nine days of each other and will be taken with the benefit of two more inflation readings. Norges Bank's Governor noted on Thursday that AI investment has increasingly been financed with debt, which may have contributed to higher interest rates, and that markets must be prepared for volatility; this week's equity moves are a test of that point ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/sentralbanksjefen-om-kronekurs-renter-og-ki-gjeld-norges-bank/s/80-95-222675)). The main risks to the outlook are, on the upside for inflation, a renewed rise in oil prices if shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is disrupted further, and, on the downside for activity, a tightening of financial conditions if the equity market correction deepens.
