# Norges Bank letter, Friday 6 November 2026

**Summary.** Norges Bank kept the policy rate at 4.50 per cent on Thursday, as all sixteen economists in Bloomberg's survey expected, and retained its readiness to raise the rate further. The statement describes inflation as too high and the krone as stronger than assumed, and notes that the market prices a further increase by the end of the first half of 2027. Economists read the decision as a hawkish hold: the bar for another increase is low if inflation surprises on the upside, but a strong krone could allow the Committee to avoid it. The krone nonetheless weakened for a third day.

## What Norges Bank said

The decision, taken at the meeting on 4 November, was unanimous. Governor Ida Wolden Bache: "Inflation is too high. It will likely be necessary to keep the policy rate elevated for a time, and we are prepared to raise the policy rate if needed to bring inflation down to target within a reasonable time horizon." The statement notes that twelve-month CPI inflation was 3.4 per cent in September and CPI-ATE 3.0 per cent, that inflation in domestically produced goods and services "remains elevated and has been higher than projected", and that "the krone has appreciated and is stronger than assumed", which in isolation dampens inflation. New information since September "pulls in different directions". The next decision is on 17 December, with Monetary Policy Report 4/26 and a new rate path. ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/topics/monetary-policy/Monetary-policy-meetings/2026/november-2026/))

## What economists and the market say

- **Handelsbanken.** Marius Gonsholt Hov, chief economist in Oslo: "It would have been bold of Norges Bank to remove this wording now", on the Committee's readiness to raise the rate. ([Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/norges-bank-holds-at-4-5-keeps-door-open-to-hike-if-inflatio/))
- **Oxford Economics.** Rory Fennessy: "the bar for a further policy tightening is low if inflation spikes higher." Economists quoted in the same report said a stronger krone could let the Committee avoid further increases this cycle. Markets price about a 50 per cent probability of a move by March. ([Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/norges-bank-holds-at-4-5-keeps-door-open-to-hike-if-inflatio/))
- **Nomura.** The hold was "as we and consensus expected". Nomura expects no change in 2026 and 2027 and sees the next move as a cut, "likely only from 2028", because disinflation in Norway is very gradual and "the bar to the first Norges Bank rate cut is quite high". ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/norwegian-krone-gradual-disinflation-delays-rate-cuts-nomura-202611051200))
- **Commerzbank.** Antje Praefcke, before the decision: the bank "is nevertheless likely to keep the door wide open for further rate hikes, including as early as December", while the market "is far from convinced that it will raise rates in December"; the krone "remains primarily driven by oil and gas prices". ([Tradingpedia](https://www.tradingpedia.com/2026/11/05/krone-traders-focus-on-norges-bank-energy-markets/))

The views differ mainly on timing: Commerzbank and the market pricing allow for a hike in December or March, Nomura sees none, and the Bank's own reference is the market's pricing of an increase by mid-2027.

## The krone and rates

The hawkish wording did not support the krone on the day. Norges Bank's EUR/NOK fix was 10.44 on Thursday, against 10.40 on Wednesday and 10.32 on Monday, and the krone weakened further overnight as US equities fell. Oil's rebound to about 99 dollars for Brent was not enough to offset the move in risk sentiment. The three-month treasury bill yields 4.73 per cent and the ten-year government bond 4.37 per cent at Wednesday's fix, with the curve inverted between three months and ten years.

## Dates

- 10 November: CPI for October (Statistics Norway); the last inflation print before the Committee's December material is assembled.
- 17 December: rate decision with Monetary Policy Report 4/26.
- 21 January 2027: the first decision of the new year.
