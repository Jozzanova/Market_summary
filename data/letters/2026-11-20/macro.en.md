# Macro, Friday 20 November 2026

**The Fed's speakers kept to the tightening line, but Thursday's data pointed the other way: the Philadelphia Fed index fell to a six-month low while its prices-paid component rose markedly, and continuing jobless claims increased. In Japan, inflation edged up in October, which keeps a Bank of Japan hike in December the most likely outcome, although the turmoil in equities has led some economists to question the timing.**

## Federal Reserve

**Speeches.** Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said that "if inflation expectations risk becoming unanchored, a stronger reaction to inflation is warranted", without signalling the size of a December move ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/jefferson20261119a.htm)). Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said "the way we bring inflation down is by having higher rates" and that there is "more tightening to do", favouring a gradual pace ([Reuters](https://www.freedom969.com/business/feds-paulson-backs-further-rate-hikes-says-shared-understanding-beats-guidance)). Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said inflation is "way too high" but declined to say whether he would support a December increase ([WISH-TV](https://www.wishtv.com/news/business/chicago-fed-president-says-inflation-worries-him-more-than-gas-prices/)). Governor Christopher Waller and Governor Lisa Cook speak today ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2026-november.htm)).

**Pricing.** Fed funds futures price a Fed increase of 24 basis points on 9 December, which corresponds to a probability of about 97 per cent for 25 basis points, and a further 18 basis points by 27 January. CME FedWatch showed a 96.4 per cent chance of a 25 basis point hike "with a small chance of 50bp" ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/wall-street-ends-sharply-lower-213427908.html)). The sell-off in equities has thus not altered the expected December move; the five-year Treasury yield fell 1.8 basis points.

## Data

{bars: Philadelphia Fed survey, November, index | General activity 6.3; Prior month 20.9; Prices paid 58.2; Prices paid, prior 43.2; New orders −0.4 | | Philadelphia Fed}

- **Philadelphia Fed.** The manufacturing index fell to 6.3 in November from 20.9, against about 20 expected, its lowest in six months; new orders fell to −0.4, while prices paid rose to 58.2 from 43.2 ([Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/regional-economic-analysis/mbos-2026-11), [MT Newswires](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/november-philadelphia-fed-regional-manufacturing-index-6-3-vs-20-1-expected-20-9-prior-ce785ed2db8ef123)). Weaker activity combined with higher input costs is the combination the Fed finds hardest to respond to.
- **Jobless claims.** Initial claims rose by 1,000 to 203,000 in the week to 14 November, and continuing claims rose by 70,000 to 1.763 million ([Department of Labor](https://www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf)). The level of initial claims remains low, but the rise in continuing claims suggests that those who lose their jobs are taking longer to find new ones.
- **Japan.** See above. Japanese markets are closed on Monday.

## Assessment

The Fed's December move appears settled in the pricing; the question is the path beyond it. The Philadelphia Fed survey and the claims figures are the first signs in some time that activity may be slowing, while the prices components and the Fed's communication point to continued cost pressure. The fall in equities and in commodity prices, if sustained, tightens financial conditions and lowers headline inflation, both of which reduce the case for a 50 basis point increase. For Japan, inflation close to 2 per cent and unstable markets make December a closer call than the 72 per cent pricing suggests. Next week brings US PCE prices and the second estimate of third-quarter GDP on 25 November.
