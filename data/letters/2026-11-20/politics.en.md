# Politics, Friday 20 November 2026

**The leaders' meeting in Shenzhen produced a pledge to bring more fuel to market but no reference to Ukraine or Iran, and its effect on oil was offset by renewed threats to Gulf supply. In Washington, disclosures about chip exports to China added to the pressure on semiconductor stocks. In Norway, the deadline Labour has set for a budget agreement expires tonight.**

## United States and China

- **Shenzhen.** Presidents Trump, Xi and Putin agreed on bringing more fuel to market, on reform of the United Nations and on reducing nuclear risk; the statement does not mention Ukraine or Iran, and Trump rejected new sanctions on Russia ([AP](https://globalnews.ca/news/12157687/trump-xi-and-putin-reach-agreements-on-oil-and-nuclear-risks)). No volumes were given. *Market channel:* a supply pledge without figures weighs on oil prices only at the margin; Brent settled little changed.
- **Chip exports.** Data released by the House Foreign Affairs Committee show that the Commerce Department approved about 879,000 Nvidia and AMD AI chips for Chinese companies between December 2025 and May 2026; Nvidia said the sales were "subject to strict conditions and limited volumes" ([Reuters](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/19/us-signed-off-on-sales-of-nearly-900000-ai-chips-to-china-in-six-months)). California's attorney general has subpoenaed Nvidia over whether its chips reached China ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). *Market channel:* regulatory risk for the largest AI stocks, which fell 3 to 8 per cent.
- **Energy permitting.** The Senate passed a bipartisan bill to speed approval of pipelines, power lines and other energy infrastructure by 62 votes to 33; its fate in the House is uncertain ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/senate-bipartisan-permitting-reform-bill-energy-baaja-trump/)).
- **Funding.** The stopgap funds the federal government until 11 December ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R49353)); Senate Majority Leader John Thune expects the debt limit to be next year's fight ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/thune-sees-debt-limit-hike-as-next-year-s-fight-semafor-reports)).

## Middle East and energy

- **Saudi Arabia.** A Houthi attack damaged the tower in Riyadh's financial district that houses the sovereign wealth fund; Saudi Arabia reported material damage and no injuries, and Saudi strikes on Sanaa were reported ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/19/saudi-led-coalition-intercepts-houthi-missiles-targeting-riyadh-and-south)). Together with reports of mines in the Strait of Hormuz, this kept a risk premium in oil ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/oil-prices-steady-supply-surplus-024608326.html)).

## Europe

- **EU–UK summit.** Prime Minister Andy Burnham meets EU leaders in Brussels today to conclude agreements on food-trade checks, linking emissions trading and a youth scheme; EU member states approved the package late on Thursday ([AFP](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261120-eu-and-uk-eye-reset-deal-at-burnham-s-brussels-summit)). *Market channel:* lower trade frictions are modestly positive for sterling and UK food exporters.
- **Ukraine.** US envoys have floated NATO membership for Ukraine in exchange for territory in Donbas ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/u-s-has-floated-nato-membership-for-ukraine-in-recent-meetings-d5c8243a)); only the summary could be read.
- **Spain.** Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez faced opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo in a televised debate ahead of the 29 November election ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/spain-s-sanchez-struggles-in-debate-against-main-rival-feijoo)).

## Norway

- **Budget.** Today is the last formal day of negotiations on the 2027 budget between the government and Sp, SV, Rødt and MDG, and some parties are open to continuing until Monday; the disputes concern the Norgespris electricity scheme, tax cuts, fuel taxes and the dental reform ([NTB](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/7P0Jr9/siste-frist-i-dag-dette-er-stridstemaene-i-budsjettforhandlingene)). *Market channel:* the size of the oil-revenue spending is the variable that matters for the krone and for Norges Bank; no figure has been reported.
