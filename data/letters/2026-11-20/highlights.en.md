# Key points, Friday 20 November 2026

**Equities.** The S&P 500 fell 2.02 per cent, its sixth decline in a row, the Nasdaq 2.25 per cent and the Dow 2.19 per cent, after early gains faded; Nvidia fell 3.3 per cent and AMD 7.7 per cent, while Anthropic closed 17.1 per cent above its offer price ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/wall-street-ends-sharply-lower-213427908.html)). STOXX Europe 600 fell 0.88 per cent and Oslo Børs 0.25 per cent, with Subsea 7 up 4.5 per cent ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/8Pm7Kw/flatt-paa-oslo-boers)). This morning the Nikkei is down 4.86 per cent and the KOSPI 5.11 per cent, and Asia outside Japan is heading for its weakest week since March 2020 ([Reuters](https://kfgo.com/2026/11/19/asian-shares-head-for-worst-week-since-2020-as-ai-trade-gloom-fails-to-abate/)).

**Government bonds.** The US 10-year yield fell 1.4 basis points to 5.05 per cent and the 5-year 1.8 basis points to 4.87 per cent, a fifth day of declines ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/treasuries-eye-longest-rally-in-five-months-on-haven-demand)); fed funds futures still price a 25 basis point Fed hike on 9 December with a probability of about 97 per cent. The German 10-year rose 2 basis points to 3.45 per cent, and the Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 1.5 basis points to 4.34 per cent.

**Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.80, EUR/USD 1.0983 and USD/JPY 159.14, the yen firmer as the sell-off reached Asia. The krone weakened: EUR/NOK is 10.715, 0.65 per cent higher, USD/NOK 9.759 and I-44 110.19 at Thursday's fix, most plausibly as a result of lower risk appetite, since oil was little changed.

**Energy.** Brent's January contract settled little changed at 91.98 dollars, as threats to Gulf supply offset a large US stock build ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/oil-prices-steady-supply-surplus-024608326.html)); the quote of 90.05 dollars this morning appears to reflect the next contract month. TTF gas rose 4.5 per cent to 84.46 euros. Gold fell 4.1 per cent to 3,534 dollars on Fed tightening expectations and margin calls: investors with losses on equities had to post more collateral with their brokers and sold gold to raise the cash.

**Drivers.** Fed officials Jefferson, Paulson and Goolsbee kept to the tightening line ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/jefferson20261119a.htm)), while the Philadelphia Fed index fell to 6.3 with prices paid higher ([Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/regional-economic-analysis/mbos-2026-11)); disclosures on chip exports to China weighed on Nvidia and AMD ([Reuters](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/19/us-signed-off-on-sales-of-nearly-900000-ai-chips-to-china-in-six-months)); Japanese core inflation edged up to 2.2 per cent, with a 72 per cent probability of a Bank of Japan hike on 18 December ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/japan-inflation-cpi-october.html)); the Shenzhen summit pledged more fuel supply without figures ([AP](https://globalnews.ca/news/12157687/trump-xi-and-putin-reach-agreements-on-oil-and-nuclear-risks)).

**Norway.** The differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, 1.80 percentage points at twelve months in government rates and about 0.89 percentage point at ten years against Germany. The Expectations Survey shows economists' five-year inflation expectation unchanged at 2.4 per cent and more businesses expecting weaker profitability ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/publications/expectations-survey/2026/q4-2026/)). No new comment on the December decision was found; the economists quoted still expect a hold. Labour's budget deadline expires tonight.
