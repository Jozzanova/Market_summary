# Markets, Friday 20 November 2026

**Anthropic's debut did not halt the sell-off in technology stocks: after early gains, Wall Street fell about 2 per cent, the S&P 500's sixth decline in a row, and the weakness spread to Asia this morning, where the region is heading for its weakest week since March 2020. Unlike earlier in the week, the decline was broad rather than confined to AI, and it was accompanied by lower Treasury yields, a sharp fall in precious metals and a weaker krone; the pricing of the Fed's December increase was unaffected.**

## Equities

**United States.** The S&P 500 fell 2.02 per cent, the Nasdaq Composite 2.25 per cent and the Dow 2.19 per cent ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/wall-street-ends-sharply-lower-213427908.html)). The S&P 500 had been up as much as 1.3 per cent early in the session, and its losing streak is now the longest in about two and a half years, leaving it 8.2 per cent below last month's record ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/2026/11/19/wall-street-looks-set-to-halt-its-losing-streak-after-anthropics-ipo-revives-ai-stocks/)). Decliners outnumbered advancers by more than four to one on the NYSE. Every sector fell; consumer discretionary (−2.79 per cent) and communication services (−2.67 per cent) fell most and energy least (−0.65 per cent). The AI proxies fell somewhat more than the market: SOX 2.42 per cent, NYSE FANG+ 2.49 per cent and the Magnificent Seven ETF 2.69 per cent. Nvidia fell 3.3 per cent, a sixth consecutive decline, after California's attorney general subpoenaed the company over whether its chips reached China ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)); AMD fell 7.7 per cent after a short position was disclosed, CoreWeave 6.1 per cent, while Palo Alto Networks rose 6.4 per cent on higher guidance ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/wall-street-ends-sharply-lower-213427908.html)). The VIX rose 3.9 points to 28.4.

**Anthropic.** The shares, priced at 83 dollars, opened at 111.50 dollars, briefly valuing the company above 2 trillion dollars, and closed 17.1 per cent above the offer price at 97.17 dollars ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/wall-street-ends-sharply-lower-213427908.html), [AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/2026/11/19/wall-street-looks-set-to-halt-its-losing-streak-after-anthropics-ipo-revives-ai-stocks/)). The fade from the opening price appears to have coincided with the turn in the wider market; Gina Martin Adams of HB Wealth pointed instead to the Fed: "we are in a tightening cycle" ([Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/wall-street-ends-sharply-lower-after-anthropic-s-stock-market-debut-ce785ed2de89f427)).

**Europe.** STOXX Europe 600 fell 0.88 per cent, a fourth daily decline and its lowest close since 23 October, led by food and beverages and health care; Novartis fell 3.2 per cent and Sanofi 2.9 per cent, while ASML, STMicroelectronics and Infineon gave up early gains ([Reuters](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/european-shares-extend-losses-anthropic-debut-tests-ai-trade)). The DAX fell 1.06 per cent and the FTSE 100 0.93 per cent.

**Norway.** Oslo Børs fell only 0.25 per cent, as the Norwegian close came before most of the US decline. Subsea 7 rose 4.5 per cent after third-quarter results and higher revenue guidance, while Höegh Autoliners fell 4.0 per cent and Equinor 1.2 per cent; DNB rose 0.8 per cent ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/8Pm7Kw/flatt-paa-oslo-boers)).

**Asia this morning.** The Nikkei is down 4.86 per cent and the KOSPI 5.11 per cent, led by chip and technology stocks; SoftBank has fallen about 16 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/japan-inflation-cpi-october.html)). Taiwan is down about 5 per cent and heading for its largest weekly fall since 1991, and MSCI's Asia-Pacific index outside Japan for its weakest week since March 2020 ([Reuters](https://kfgo.com/2026/11/19/asian-shares-head-for-worst-week-since-2020-as-ai-trade-gloom-fails-to-abate/)). The Hang Seng is down 1.71 per cent and Shanghai 1.57 per cent. US equity futures are 0.2 to 0.6 per cent lower.

## Fixed income and credit

Treasuries gained for a fifth day, the longest run since late June, on demand for safe assets ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/treasuries-eye-longest-rally-in-five-months-on-haven-demand)). The 10-year yield fell 1.4 basis points to 5.05 per cent and the 5-year 1.8 basis points to 4.87 per cent; the moves are small given the size of the equity decline, which suggests that the expected Fed increase limits how far yields can fall. The German 10-year yield rose 2 basis points to 3.45 per cent on Thursday, before the US sell-off, and the Japanese 10-year fell 4.6 basis points to 2.87 per cent. The Norwegian 10-year yield (live) fell 1.5 basis points to 4.34 per cent, while the 1-year rose 1.7 basis points to 4.85 per cent. No verified figure for US credit spreads on Thursday was found.

## Commodities and currencies

**Energy.** Brent's January contract settled 3 cents higher at 91.98 dollars and WTI 5 cents lower at 83.21 dollars, the lowest since August, as reports of mines in the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi strikes on Riyadh offset a large US stock build and the leaders' declaration in Shenzhen ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/oil-prices-steady-supply-surplus-024608326.html)). The front-contract quote of 90.05 dollars this morning appears to reflect the next delivery month rather than a fall of 2 per cent; no report of such a fall was found. TTF gas rose 4.5 per cent to 84.46 euros; no source explains the move.

**Metals.** Gold fell 4.1 per cent to 3,534 dollars, its lowest since September 2025, silver 6.6 per cent and platinum 4.3 per cent, as expectations of Fed tightening, a firm dollar and margin calls from the equity sell-off weighed ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/gold-slides-to-14mth-low-set-for-weekly-drop-amid-fed-hike-bets-5040648)). Lee Hardman of MUFG said "the sharp hawkish repricing of Fed rate hike expectations is contributing (to) the weakness in commodity prices" ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/us-dollar-hovers-near-19-month-high-aussie-falls-soft-jobs/)). Copper fell 2.3 per cent to its lowest since March ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/copper-rout-deepens-as-ai-selloff-wreaks-havoc-in-metals-markets)).

**Currencies.** The dollar index is 103.80, close to its highest since April 2025; EUR/USD is 1.0983. The yen has firmed this morning to 159.14 per dollar as the sell-off spread to Asia ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/yen-outperforms-g-10-peers-as-tech-selloff-fuels-haven-demand)), and the Australian dollar fell 0.7 per cent. The krone weakened: EUR/NOK is 10.715 this morning, 0.65 per cent higher, and USD/NOK 9.759, 0.60 per cent higher, while the import-weighted I-44 (a rise is a weaker krone) was 110.19 at Thursday's fix, up 0.25 per cent. With the oil price little changed, the weakening is most plausibly explained by lower risk appetite, to which the krone is sensitive; no Norwegian source attributes the move.
