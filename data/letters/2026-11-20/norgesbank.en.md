# Norges Bank, Friday 20 November 2026

**The fourth-quarter Expectations Survey shows longer-term inflation expectations broadly unchanged: economists still expect 2.4 per cent five years ahead, while business leaders' and households' expectations remain far above the target. Businesses have become markedly more pessimistic about profitability. No economist comment on the 17 December decision was found, and the krone weakened further as risk appetite fell.**

## New data

{bars: Expectations Survey Q4, inflation five years ahead, per cent | Economists 2.4; Social partners 2.4; Business leaders 5.4; Households 5.1 | | Norges Bank}

**Expectations Survey.** Economists expect inflation of 3.1 per cent twelve months ahead, up 0.1 percentage point, and 2.7 and 2.4 per cent two and five years ahead, both unchanged; the social partners expect 2.6 and 2.4 per cent. Business leaders expect 4.6 and 5.4 per cent, unchanged, and households 4.6 per cent in two to three years and 5.1 per cent in five years, up 0.3 percentage point. Expected wage growth for 2026 is 4.3 per cent among economists and 4.5 per cent among the social partners, up 0.1, while business leaders lowered their own-company figure by 0.2 to 4.1 per cent. The share of business leaders expecting weaker profitability rose 7.6 percentage points to 37.3 per cent, and 62.8 per cent of households expect higher interest rates ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/publications/expectations-survey/2026/q4-2026/)).

*Reading.* Stable expectations among economists and the social partners, despite inflation above 3 per cent, suggest that the anchor holds among those closest to wage formation. The rise in households' five-year expectations and the slight increase in wage expectations point the other way. Weaker expected profitability and the 30 per cent of businesses expecting fewer employees suggest that activity is softening, which on balance supports the view that no further hike is required, although the survey offers little that settles the December decision.

## What others say

- **On the survey.** Kjetil Olsen, chief economist at Nordea, said: "Ved å holde renta høy bidrar det til å dempe aktiviteten i norsk økonomi"; Nejra Macic, chief economist at NHO, pointed to the third of businesses expecting weaker profitability ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/rogaland/norges-bank-med-ny-forventningsundersokelse_-stangeland-maskin-frykter-inflasjonen-ikke-vil-bremse-1.18084262)). E24 noted that only the economists expect unchanged wage growth ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/aGwy9O/de-fleste-venter-naa-noe-hoeyere-loennsvekst)); DN described the changes in inflation expectations as small ([DN](https://www.dn.no/makrookonomi/norges-bank/makrookonomi/norges-bank-undersokelse-sma-endringer-i-inflasjonsforventningene/2-1-2076502)).
- **On the rate path.** No new comment from bank economists on the 17 December decision was found. The standing views are unchanged: Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects the rate to be held in December, and Harald Magnus Andreassen of SpareBank 1 Markets expects no hike this year or next ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)). None of the economists quoted expects a hike in December.
- **Market pricing.** No new figure was found; the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.715 this morning, 0.65 per cent higher than at the previous close, and USD/NOK 9.759, 0.60 per cent higher; I-44 was 110.19 at Thursday's fix, up 0.25 per cent (a rise is a weaker krone). The krone has weakened 1.4 per cent against the euro over the past week.

**Why.** Brent was little changed on Thursday, so the terms of trade do not explain the move. The more likely driver is lower risk appetite: the krone tends to weaken when global equities fall, and the decline deepened after the Norwegian close. No Norwegian source attributes the move.

**Differentials.** Against the euro area the differential is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate and 1.80 percentage points at twelve months in government rates (the stand-in for the swap differential), unchanged. The Norwegian 10-year yield (live) fell 1.5 basis points to 4.34 per cent while the German 10-year rose 2 basis points, so the long differential narrowed to about 0.89 percentage point against Germany. The differentials have changed too little to explain the weakening.
