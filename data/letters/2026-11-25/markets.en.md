# Markets, Wednesday 25 November 2026

**Monday's recovery in AI shares did not hold: semiconductors gave back most of their gain on Tuesday and the selling has spread to Seoul, Tokyo and Taipei this morning, while shares outside technology rose and the Dow gained. Reports that Washington is about to announce new tariffs on China, the European Union and fourteen other economies added to the caution. Government bond yields edged down, oil recovered on new attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, and the krone weakened slightly.**

## Equities

- **United States: a rotation out of AI rather than a broad decline.** The S&P 500 fell 0.50 per cent, its eighth decline in nine sessions, and the Nasdaq Composite 1.48 per cent, while the Dow rose 0.85 per cent and most stocks in the S&P 500 rose ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/market-outlook/2026/11/24/tech-stocks-wane-a-day-after-a-huang-rally-as-anthropic-tumbles-below-its-initial-offering-price/)). Health care rose 2.95 per cent and consumer staples 1.66 per cent, while technology fell 3.26 per cent. The VIX changed little, falling 0.2 points to 26.4. Dollar Tree rose 11.2 per cent and Kohl's 19.5 per cent after results above expectations.
- **AI and semiconductors: Monday's gain largely reversed.** The semiconductor index SOX fell 7.88 per cent, after rising 14.03 per cent on Monday; NYSE FANG+ fell 1.75 per cent and the Magnificent Seven ETF 0.92 per cent. Nvidia fell 5.9 per cent, CoreWeave 11.8 per cent and GE Vernova 4.8 per cent, and Anthropic fell 6.5 per cent to below its offering price of last week ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/market-outlook/2026/11/24/tech-stocks-wane-a-day-after-a-huang-rally-as-anthropic-tumbles-below-its-initial-offering-price/)). AP attributes the reversal to doubts that AI investment will earn enough to justify the spending on data centres, high interest rates, which raise the cost of borrowing for that spending, and the prospect of tighter regulation after the Democratic gains in the midterm elections. Reports of new tariffs weighed on chip and data-centre shares in particular ([AP](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/11/24/us-ready-to-slap-tariffs-on-china-eu-and-others-over-subsidized-production-ap-sources-say/)).
- **Europe and Norway lower.** STOXX Europe 600 fell 0.66 per cent after its strongest day since early May, the DAX 1.26 per cent and the FTSE 100 0.13 per cent. Technology fell nearly 3 per cent: Aixtron fell 16.3 per cent after a downgrade by UBS, Infineon more than 9 per cent and ASM International more than 8 per cent, while miners fell almost 5 per cent as metal prices fell. Sanofi rose 9.7 per cent after agreeing to buy the US drugmaker Insmed for 41 billion dollars, and health care rose nearly 2 per cent ([Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/world-indices/articles/europes-stoxx-600-subdued-ai-082727732.html)). Oslo Børs fell 0.87 per cent and closed at the day's low; Nordic Semiconductor fell 6.7 per cent with the US chip makers ([NTB](https://www.adressa.no/nyheter/innenriks/i/KL3x8E/oslo-boers-aapnet-opp-0-35)), and Tomra fell 8.8 per cent after lowering its long-term margin target at its capital markets day to 16–18 per cent for 2030–32, against an earlier ambition of 18 per cent by 2030 ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/3BJ8jX/tomra-faller-paa-oslo-boers)).
- **Asia this morning: chip makers lead the decline.** KOSPI is down 6.30 per cent and the Nikkei 225 2.86 per cent; Samsung Electronics is down 9.1 per cent, SK hynix 8.6 per cent and SoftBank Group 16.1 per cent, and Taiwan's index is down more than 6 per cent with TSMC 8.1 per cent lower ([AP](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/asian-stocks-mostly-decline-ai-040526833.html)). The Hang Seng is down 0.40 per cent and the Shanghai Composite 1.21 per cent. US equity futures point lower. The Korean and Japanese markets had risen most on Tuesday morning, so the reversal is largest there.

## Fixed income and credit

- **Yields edged down.** The US 10-year yield fell 1.4 basis points to 4.90 per cent and the 5-year 1.6 basis points to 4.71 per cent. A slightly soft 5-year auction and a stronger-than-expected Conference Board consumer confidence index, which rose 11.4 points to 103.3, did not move yields much ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html)). Fed funds futures imply an increase of 23.9 basis points at the Fed meeting on 9 December, against 25.6 basis points on Tuesday morning; a quarter-point Fed hike thus remains almost fully priced.
- **Europe, Norway and Japan.** The German 10-year yield fell 4 basis points to 3.33 per cent and the 2-year 2 basis points to 3.06 per cent. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 6.5 basis points to 4.16 per cent and the 5-year 8 basis points to 4.27 per cent, more than in Germany. The Japanese 10-year fell 5.7 basis points to 2.78 per cent. In the United States, high-yield spreads at Monday's fix had narrowed 13 basis points to 3.63 percentage points.

## Commodities and currencies

- **Oil recovered on Tuesday.** The January Brent contract rose 2.3 per cent to 91.40 dollars on Tuesday after reports of further attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, although thin trading before Thanksgiving amplified the move; signs of oversupply, including the IEA's expectation of a surplus this quarter, limited the gain ([Bloomberg](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/oil_climbs_despite_growing_oversupply_fears-24-nov-2026-185160-article/)). The front-month price series in the brief shows 87.49 dollars this morning; the move overnight could not be verified against a news source, and part of the gap may reflect a change of contract month. TTF gas rose 2.15 per cent to 91.67 euros.
- **Metals fell.** Gold is 3,549 dollars this morning, down 5.2 per cent over the week, and aluminium fell 4.32 per cent. Copper is up 1.58 per cent this morning after Tuesday's decline.
- **Currencies.** EUR/USD is 1.0947, up 0.36 per cent, the dollar index 103.99 and USD/JPY 159.37. The krone weakened slightly: EUR/NOK rose 0.32 per cent to 10.761 and USD/NOK is 9.834, little changed, while I-44 rose 0.05 per cent to 111.04 at Tuesday's fix (a rise is a weaker krone). Lower equity prices in Asia this morning and weaker risk appetite are the likely reason; no source attributes the move.
