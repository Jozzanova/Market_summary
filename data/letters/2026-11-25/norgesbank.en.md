# Norges Bank, Wednesday 25 November 2026

**Household credit growth was unchanged in October, and no new comments by economists on the 17 December decision were found; the standing view among those quoted is a hold. The krone weakened a little further as risk appetite declined, while Norwegian long-term yields fell more than German yields. The labour force survey is published this morning, and the national accounts for the third quarter follow on Thursday.**

## New data

- **Credit growth stable.** Twelve-month growth in household debt was 4.5 per cent at the end of October, unchanged from September, and total domestic debt growth for the public (K2) was 4.9 per cent, also unchanged; growth in debt for non-financial corporations rose to 6.1 per cent from 5.9 per cent ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/bank-og-finansmarked/finansielle-indikatorer/statistikk/kredittindikator/artikler/innenlandsk-gjeld)). Household credit growth thus remains moderate despite the higher policy rate, which suggests that the housing market has not weakened markedly; the pickup in corporate borrowing is consistent with continued investment demand.
- **Today and the rest of the week.** Statistics Norway publishes the labour force survey at 08:00; the last reading was an unemployment rate of 4.6 per cent (trend, September), unchanged from August ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/sysselsetting/statistikk/arbeidskraftundersokelsen)). The national accounts for the third quarter follow on Thursday, after mainland GDP growth of 0.3 per cent in the second quarter ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/nasjonalregnskap-og-konjunkturer/nasjonalregnskap/statistikk/nasjonalregnskap)), and retail trade and registered unemployment on Friday. No consensus figures for these releases have been reported.

## What others say

- **On the rate path.** No economist published a new view on Norges Bank in the past 24 hours, and no new market pricing was reported. The views on file are unchanged: Kyrre M. Knudsen, chief economist at SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge, expects the policy rate to be kept unchanged in December with a signal that the peak has been reached ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)); Frank Jullum of Danske Bank believes "toppen er nådd" but noted that an inflation figure three tenths above expectations would make a December hike "helt klart aktuelt igjen" ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/eG3QBa/tror-rentetoppen-er-naadd-balanserer-paa-en-knivsegg)); Kjersti Haugland of DNB Carnegie calls the krone "en joker" that could force a hike if it weakens markedly ([Dagbladet Børsen](https://borsen.dagbladet.no/nyheter/en-joker-kan-avgjore/85377056)). JP Morgan expects a further hike in the first quarter. None of the economists quoted expects a hike in December. The last reported market reading is about a 35 per cent probability of a Norges Bank hike on 17 December (Handelsbanken, 5 November).
- **On the krone.** Nettavisen notes that USD/NOK has risen from about 9.19 at the start of November to about 9.83, as the dollar strengthened on the pricing of a Fed hike in December; ING's Chris Turner said "dollaren har kommet langt på kort tid" ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/dollaren-pa-sitt-sterkeste-pa-20-maneder-dette-betyr-det-for-norske-kroner/s/80-95-277220)). Haugland's remark is relevant here: the krone has weakened about 1.7 per cent against the euro over the past week.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK is 10.761 this morning, up 0.32 per cent, and USD/NOK 9.834, little changed; I-44 rose 0.05 per cent at Tuesday's fix to 111.04 (a rise is a weaker krone), about 1.3 per cent higher than a week ago.

**Why.** The weakening is consistent with lower risk appetite: equities fell in Europe and Asia, and in such periods investors tend to reduce positions in smaller currencies such as the krone. Oil rose on Tuesday, which would normally support the krone, so it does not explain the move. No source attributes the move explicitly.

**Differentials.** The policy rate differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points. The 12-month differential in government rates was 1.84 percentage points at Monday's fix, little changed over the week, so the short differential has not moved against the krone. The Norwegian 10-year (live) fell 6.5 basis points on Tuesday to 4.16 per cent, against 4 basis points in Germany, so the long differential narrowed to about 0.83 percentage point. The Norwegian 10-year has fallen 24 basis points over the week, more than the German.
