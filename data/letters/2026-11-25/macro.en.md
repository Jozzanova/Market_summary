# Macro, Wednesday 25 November 2026

**Inflation figures from Australia and Japan this morning were higher than expected, and ECB Governing Council member Olaf Sleijpen said that the direction for December is clear, so the case for further tightening outside the United States has strengthened. In the United States, a strong rise in consumer confidence did little to move the market, which still prices a Fed hike on 9 December almost fully ahead of today's PCE figures.**

## Federal Reserve

- **Pricing almost unchanged.** Fed funds futures imply an increase of 23.9 basis points on 9 December, a probability of about 96 per cent of a quarter-point Fed hike, and a further 17 basis points by 27 January; on Tuesday morning the December figure was 25.6 basis points. CME FedWatch showed a probability of 100 per cent on Tuesday ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html)). The small decline reflects slightly lower yields as equities fell, not new information on inflation.
- **Williams.** New York Fed President John Williams said that "there is still work to do" to return inflation to 2 per cent, and that it should be done "over time" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/john-williams-fed-inflation-rate-hikes.html)). He did not rule out hikes at consecutive meetings.
- **Today.** The PCE price index for October, the Fed's preferred measure, is expected to rise 0.3 per cent on the month and 3.8 per cent over twelve months, up from 3.6 per cent in September ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html)); core PCE rose 3.1 per cent in September. The second estimate of third-quarter GDP and the Beige Book follow. A figure in line with expectations would confirm the case for a December hike; the pricing beyond December is more sensitive to the core measure.

## Other central banks

- **ECB: December looks settled, the path after it is open.** Dutch central bank Governor Olaf Sleijpen said "the direction is clear" for December but that the ECB takes "a meeting-by-meeting approach" and that the path after December is uncertain; he also said that France's fiscal problems "need to be fixed in Paris primarily" ([Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/ecb-policy-direction-clear-december-150810000.html)). Reuters names Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel among several policymakers who have said the ECB will likely need to tighten next month. The Governing Council discusses banks' minimum reserve requirement today; a higher reserve requirement means that more of the banks' deposits at the central bank earn no interest, which lowers the Eurosystem's interest expense and tightens liquidity at the margin. Germany's 2-year yield fell 2 basis points to 3.06 per cent, so the comments added nothing to what the market already priced.
- **Reserve Bank of Australia.** Consumer prices rose 4.9 per cent in the twelve months to October, up from 4.3 per cent, and the trimmed mean rose 3.8 per cent, up from 3.6 per cent ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/consumer-price-index-australia/latest-release)). Electricity prices rose 25.2 per cent as federal rebates ended, and fuel rose 32.5 per cent. The rise in the trimmed mean shows that the increase is not only energy; it weakens the case for the RBA cut that money markets had begun to price for 2027 after unemployment rose to 4.9 per cent.
- **Bank of Japan.** The services producer price index rose 4.2 per cent in the twelve months to October, the fastest pace since 1990, driven by ocean freight, leasing and hotel charges; prices in labour-intensive services rose a slower 2.7 per cent. Most economists polled by Reuters expect a BoJ hike in December ([Reuters](https://wkzo.com/2026/11/24/japans-corporate-services-inflation-spikes-in-october/)). The Japanese 10-year yield nevertheless fell 5.7 basis points to 2.78 per cent on Tuesday as global yields declined.

- **US consumers.** The Conference Board index rose 11.4 points to 103.3, against 92.2 expected ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html)); confidence rose among Democrats and independents ([AP](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/market-outlook/2026/11/24/tech-stocks-wane-a-day-after-a-huang-rally-as-anthropic-tumbles-below-its-initial-offering-price/)). Lower petrol prices since October are a likely reason. A firmer household sector reduces the risk that a December Fed hike would weaken demand more than intended.

## Assessment

The balance of risks still points to higher policy rates in most advanced economies. Inflation outside energy is rising in Australia and Japan, and ECB speakers continue to argue for a hike in December, which suggests that lower oil prices alone are unlikely to bring inflation back to target. Against this stand the fall in AI shares, which weighs on wealth and investment in technology, and new US tariffs, which would raise US prices in the short run but dampen global trade. Today's PCE figures are the last major US inflation reading before the Fed meeting.
