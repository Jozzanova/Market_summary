# Key points, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Markets.** Equities rallied broadly on Monday: the S&P 500 rose 2.35 per cent to 7,550, the Nasdaq 2.74 per cent, STOXX Europe 600 1.3 per cent and Oslo Børs 1.27 per cent, and overnight the KOSPI (+3.8 per cent) and the Nikkei (+2.6 per cent) followed. The semiconductor index SOX gained 6.9 per cent but is still 20 per cent below its level a month ago. Government yields were little changed: the US 10-year at 4.86 per cent, the euro-area AAA 10-year at 3.29 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year at 4.29 per cent. The dollar index is 101.7, EUR/USD 1.123 and USD/JPY around 158. Brent is 91.4 dollars, 12.7 per cent lower than a week ago.

**Drivers.** Oil fell about 6 dollars after President Trump said new strikes on Iran are "very unlikely", which eased inflation worries and lifted risk appetite; Tehran denies that talks are under way and the Strait of Hormuz remains closed. Semiconductors recovered part of last week's fall (AMD +13 per cent, Broadcom +9 per cent), while Nvidia fell 4 per cent after its board opened an inquiry concerning the chief executive and Trump said the FBI is "looking at" the matter. No central bank changed rates and the Fed, the ECB and the Bank of England were silent; the Bank of Japan's summary of opinions from the October meeting points to a December hike, the RBNZ calls inflation expectations "worrying", and Australian consumer confidence fell after the RBA's fifth hike. China's exports rose 35 per cent in October; Japanese household spending fell 6.8 per cent. Today: US CPI at 14:30 CET, the last print before the Fed meets on 9 December.

**Norway.** October CPI came in at 3.6 per cent, a notch below the 3.7 consensus, and CPI-ATE at 2.7 per cent, as expected and as Norges Bank had assumed; electricity pushed up, food and imported goods down. Economists at Handelsbanken, SpareBank 1 Markets and SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge all expect an unchanged rate in December, and several say the peak has been reached; none of the economists quoted expects a hike, and before the print the market priced about a 35 per cent chance of one. The krone weakened all the same: EUR/NOK is 10.62, 0.8 per cent higher than on Monday and 2.0 per cent on the week, with Brent below 90 dollars. The rate differential against the euro is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate and about 1.0 at ten years. The Financial Stability Report comes on Wednesday.
