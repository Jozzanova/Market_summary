# Key points, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Markets.** Equities rose broadly on Monday. The S&P 500 gained 2.35 per cent to 7,550, the Nasdaq 2.74 per cent, STOXX Europe 600 1.3 per cent and Oslo Børs 1.27 per cent, and in Asia the KOSPI (+4.4 per cent) and the Nikkei (+2.9 per cent) followed. The semiconductor index SOX rose 6.9 per cent but remains 20 per cent below its level a month ago. Government yields were little changed: the US 10-year at 4.86 per cent, the euro-area AAA 10-year at 3.29 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year at 4.29 per cent. The dollar index is 101.7, EUR/USD 1.123 and USD/JPY about 158. Brent is 91.4 dollars, 12.7 per cent lower than a week ago.

**Drivers.** The rise in equities came with no change in yields. That points to lower oil and buying back after last week's fall rather than to a shift in rate expectations. Oil fell about 6 dollars after President Trump said new strikes on Iran are "very unlikely". The fall reflects a lower risk premium, not better supply: Tehran denies that talks are under way and the Strait of Hormuz remains closed. Semiconductors recovered part of last week's losses (AMD +13 per cent, Broadcom +9 per cent), while Nvidia fell 4 per cent after its board opened an inquiry into the chief executive and Trump said the FBI is "looking into" the matter. No central bank changed rates, and the Fed, the ECB and the Bank of England were silent. The Bank of Japan's summary of the October meeting shows a majority seeing a hike as necessary in the near term, the RBNZ calls inflation expectations "worrying", and Australian consumer confidence is falling after the fifth hike of the year. China's exports rose 35 per cent in October, while Japanese household spending fell 6.8 per cent. US CPI at 14:30 CET is the last print before the Fed meets on 9 December.

**Norway.** Inflation in October was 3.6 per cent, a notch below the 3.7 consensus, and CPI-ATE 2.7 per cent, as expected and in line with Norges Bank's projection. Electricity prices pulled up, food and imported goods pulled down. Economists at Handelsbanken, SpareBank 1 Markets and SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge all expect an unchanged rate in December, and several judge that the peak has been reached. None of those quoted expects a hike; before the print the market priced about a 35 per cent probability of one. The krone weakened all the same: EUR/NOK is 10.62, 0.8 per cent higher than on Monday and 2.0 per cent on the week, with Brent below 90 dollars. The weakening follows the oil price, not rate expectations; the differential against the euro area is unchanged at 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate and about 1.0 point at ten years. The Financial Stability Report comes on Wednesday.
