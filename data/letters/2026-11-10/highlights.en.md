# Key points, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Markets.** Equities rallied broadly on Monday: the S&P 500 rose 2.35 per cent to 7,550, the Nasdaq 2.74 per cent, STOXX Europe 600 1.3 per cent and Oslo Børs 1.27 per cent, and overnight the KOSPI (+3.8 per cent) and the Nikkei (+2.6 per cent) followed. The semiconductor index SOX gained 6.9 per cent but is still 20 per cent below its level a month ago. Government yields were little changed: the US 10-year at 4.86 per cent, the euro-area AAA 10-year at 3.29 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year at 4.29 per cent. The dollar index is 101.7, EUR/USD 1.123 and USD/JPY around 158. Brent is 91.4 dollars, 12.7 per cent lower than a week ago.

**Drivers.** Oil fell about 6 dollars after President Trump said new strikes on Iran are "very unlikely", which eased inflation worries and lifted risk appetite; Tehran denies that talks are under way and the Strait of Hormuz remains closed. Semiconductors recovered part of last week's fall (AMD +13 per cent, Broadcom +9 per cent), while Nvidia fell 4 per cent after its board opened an inquiry concerning the chief executive and Trump said the FBI is "looking at" the matter. No central bank changed rates and the Fed, the ECB and the Bank of England were silent; the Bank of Japan's summary of opinions from the October meeting points to a December hike, the RBNZ calls inflation expectations "worrying", and Australian consumer confidence fell after the RBA's fifth hike. China's exports rose 35 per cent in October; Japanese household spending fell 6.8 per cent. Today: CPI in Norway (08:00) and the US (14:30 CET), the last print before the Fed meets on 9 December.

**Norway.** EUR/NOK is 10.55, unchanged from Monday evening but 1.3 per cent higher than a week ago; the I-44 was 108.81, 1.5 per cent weaker on the week but 9.2 per cent stronger this year. The krone did not follow equities up, probably because of the drop in oil. The rate differential against the euro is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate and about 1.0 point at ten years against the AAA curve, and the market is not pricing a tighter path. Norges Bank has said nothing new since it held the rate at 4.50 per cent on 5 November, ready to raise if needed; consensus for today's CPI is 3.7 per cent headline and 2.7 per cent for CPI-ATE, and the Financial Stability Report comes on Wednesday.
