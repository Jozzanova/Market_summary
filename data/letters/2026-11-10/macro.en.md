# Macro, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Summary.** No central bank changed policy in the past 24 hours, and the policy-rate table is unchanged. The Bank of Japan's summary of opinions from the October meeting shows that many board members judge a hike to be necessary in the near term. That strengthens expectations of a move to 1.5 per cent in December, even though Japanese household spending fell 6.8 per cent on the year in September. China's exports rose 35 per cent in October, and its trade surplus for the year to date passed one trillion dollars. The day's most important data are consumer prices in Norway and the United States; the US figure is the last before the Federal Reserve meets on 9 December.

- **Federal Reserve.** No new policy communication on Monday. Expectations of a December hike remain in place after the strong October employment report ([Reuters](http://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-11-09/wall-st-jumps-back-oil-slide-eases-inflation-worries-nvidia-slumps)), and Goldman Sachs cut its gold forecast for 2026 to 4,150 dollars, citing a more restrictive Fed ([Quartz](https://qz.com/goldman-sachs-lowers-gold-forecast-fed-hawkish-110626)). Today's CPI is expected to show +0.3 per cent on the month, down from +0.6 per cent in September, and +0.2 per cent for the core. A softer print would temper hike expectations, but the absence of twelve-month rates makes the figure harder to read, for the market and for the Fed alike ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/bls-will-not-publish-12-month-cpi-core-cpi-figures-for-october), [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm)). The 10-year breakeven inflation rate has fallen to 2.22 per cent, 7 basis points lower over the week, in line with the fall in oil.
- **Bank of Japan.** The summary shows that many members judge a hike to be necessary in the near term ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/boj-summary-suggests-good-chance-of-near-term-interest-rate-hike)). One member put the neutral rate "considerably above 1 percent", while more cautious members pointed to weak consumption ([Bank of Japan](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmsche_minu/opinion_2026/opi261030.pdf)). The October decision was 8–1, with Hajime Takata voting for 1.5 per cent ([Bank of Japan](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k261030a.pdf)). Read together, this indicates that the board regards the current rate as still accommodative and intends to continue normalising, with weak consumption governing the pace rather than the direction.
- **Asia-Pacific.** Reserve Bank of New Zealand Governor Conway described inflation expectations as "worrying" ahead of the December decision ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/new-zealand-inflation-expectations-worrying-rbnz-s-conway-says)). Australian consumer confidence fell further in November after the RBA's fifth hike this year ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/australia-s-consumer-confidence-slumps-on-cost-of-living-fears)); Westpac expects a pause in December.

## Data of note

- **China.** Exports rose 35.2 per cent on the year in October and imports 41.6 per cent, both stronger than expected. The monthly surplus narrowed to about 105 billion dollars, while the surplus for January–October passed one trillion dollars, a month earlier than last year ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/china-trade-october-exports-imports-surplus-.html), [AP](https://www.ksat.com/news/2026/11/10/chinas-trade-surplus-surpasses-1-trillion-for-a-2nd-straight-year-as-exports-surge-35/), [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/a267d321-a489-4426-940e-1e4be712dc09)). Part of the growth reflects a weak base a year ago, but the size of the surplus makes it a trade-policy issue ahead of the APEC meeting. Crude imports were the highest since March as more oil flowed from the Gulf ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/china-oil-imports-rebound-as-more-crude-flows-from-persian-gulf)).
- **Japan.** Household spending fell 6.8 per cent on the year in September, against an expected 3.1 per cent, the tenth consecutive decline, and 2.8 per cent on the month ([Reuters](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/japan-september-household-spending-slumps-000421366.html)). The ministry points to poor weather, but ten falls in a row suggest that real wage growth is not yet carrying consumption.
- **Norway.** CPI for October is published at 08:00; consensus is 3.7 per cent for CPI and 2.7 per cent for CPI-ATE, against 3.4 and 3.0 per cent in September. Higher headline and lower core inflation would in that case reflect energy prices, not domestic price pressure. Norwegian producer prices for October, due Monday, could not be verified.

## Assessment

The balance of risks has shifted somewhat towards lower inflation over the past week. Brent is almost 13 per cent lower, US breakevens have fallen, and Treasury yields are about 40 basis points below their level a month ago. That suggests the market expects a limited tightening in the United States. Against this, the central banks of Japan, Australia and New Zealand continue to express concern about inflation expectations, and the shortage in the oil market is unresolved: Iran keeps the Strait of Hormuz closed until its conditions are met, and Gulf exports remain well below pre-war levels. The fall in oil therefore reflects a lower probability of escalation, not better supply, and could reverse quickly. Weak consumption in Japan and Australia shows the cost of tightening to households and limits how far those central banks can go. Today's US CPI will decide whether a December hike remains the central case; without twelve-month rates, the monthly change and the core will carry the whole interpretation.
