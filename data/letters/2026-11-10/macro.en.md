# Macro letter, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Summary.** No central bank changed policy in the past 24 hours, and the policy-rate table is unchanged. The Bank of Japan's summary of opinions from its October meeting showed that many board members see a near-term hike as necessary, strengthening expectations of a move to 1.5 per cent in December, even as Japanese household spending fell 6.8 per cent on the year. China's exports rose 35 per cent in October and its trade surplus for the year passed one trillion dollars. The day's decisive data are consumer prices in Norway and the United States; the US figure is the last before the Federal Reserve meets on 9 December.

## Assessment

The balance of risks has shifted slightly towards lower inflation over the past week. Brent is almost 13 per cent lower, US breakevens have fallen and Treasury yields are about 40 basis points below their level a month ago, which suggests that markets see a limited tightening cycle in the United States. Against that, central banks in Japan, Australia and New Zealand continue to signal concern about inflation expectations, and the oil supply squeeze has not been resolved: Iran says Hormuz stays closed until its conditions are met, and Gulf exports remain well below pre-war levels. Weak consumption in Japan and Australia shows the cost of tightening on households. Today's US CPI will determine whether the December hike remains the central case.
