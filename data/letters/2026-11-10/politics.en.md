# Politics, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Summary.** The Iran conflict remained the main political driver of markets. President Trump said after a call with Qatar's prime minister that renewed military action looks unlikely, which pulled oil lower, but Tehran denied that talks are under way and repeated that the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until its conditions are met. In Washington, the Nvidia affair has become a political issue, and Democrats are considering using the 11 December funding deadline to constrain the war. China's trade surplus passed one trillion dollars ahead of an expected Trump–Xi meeting next week.

## Middle East and energy

Trump said he sees "positive momentum" towards ending the conflict and that renewed US military action is unlikely, adding that Gulf states would prefer to keep the US naval blockade in place. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said no negotiations are taking place and that the US must meet the seven conditions set out in June's interim agreement before the strait reopens ([Reuters](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/irans-top-security-official-says-165926144.html)). Secretary of State Rubio called Tehran's demands unacceptable, and attacks continued, including a fire on a Saudi east-west pipeline on Monday ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/0B5rAG/braatt-fall-i-oljeprisen)). US refuelling aircraft were moved from a second UK base because of an Iranian threat ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/u-s-aircraft-evacuated-from-second-u-k-base-due-to-iran-threat-835a8b58)). The market channel is the oil price. Brent has fallen to 91.4 dollars because the probability of escalation is judged lower, but the two sides' positions remain far apart, and as long as the strait is closed and the blockade stands, physical supply is unchanged. That limits the scope for a lasting fall in price.

## United States

- **Nvidia.** Trump said the FBI is "looking into" chief executive Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/cbabf2da-2079-4b69-88e3-0ba84cf8d6fd)), and members of the House China committee have asked for answers. The channel is export controls on chips to China, which Huang has lobbied to loosen ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/how-jensen-huangs-affair-spiraled-into-a-national-security-nightmare-d9b43304)). The affair raises the likelihood of tighter controls, with consequences for the semiconductor supply chain in Asia.
- **Congress.** Democrats are discussing restrictions on Iran war funding in the bill needed by 11 December to avoid a shutdown ([Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/11/as-trump-renews-iran-nuke-threats-dems-ponder-blocking-war-funds/)). Republican representative Thomas Massie introduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Hegseth, whom Trump defended ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-11-10/hegseth-hits-out-at-critics-as-massie-pushes-impeachment-video)). The funding deadline is the first point at which the war can meet real resistance in Congress, and at the same time a new source of shutdown risk.

## Asia

China's surplus passed one trillion dollars for January–October, which the FT judges likely to sharpen tensions with trading partners ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/a267d321-a489-4426-940e-1e4be712dc09)). Xi and Trump are expected to meet at the APEC summit in Shenzhen next week ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3373969/xi-and-trump-set-apec-talks-major-deliverables-expected-wait-g20)). WuXi AppTec was removed from the Pentagon's list of Chinese military companies ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/wuxi-apptec-removed-from-pentagon-s-chinese-military-firms-list)), a small but concrete sign that both sides want calm before the meeting.
