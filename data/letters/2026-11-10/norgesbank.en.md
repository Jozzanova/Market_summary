# Norges Bank letter, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Summary.** There was no new communication from Norges Bank on Monday; the latest items remain last Thursday's decision to keep the policy rate at 4.50 per cent and the speeches that day. Today's October CPI is the first test of that decision: economists expect CPI-ATE to ease to 2.7 per cent from 3.0 per cent, while headline inflation rises to 3.7 per cent. The Financial Stability Report follows on Wednesday. The standing view is a hold with a readiness to hike if needed.

## What Norges Bank said

Nothing new since 5 November ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/news/)). The Committee's message stands: it "will likely be necessary to keep the policy rate elevated for a time", and the Committee is "prepared to raise the policy rate if needed" ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/topics/monetary-policy/Monetary-policy-meetings/2026/november-2026/)).

## What economists and the market say

- The Bloomberg consensus for October is 3.7 per cent for CPI and 2.7 per cent for CPI-ATE ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/qOnMOg/venter-paa-signaler-kan-snu-opp-ned-paa-hele-regnestykket)). Harald Magnus Andreassen of Sparebank 1 Markets said the figures would have to surprise well on the upside to matter, and he does not expect a hike in 2026 (same source).
- Ahead of last week's meeting, Danske Bank's Frank Jullum said he believes the peak has been reached but that even a small upside surprise in inflation would make a December hike "clearly relevant", while JP Morgan's Allan Monks expects one more hike in the first quarter ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/eG3QBa/tror-rentetoppen-er-naadd-balanserer-paa-en-knivsegg)). SEB's Erica Dalstø reads the bank's guidance as readiness to act rather than a plan to hike (same source).
- No new comments on the rate path from DNB Carnegie, Nordea, Handelsbanken, Swedbank or the international banks were found for Monday.

## The krone and rates

EUR/NOK is at 10.55 this morning, unchanged from Monday evening but 1.3 per cent higher over the week, and the I-44 index stood at 108.81 on Monday, 1.5 per cent weaker over the week. The krone did not follow equities higher on Monday, most plausibly because Brent fell about 6 dollars, though no source has attributed the move. A weaker krone works against the disinflation Norges Bank expects, but the index is still 9.2 per cent stronger this year. At Friday's fixes the 3-year government yield was 4.60 per cent and the 10-year 4.29 per cent, both lower over the week; the market is not pricing a firmer path.
