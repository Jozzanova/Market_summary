# Norges Bank letter, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Summary.** October CPI came in at 3.6 per cent, a notch below the 3.7 consensus, and CPI-ATE fell to 2.7 per cent from 3.0, exactly as expected and as Norges Bank had assumed. Economists read the print as the end of the hike debate: Handelsbanken, SpareBank 1 Markets and SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge all expect an unchanged rate in December, and several say the peak has been reached. The krone nevertheless weakened through the day, to 10.62 against the euro, with Brent below 90 dollars.

## New data

- October CPI: 3.6 per cent on twelve months (3.4 in September) and 0.3 per cent on the month. CPI-ATE 2.7 per cent (3.0), also 0.3 per cent on the month. Consensus was 3.7 and 2.7 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/priser-og-prisindekser/konsumpriser/statistikk/konsumprisindeksen)).
- Electricity pushed the headline up: electricity including grid fees fell 0.4 per cent from September, against 8.3 per cent in October last year when the Norgespris scheme and a lower electricity tax kicked in. Food and non-alcoholic drinks fell 0.4 per cent on the month, the third fall in a row, and are 1.4 per cent higher than a year ago; imported goods are 0.7 per cent higher. Goods 2.8 per cent, services 4.1 per cent ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/priser-og-prisindekser/konsumpriser/statistikk/konsumprisindeksen), [E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).
- The drop in core inflation is partly a base effect: the strong monthly rise of October 2025 fell out of the twelve-month rate ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).

## What others say

- Marius Gonsholt Hov, Handelsbanken: the print "does not change rate expectations now". It "clearly looks like" an unchanged rate for the rest of the year, and a hike before Christmas would have needed "a fairly strong upside" in today's figure and in the November one ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).
- Harald Magnus Andreassen, SpareBank 1 Markets: lower than "we and the market expected", but as Norges Bank expected; inflation is now on the path the bank assumed. He sees neither a hike in December nor next year ([Frifagbevegelse/NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)).
- Kyrre M. Knudsen, SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge: "This is good news". He expects an unchanged rate in December and a signal that the peak has been reached; a stronger krone and oil below 90 dollars pull inflation further down ([TV 2](https://www.tv2.no/nyheter/mener-prisveksten-kan-vaere-gode-nyheter/19412716/), [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/alt-blir-dyrere-kpi-pa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober/s/80-95-235224)).
- E24 sums up that several economists lean towards the peak having been reached, citing the stronger krone and lower rate expectations abroad ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)). Before the print the market priced about a 35 per cent probability of a December hike ([Estate Nyheter](https://www.estatenyheter.no/inflasjon-norges-bank-obligasjonsrenter/norges-bank-forberedt-pa-a-sette-renten-opp/522335), 5 November); no updated market pricing has been published today. No named economist expects a hike in December.

## The krone and rates

EUR/NOK is 10.62 this afternoon, 0.8 per cent higher than on Monday and 2.0 per cent higher over the week; USD/NOK is 9.49. The krone weakened after the print and further through the day as Brent fell to 89 dollars, 3.4 per cent below Monday. The I-44 index stood at 108.81 at Monday's fix, 1.5 per cent weaker over the week but 9.2 per cent stronger this year. The 3-year government yield was 4.59 per cent and the 10-year 4.28 per cent on Monday, little changed; the differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate and about 1.0 point at ten years against the AAA curve. A core print on the path gives the market no reason to price a firmer path, and today's krone weakness looks like an oil story rather than a rates story.
