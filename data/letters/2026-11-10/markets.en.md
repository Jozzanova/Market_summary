# Markets, Tuesday 10 November 2026

**Summary.** Equities rose broadly on Monday. Semiconductor shares recovered part of last week's fall, and a marked drop in the oil price eased concern about inflation: the S&P 500 rose 2.35 per cent to 7,550, the Nasdaq Composite 2.74 per cent and the semiconductor index SOX 6.9 per cent, while Nvidia fell about 4 per cent after its board opened an inquiry concerning the chief executive. The rise continued in Asia on Tuesday, with the KOSPI up 4.4 per cent and the Nikkei 225 up 2.9 per cent. Government yields were little changed, which suggests the move was driven by lower oil and positioning rather than by a shift in rate expectations. Brent has fallen further to 91.4 dollars on the prospect of de-escalation with Iran, although Tehran denies that talks are under way. Consumer prices in Norway (08:00) and the United States (14:30 CET) are the day's most important data.

## Equities

- **United States.** All eleven S&P 500 sectors rose, led by industrials (+3.8 per cent), technology (+3.7 per cent) and consumer discretionary (+2.9 per cent); energy (+0.5 per cent) and staples (+0.9 per cent) lagged. The breadth points to a general rise in risk appetite rather than a rotation between sectors. AMD rose about 13 per cent and Broadcom nearly 9 per cent, while Nvidia was the only decliner in the semiconductor index after its board opened an inquiry into whether a foreign intelligence operation targeted Jensen Huang ([Reuters via Kitco](http://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-11-09/wall-st-jumps-back-oil-slide-eases-inflation-worries-nvidia-slumps), [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-9-2026)). President Trump said the FBI is "looking into" the matter ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/cbabf2da-2079-4b69-88e3-0ba84cf8d6fd)). The AI proxies rose more than the market but remain clearly below their October levels: the SOX is 20.2 per cent lower than a month ago, NYSE FANG+ rose 3.1 per cent, the Magnificent Seven ETF 2.3 per cent and the AI ETFs between 2.7 and 4.0 per cent. Morgan Stanley and UBS judge last week's fall to have been overdone. The VIX fell 22 per cent to 19.9, back to levels consistent with normal risk-taking.
- **Europe.** The STOXX Europe 600 rose 1.3 per cent, the Euro Stoxx 50 1.9 per cent and the DAX 1.7 per cent.
- **Norway.** The benchmark index rose 1.27 per cent to 2,079.5, led by Kongsberg Gruppen (+4.2 per cent). Autostore rose about 11 per cent after an upgrade from DNB Carnegie, while Equinor fell 2.6 per cent and Aker BP 1.2 per cent with the oil price ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/ez1gpO/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)). That the index rose despite the fall in energy shares shows that the gain was broadly based. The index is 23.2 per cent higher this year.
- **Asia.** Chipmakers led again on Tuesday: Samsung gained 4.7 per cent and SK Hynix 6.7 per cent ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/ai-softbank-sk-hynix-samsung-wall-street-tech-stocks.html)), taking the KOSPI up 4.4 per cent to 5,239, still about a fifth below its level a month ago. The Hang Seng rose 0.6 per cent and Shanghai 0.2 per cent ([AP via Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/11/09/stock-markets-ai-earnings-oil/8cb975d8-dcb9-11f1-912c-4fbb80d73f7f_story.html)).

## Fixed income and credit

- Treasuries were little changed despite the rise in equities: the 10-year yield is 4.86 per cent and the 5-year 4.66 per cent on live prints, as the market awaits the inflation data ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/inflation-data-to-test-limits-of-treasury-rally-after-haven-bid)). At the latest official fixes the 2s10s curve stood at 35 basis points, the euro-area AAA 10-year yield at 3.29 per cent and the Norwegian 10-year at 4.29 per cent. That yields did not rise as risk appetite returned suggests the market reads lower oil as disinflationary, and that expectations for the Fed in December are unchanged pending today's CPI.
- US high-yield spreads narrowed 6 basis points to 342 basis points, in line with equities. Japan's 30-year auction drew weaker demand than average ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/japan-30-year-bond-sale-sees-weaker-demand-than-12-month-average-mwa53xcl)), a sign that investors require a higher term premium as the Bank of Japan approaches another hike.

## Commodities and currencies

- **Oil.** Brent fell about 6 dollars during Monday after Trump said new strikes on Iran are "very unlikely" ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/0B5rAG/braatt-fall-i-oljeprisen)), and is at 91.4 dollars on Tuesday morning, 12.7 per cent lower over the week. Iran's Mohsen Rezaei said no negotiations are taking place and that the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until the US meets Iran's seven conditions ([Reuters](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/irans-top-security-official-says-165926144.html)); Rystad's Claudio Galimberti notes that Gulf exports remain 20 per cent below pre-war levels. The fall in price thus reflects a lower risk premium, not better physical supply, and the scope for a lasting decline is limited while the strait is closed. European gas (TTF) fell 3.6 per cent to 82.5 euros.
- **Metals.** Gold is at 4,124 dollars, little changed; copper rose 0.4 per cent, tracking technology shares ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/copper-extends-rebound-as-global-technology-stocks-resume-rally)).
- **FX.** The dollar index is 101.7 and EUR/USD 1.123; USD/JPY is about 158.
- **The krone.** EUR/NOK is at 10.55, unchanged from Monday evening, and USD/NOK at 9.39, 0.7 per cent lower after the dollar softened. The krone has not strengthened with equities. The most plausible explanation is the oil price: when oil falls, the terms of trade weigh more heavily on the krone than higher risk appetite. No source has attributed the move. EUR/NOK is 1.3 per cent higher over the week, and the import-weighted index I-44 stood at 108.81 on Monday, 1.5 per cent weaker over the week but 9.2 per cent stronger this year. The week's weakening therefore changes little in the picture of a krone that is still damping inflation.
