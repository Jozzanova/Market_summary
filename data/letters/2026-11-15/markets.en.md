# Markets, Sunday 15 November 2026

**There has been no trading in equities, bonds or most currencies since Friday's close, so this report rests on Friday's session and the news of the weekend. Friday was marked by a renewed fall in semiconductor shares and a further rise in Treasury yields; over the weekend, Boston Fed president Susan Collins argued for steady, gradual hikes rather than faster ones, while Bank of England chief economist Huw Pill said "we have further to go". The one market that traded through the weekend, bitcoin, fell markedly.**

## Equities

**United States.** On Friday the S&P 500 fell 0.41 per cent and the Nasdaq Composite 0.91 per cent, while the Dow rose 0.10 per cent; all three gained 2.1–2.5 per cent over the week ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The AI and semiconductor proxies fell markedly more than the market: SOX fell 3.43 per cent, NYSE FANG+ 1.00 per cent and the AI ETF AIQ 1.29 per cent, while the Magnificent Seven ETF was little changed (−0.29 per cent). AMD fell 8.75 per cent and Broadcom 4.76 per cent, and Nvidia lost 10.3 per cent over the week ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/9Bvmmr/wall-street-aapner-opp)). Consumer staples (+1.4 per cent) and communication services (+1.1 per cent) rose, consistent with a rotation away from rate- and AI-sensitive sectors. VIX rose 0.6 points to 19.3. Nvidia's results on Tuesday are the next test for the sector.

**Europe and Norway.** STOXX Europe 600 was unchanged on Friday (+0.02 per cent) and rose 2.4 per cent over the week; the DAX gained 3.6 per cent over the week. Oslo Børs rose 0.25 per cent on Friday, led by Equinor (+1.87 per cent), and 2.85 per cent over the week ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/Qroo2P/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)).

**Asia.** On Friday the Nikkei rose 0.53 per cent and the Hang Seng fell 1.54 per cent. There is no Asian session today; Australian housing data released on Sunday showed auction clearance rates at the lowest in more than six years as higher policy rates damp demand ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/australia-auction-clearances-slump-to-six-year-low-as-rates-rise)).

## Fixed income and credit

On Friday the 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.15 per cent and the 5-year 4 basis points to 4.98 per cent; over the week the 5-year rose 33 basis points, the worst week for Treasuries since April 2025 according to the FT ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/7bebace2-dc87-410a-ae29-ffd9f3176def)). Fed funds futures price a Fed hike of about 27 basis points on 9 December and a further 20 basis points by the 27 January meeting. Collins's remarks on Saturday, that a "steady, gradual path seems appropriate" and that the rise in longer-term rates since the start of the year may weigh on demand more than she assumes ([Reuters](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/feds-collins-says-gradual-rate-154619584.html)), argue against the 50 basis point move that some had begun to price, but she also said the path of rates over the next year might be "a bit higher" than she thought a couple of months ago. The German 10-year yield rose 7 basis points on Friday to 3.43 per cent. US corporate bonds have drawn inflows as investors seek shelter from equity volatility, Bloomberg reports, with high-grade yields near 6 per cent ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-14/juicy-6-yields-gain-appeal-after-nvidia-drama-credit-weekly)).

## Commodities and currencies

**Energy.** Brent was unchanged on Friday at 95.84 dollars and fell 2.5 per cent over the week, as the IEA found the oil market in surplus ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/20b68fe2-9d60-4c0c-b714-1f3e03015823)). On Sunday morning Bahrain warned the public to take cover during a new Iranian attack ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-attacks-bahrain-as-conflict-escalates-62ffd1b3)); the effect on oil will first be visible when futures open tonight. TTF gas fell 1.4 per cent on Friday to 90.95 euros.

**Metals.** Gold was unchanged at 3,886 dollars and lost 4.6 per cent over a week of rising real yields; silver fell 1.7 per cent and copper 1.5 per cent on Friday.

**Currencies.** The dollar index rose 0.23 per cent on Friday to 103.41, EUR/USD fell 0.41 per cent to 1.105 and USD/JPY is 159.9. EUR/NOK fell 0.30 per cent to 10.568 and USD/NOK is 9.57, 0.12 per cent higher in the latest quote; I-44 (a rise is a weaker krone) was 109.34 at Friday's fix, 0.5 per cent weaker on the week. The krone's firmness against the euro is likely supported by the oil price and by the 12-month differential in government rates of 1.82 percentage points, while the narrow differential against the United States (0.13 percentage points) leaves the krone exposed to further dollar strength. Bitcoin fell 5.0 per cent over the past day to about 64,800 dollars and is 13 per cent lower over the week; no source attributes the weekend move, but it follows outflows from bitcoin funds reported on Saturday ([E24](https://e24.no/internasjonal-oekonomi/i/oMRXJj/milliarder-renner-ut-av-bitcoinfond)).
