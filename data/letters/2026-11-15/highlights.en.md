# Key points, Sunday 15 November 2026

**Equities.** There has been no trading since Friday, when the S&P 500 fell 0.41 per cent and the Nasdaq 0.91 per cent while the Dow rose 0.10 per cent; all three rose 2.1–2.5 per cent over the week ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). SOX fell 3.43 per cent as AMD and Broadcom reversed earlier gains, and Nvidia lost 10.3 per cent over the week ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/9Bvmmr/wall-street-aapner-opp)). STOXX Europe 600 was unchanged on Friday and Oslo Børs rose 0.25 per cent, 2.85 per cent over the week ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/Qroo2P/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)). There is no Asian session today.

**Government bonds.** On Friday the US 10-year yield rose 5 basis points to 5.15 per cent and the 5-year 4 basis points to 4.98 per cent, 33 basis points higher over the week ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/7bebace2-dc87-410a-ae29-ffd9f3176def)); the German 10-year rose 7 basis points to 3.43 per cent. Fed funds futures price a Fed hike of about 27 basis points on 9 December and a further 20 basis points in January.

**Currencies.** The dollar index ended the week at 103.41 after a rise of 1.1 per cent, EUR/USD at 1.105 and USD/JPY at 159.9. EUR/NOK closed Friday at 10.568, 1.2 per cent higher on the week, and USD/NOK is 9.57; I-44 was 109.34, 0.5 per cent weaker on the week, as the dollar's rate advantage outweighed the support from oil. Bitcoin fell 5.0 per cent over the weekend to about 64,800 dollars.

**Energy.** Brent ended Friday at 95.84 dollars, 2.5 per cent lower on the week as the IEA found a surplus ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/20b68fe2-9d60-4c0c-b714-1f3e03015823)); a new Iranian attack on Bahrain on Sunday morning ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-attacks-bahrain-as-conflict-escalates-62ffd1b3)) is a risk to the open tonight. TTF gas fell 1.4 per cent on Friday to 90.95 euros.

**Drivers.** Boston Fed president Susan Collins argued on Saturday for a "steady, gradual path" of hikes, though she now sees the rate path a bit higher than two months ago ([Reuters](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/feds-collins-says-gradual-rate-154619584.html)); Bank of England chief economist Huw Pill said "we have further to go" ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/14/interest-rate-rises-inflation-bank-of-england-chief-economist)); US officials estimate that Iran has rebuilt its missile stock to up to 2,500 ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/u-s-figures-iran-still-has-up-to-2-500-missiles-countering-trump-assertions-4c6f4d67)); the APEC summit and the Trump–Xi–Putin meeting this week follow the ASEAN meeting from Sunday ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/15/asia-diplomatic-summits-trump-xi-putin-apec-asean)).

**Norway.** The differential against the euro area is 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, 1.82 percentage points at twelve months in government rates and 0.94 percentage points at ten years; against the United States the 12-month differential is only 0.13 percentage points. No economist published a new view on Norges Bank over the weekend; the economists quoted expect a hold on 17 December, and the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike. A Wood Mackenzie analysis for the energy ministry finds that a high CO₂ tax could cut shelf output by 11–13 per cent in 2040 ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/kMJR06/analyse-avgift-kan-stenge-oljefelt-tidlig)).
