# Norges Bank, Sunday 15 November 2026

**No new Norwegian key figures were published over the weekend, and no bank economist or commentator published a new view on Norges Bank that could be found. The standing expectations are unchanged: the economists quoted after the October CPI expect the policy rate to be held at 4.50 per cent on 17 December, while the latest reported market pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike. The new elements abroad point the same way as last week: Fed and Bank of England officials continue to argue for further tightening.**

## New data

None since Friday. The next Norwegian figures are external trade in goods for October on Monday and the labour cost index for the third quarter on Wednesday. Outside the official statistics, Wood Mackenzie's analysis for the energy ministry finds that a high CO₂ tax could reduce shelf production by 11–13 per cent in 2040 compared with a low tax ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/kMJR06/analyse-avgift-kan-stenge-oljefelt-tidlig)); this bears on the long-term outlook for petroleum investment rather than on the rate path in the near term.

## What others say

- **The bank economists' views are unchanged.** Marius Gonsholt Hov of Handelsbanken expects a hold in December and considers the peak reached, and Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects a hold and a signal that the peak has been reached ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)). Kjersti Haugland of DNB Carnegie expects the policy rate to stay at 4.50 per cent in December and through the first half of 2027, as she said ahead of the November meeting ([Bygg.no](https://www.bygg.no/okonomi/sjefokonom-om-renta-kan-se-ut-som-rentetoppen-er-nadd/2969973)).
- **Market pricing:** the latest reported figure remains Handelsbanken's reading of about 35 per cent for a December hike and about 50 per cent by March (5 November); no newer figure was found.

None of the bank economists quoted expects a hike in December. The case for tightening rests on high household inflation expectations and on tightening abroad: Boston Fed president Susan Collins expects the US rate path to be "a bit higher" than she thought a couple of months ago ([Reuters](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/feds-collins-says-gradual-rate-154619584.html)), and Bank of England chief economist Huw Pill said "we have further to go" ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/14/interest-rate-rises-inflation-bank-of-england-chief-economist)). Higher rates abroad narrow the differential and, all else equal, weaken the krone, which would add to imported inflation. The Expectations Survey on Thursday is the next test of whether expectations have moved further from the target.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK closed Friday at 10.568, 0.30 per cent lower on the day and 1.2 per cent higher on the week; USD/NOK is 9.57, 3.0 per cent higher on the week. I-44 was 109.34 at Friday's fix, 0.5 per cent weaker on the week and 1.0 per cent stronger than at the September Monetary Policy Report.

**Why.** The krone weakened over the week mainly against the dollar, which rose as US short rates moved up; oil prices, 2.5 per cent lower on the week, gave less support than in October. The 12-month differential against the United States has narrowed to 0.13 percentage points, which leaves little rate cushion against further dollar strength.

**Differentials.**
- Against the euro area: 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate; 1.82 percentage points at twelve months in government rates (the Norwegian 12-month bill against the euro AAA 1-year rate, a stand-in for the swap differential), little changed on the week; 0.94 percentage points at ten years.
- Against the United States: 0.13 percentage points at twelve months in government rates.
