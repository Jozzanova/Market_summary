# Markets, weekly report week 46 (6–13 November 2026)

**Equities rose broadly over the week even as government yields rose markedly. Lower oil and a rebound in AI-related shares carried equities early in the week, while firmer US inflation and a run of Fed officials arguing for more tightening drove the largest weekly rise in US short yields in two years. At the end of the week the leadership change at Nvidia and higher yields turned semiconductor shares lower, and investors moved towards defensive sectors.**

## Equities

**United States.** The S&P 500 rose 2.50 per cent, the Nasdaq Composite 2.27 per cent, the Dow 2.07 per cent and the Russell 2000 1.32 per cent. Energy led the sectors (+5.4 per cent), followed by industrials and communication services; materials and real estate lagged. The VIX fell 6.3 points to 19.3.

**AI and semiconductors.** SOX rose 8.05 per cent, against 3.34 per cent for NYSE FANG+ and 1.67 per cent for the Magnificent Seven ETF. The gain came mid-week: CoreWeave's results on Wednesday eased concern that AI earnings would come too late to service data-centre debt ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/stocks/investors-run-back-to-ai-stocks-less-than-a-week-after-a-historic-selloff-854483e8)). Nvidia moved the other way: its board opened an inquiry concerning Jensen Huang on Monday, Huang resigned on Thursday with Colette Kress as interim chief executive ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)), and the shares lost 10.3 per cent on the week ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/9Bvmmr/wall-street-aapner-opp)). On Friday SOX fell 3.43 per cent as AMD and Broadcom, which had risen on expectations of winning Nvidia's business, reversed.

**Europe and Norway.** STOXX Europe 600 rose 2.44 per cent and the DAX 3.60 per cent; euro-area banks rose 4.2 per cent, consistent with higher yields supporting margins. Oslo Børs rose 2.85 per cent ([DN](https://www.dn.no/bors/oslo-bors/hydrogenpro/bitcoin/oslo-bors-endte-opp-nar-tre-prosent-for-uken/2-1-2073730)); from Wednesday Equinor, Aker BP and the tanker companies led as oil recovered ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/AR2eBx/oppgang-paa-oslo-boers)).

**Asia.** The Nikkei rose 6.46 per cent and the KOSPI 4.56 per cent, led by chipmakers, while the Hang Seng fell 3.45 per cent; Chinese AI shares fell on Friday on concern about tighter US chip restrictions ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/0d4e93e4-895e-4ae0-a01b-833a3ed440a8)).

## Fixed income and credit

The US 5-year yield rose 33 basis points to 4.98 per cent, the 10-year 30 basis points to 5.15 per cent and the official 2-year 37 basis points to 4.88 per cent, so the curve flattened. Reuters described it as the largest weekly rise in 2-year yields in two years, with the CME probability of 50 basis points of Fed tightening by January up to 67 per cent from 16 per cent ([Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449075/us-treasuries-two-year-yields-headed-for-biggest-weekly-jump-in-two-years)). The 10-year real yield rose 28 basis points while the breakeven was unchanged at 2.24 per cent: the market priced tighter policy, not higher inflation. The German 2-year rose 18 basis points and the 10-year 14 basis points to 3.43 per cent; the Norwegian 10-year rose 6 basis points to 4.36 per cent, less than abroad. US high-yield spreads narrowed 19 basis points, in line with equities.

## Commodities and currencies

**Oil and gas.** Brent fell 2.52 per cent to 95.84 dollars. It touched 91.49 dollars on Tuesday, the lowest since 31 August, on US signals that the Iran war was winding down ([Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/2026/11/11/oil-hits-two-month-lows-as-us-eases-iran-fears)), rose 3.3 per cent on Thursday after President Trump wrote that the US does not need a deal with Iran, and was unchanged on Friday as the IEA described a market in surplus ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/20b68fe2-9d60-4c0c-b714-1f3e03015823)). TTF gas rose 6.27 per cent to 90.95 euros. Gold fell 4.63 per cent as real yields rose.

**Currencies.** The dollar index rose 1.08 per cent to 103.41, the highest since April 2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)); EUR/USD fell 1.58 per cent to 1.105 and USD/JPY rose to 159.9, prompting warnings from Japan's finance minister ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/japan-s-katayama-warns-of-fx-steps-after-one-sided-rapid-moves)). EUR/NOK rose 1.21 per cent to 10.568, USD/NOK 2.84 per cent to 9.564 and I-44 0.51 per cent to 109.34 (a rise is a weaker krone). The krone weakened through Tuesday as oil fell and the dollar rose, and recovered part of the move against the euro from Wednesday as oil rose.

## Turning points of the week

- **Monday 9 November:** equities rose as oil fell about 6 dollars on Trump's Iran comments.
- **Tuesday 10 November:** US CPI above expectations; the 2-year rose 18 basis points.
- **Wednesday 11 November:** CoreWeave's results lifted AI shares.
- **Thursday 12 November:** Huang resigned; Fed officials argued for more tightening; Brent rose 3.3 per cent.
- **Friday 13 November:** firm producer prices; SOX fell 3.4 per cent.
