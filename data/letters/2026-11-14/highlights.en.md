# Key points, weekly report week 46 (6–13 November 2026)

**The week was shaped by a renewed repricing of the Federal Reserve. October consumer prices rose more than expected, a broad group of Fed officials argued for further tightening, and a hike on 9 December went from probable to fully priced; US short yields had their largest weekly rise since 2024. Equities nonetheless rose, as lower oil early in the week and a recovery in AI-related shares outweighed higher yields, while the leadership change at Nvidia made the semiconductor sector volatile towards the end of the week. The krone weakened, more against the dollar than against the euro.**

**Equities.** The S&P 500 rose 2.50 per cent over the week, the Nasdaq Composite 2.27 per cent and the Dow 2.07 per cent; STOXX Europe 600 rose 2.44 per cent, the DAX 3.60 per cent and Oslo Børs 2.85 per cent ([DN](https://www.dn.no/bors/oslo-bors/hydrogenpro/bitcoin/oslo-bors-endte-opp-nar-tre-prosent-for-uken/2-1-2073730)). The semiconductor index SOX rose 8.05 per cent despite a fall of 3.43 per cent on Friday, while Nvidia lost 10.3 per cent after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive ([E24](https://e24.no/boers-og-finans/i/9Bvmmr/wall-street-aapner-opp)). Energy (+5.4 per cent) was the strongest US sector. In Asia the Nikkei rose 6.46 per cent and the KOSPI 4.56 per cent, while the Hang Seng fell 3.45 per cent.

**Government bonds.** The US 5-year yield rose 33 basis points to 4.98 per cent and the 10-year 30 basis points to 5.15 per cent, the worst week for Treasuries since April 2025 ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/7bebace2-dc87-410a-ae29-ffd9f3176def)). Real yields led, while the breakeven was little changed: the market priced tighter policy, not higher inflation. The euro-area AAA 10-year rose 13 basis points to 3.41 per cent and the German 2-year 18 basis points; the Norwegian 10-year rose 6 basis points to 4.36 per cent.

**Currencies.** The dollar index rose 1.08 per cent to 103.41, the highest since April 2025, EUR/USD fell 1.58 per cent to 1.105 and USD/JPY rose to 159.9 after crossing 160 on Thursday. EUR/NOK rose 1.21 per cent to 10.568, USD/NOK 2.84 per cent to 9.564 and I-44 0.51 per cent to 109.34 (a rise is a weaker krone). Lower oil weakened the krone early in the week; from Wednesday higher oil supported it against the euro, while the wider US rate advantage kept it weak against the dollar.

**Commodities.** Brent fell 2.52 per cent to 95.84 dollars. It touched a two-month low on Tuesday after President Trump said new strikes on Iran were unlikely, and recovered on Thursday after he wrote that the US does not need a deal with Iran; on Friday the IEA found the market in surplus ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/20b68fe2-9d60-4c0c-b714-1f3e03015823)). TTF gas rose 6.27 per cent to 90.95 euros. Gold fell 4.63 per cent as real yields rose.

**Data and central banks.** US consumer prices rose 0.4 per cent in October and core prices 0.3 per cent, against 0.3 and 0.2 expected ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)); producer prices rose 6.5 per cent on the year ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.nr0.htm)). Fed presidents Williams, Daly, Hammack and Goolsbee and Governor Waller argued for more tightening ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/fed-s-williams-says-inflation-far-too-high-still-work-to-do)), and Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel called a further ECB hike "highly probable" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/nagel-says-highly-probable-that-ecb-will-have-to-act-again)). No G10 central bank changed rates.

**Norway.** Inflation was 3.6 per cent in October and CPI-ATE 2.7 per cent, against a consensus of 3.7 and 2.7 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/priser-og-prisindekser/konsumpriser/statistikk/konsumprisindeksen)); the economists quoted afterwards expect Norges Bank to hold in December ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)), and the last reported pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike. The 12-month differential in government rates against the euro area narrowed about 12 basis points to 1.82 percentage points, and against the United States about 32 basis points to 0.13 points. Statistics Norway lowered its 2026 petroleum investment estimate to 266 billion kroner ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/Qrlp88/jekker-ned-aarets-oljeinvesteringer)), and the Supreme Court ruled that the approvals of three oil fields must be reassessed ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/M5gz2r/klimasoeksmaalet-tillatelser-maa-behandles-paa-nytt)).

**The week ahead.** Nvidia reports on Tuesday, its first results since the change of chief executive, alongside UK labour market data. On Wednesday come UK consumer prices, the final euro-area HICP (flash 4.0 per cent), the minutes of the Fed's October meeting and the pricing of Anthropic's listing. APEC meets in Shenzhen on 18–19 November, with a Trump–Xi–Putin meeting on Thursday ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/trump-to-meet-with-xi-putin-nov-19-in-china-white-house-says)), the same day as Norges Bank's Expectations Survey; Labour's deadline for a 2027 budget agreement is Friday. Markets enter the week with a Fed hike on 9 December fully priced and about 13 per cent for 50 basis points ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).
