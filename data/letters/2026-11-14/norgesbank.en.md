# Norges Bank, weekly report week 46 (6–13 November 2026)

**The Norwegian week was dominated by the October CPI, which came in a notch below consensus on the headline and in line with Norges Bank's projection on the core. The economists quoted afterwards expect the policy rate to be held at 4.50 per cent in December, and several judge that the peak has been reached. Abroad, the case for tightening strengthened, which narrowed the krone's rate advantage: the krone weakened 1.2 per cent against the euro and 2.8 per cent against the dollar over the week.**

## New data

**CPI, October.** Inflation was 3.6 per cent on twelve months (3.4 in September) and CPI-ATE 2.7 per cent (3.0), against a consensus of 3.7 and 2.7 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/priser-og-prisindekser/konsumpriser/statistikk/konsumprisindeksen)). The rise in the headline rate is a base effect from electricity, where Norgespris and a lower electricity tax lowered prices in October last year; food prices fell for the third month in a row. The fall in core inflation is partly a base effect as well ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).

**Investment survey.** Statistics Norway lowered its estimate for petroleum investment in 2026 by 9 billion kroner to 266 billion and gave a first estimate of 237 billion for 2027 ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/Qrlp88/jekker-ned-aarets-oljeinvesteringer)). First estimates are routinely revised up, so the figures point to a gradual decline in petroleum investment and a somewhat weaker impulse to the mainland economy in 2027.

**Not verified.** Producer prices for October (Monday) and the balance of payments for the third quarter (Friday) were due, but no report of the figures could be verified.

**Norges Bank.** The Financial Stability Report on Wednesday kept the countercyclical buffer at 2.5 per cent ([Norges Bank](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/news/Press-releases/2026/2026-11-11-fsr2/)).

## What others say

- **Marius Gonsholt Hov, Handelsbanken:** the print "does not change rate expectations now"; a hike before Christmas would have required "a fairly strong upside" in this print and the November one ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).
- **Harald Magnus Andreassen, SpareBank 1 Markets:** inflation is on the path the bank assumed; he sees no hike in December or next year ([NTB](https://www.frifagbevegelse.no/nyheter/okonomer-avlyser-renteokning-i-ar/2732723)).
- **Kyrre M. Knudsen, SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge:** "good news"; he expects a hold and a signal that the peak has been reached ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/alt-blir-dyrere-kpi-pa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober/s/80-95-235224)).
- **Kjetil Storesletten, University of Minnesota,** argued that the policy rate should be 5–6 per cent because household inflation expectations are high, and Torfinn Harding of Kristiania University College said people no longer trust the inflation target ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/rente-og-inflasjon-eksperter-frykter-tapt-tillit-til-norges-bank/s/80-95-240062)).

None of the bank economists quoted expects a hike in December. The latest reported market pricing is Handelsbanken's reading of about 35 per cent for a December hike (5 November); no newer figure was reported during the week. The argument for tightening rests on household inflation expectations and on tightening abroad, while core inflation on the bank's projection pulls against it.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK rose 1.21 per cent to 10.568, USD/NOK 2.84 per cent to 9.564 and I-44 0.51 per cent to 109.34 (a rise is a weaker krone). The krone weakened through Tuesday, when Brent touched a two-month low and the dollar strengthened after the US CPI; from Wednesday higher oil supported it against the euro, while the dollar held its gains.

**Differentials.**
- Against the euro area: 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate, unchanged; 1.82 points at twelve months in government rates (the Norwegian 12-month bill against the euro AAA 1-year, a stand-in for the swap differential), about 12 basis points narrower as the euro rate rose; 0.95 points at ten years, about 6 basis points narrower.
- Against the United States: 0.13 points at twelve months in government rates, about 32 basis points narrower as US short rates rose.

The Norwegian 10-year rose 6 basis points to 4.36 per cent, less than the euro-area AAA 10-year (13 basis points) and the US 10-year (30 basis points). A narrower rate advantage works against the krone; I-44 is nonetheless about 8.8 per cent stronger than at the start of the year, so the krone's contribution to imported inflation remains dampening.
