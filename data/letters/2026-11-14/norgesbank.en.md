# Norges Bank, Saturday 14 November 2026

**Summary.** No bank economist published a new view on Norges Bank on Friday, and no new Norwegian key figure that could be verified moved expectations. The standing view is unchanged: the economists quoted after the October CPI expect the policy rate to be held at 4.50 per cent in December, while the latest reported market pricing is about 35 per cent for a hike. The Supreme Court's ruling on three oil fields and further tightening signals abroad are the new elements for the outlook.

## New data

No new Norwegian key figure that moves the rate outlook was published on Friday. Statistics Norway's balance of payments for the third quarter was due at 08:00, but no report of the figures could be verified. The Supreme Court's ruling that the approvals of Tyrving, Breidablikk and Yggdrasil must be reassessed does not stop production ([E24](https://e24.no/energi-og-klima/i/M5gz2r/klimasoeksmaalet-tillatelser-maa-behandles-paa-nytt)); together with Thursday's investment survey, which lowered the 2026 estimate for petroleum investment, it points to somewhat weaker impulses from the petroleum sector over time, although Yggdrasil, at 195 billion kroner the largest project on the shelf, continues.

## What others say

- **The standing bank views** are unchanged: Marius Gonsholt Hov of Handelsbanken expects a hold in December and considers the peak reached, and Kyrre M. Knudsen of SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge expects a hold and a signal that the peak has been reached ([E24](https://e24.no/norsk-oekonomi/i/M5gG7R/prisveksten-laa-paa-3-6-prosent-i-oktober)).
- **The debate on credibility** from Thursday continues: professor Kjetil Storesletten has argued for a policy rate of 5–6 per cent because household inflation expectations are high ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/rente-og-inflasjon-eksperter-frykter-tapt-tillit-til-norges-bank/s/80-95-240062)).
- **Market pricing:** the latest reported figure is Handelsbanken's reading of about 35 per cent for a December hike and about 50 per cent by March (5 November); no newer figure was found.

None of the bank economists quoted expects a hike in December. The argument for tightening rests on the gap in household inflation expectations and on tightening abroad: the Fed is fully priced to hike on 9 December ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)) and Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel considers a further ECB hike highly probable ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/nagel-says-highly-probable-that-ecb-will-have-to-act-again)). The Expectations Survey on 19 November is the next test.

## The krone and rates

**Exchange rates.** EUR/NOK fell 0.30 per cent on Friday to 10.568 and USD/NOK rose 0.11 per cent to 9.564. I-44 was 109.34 at Friday's fix, 0.06 per cent higher on the day; it is 0.5 per cent higher (a weaker krone) on the week and 1.0 per cent lower than at the September Monetary Policy Report.

**Why.** Firm oil prices supported the krone against the euro, while the dollar was lifted by expectations of further Fed tightening; the 12-month differential against the United States has narrowed to 0.13 percentage points.

**Differentials.**
- Against the euro area: 2.00 percentage points on the policy rate; 1.82 points at twelve months in government rates (the Norwegian 12-month bill against the euro AAA 1-year rate, a stand-in for the swap differential), 2 basis points narrower; 0.94 points at ten years (Thursday's fixes).
- Against the United States: 0.13 points at twelve months in government rates, 8 basis points narrower as US short rates rose.
